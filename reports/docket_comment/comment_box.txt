Comment on Docket No. FDA-2025-N-6075 (RIN 0910-AI94). Submitted by Rikhin Kavuru, individual. A three-page version with fuller reasoning is attached.

DISCLOSURE OF INTEREST. I work on early-stage software for sterile drug manufacturing, a release-decision support layer,, and I have sought pre-seed funding for it. It has no product on the market, no customer, no revenue, and no application pending before the Agency. A rule making distributed manufacturing registration more workable could enlarge the market that work is directed at, so my interest is indirect but real. I submit as an individual, and state the interest rather than leave the Agency to discover it.

WHAT THIS IS. I am a high school student. Over the past year I built an open, pre-registered engineering and operations model of sterile-injectable supply, testing whether regionally distributed manufacturing reduces shortage risk better than safety stock, dual sourcing, reserved capacity, or more centralized capacity. Its inputs are illustrative placeholders I chose myself: not measurements, not validated, and not yet independently reviewed. I assert nothing about whether any system is compliant with FDA requirements or ready to manufacture drugs. What the model can show is which constraints determine the outcome.

1. PLEASE DEFINE "UNIFIED PHARMACEUTICAL QUALITY SYSTEM." The preamble notes this term and "equivalent" are undefined and seeks comment on whether they should be defined. They should be, and the ambiguity worth resolving is which quality-unit functions may be exercised centrally across distributed manufacturing units and which must stay resident at each unit.

This is not a drafting preference. Every multi-site architecture in my model rests on an assumption that one quality unit can perform batch disposition for several registered locations, with only some functions resident locally. I recorded it as unsourced: my only basis is my own reading of 21 CFR 211.22(a), which assigns the quality control unit responsibility for approving or rejecting each batch without addressing the distributed case. If it is wrong, each unit carries a full resident quality function and the configuration stops being distributed in any economically meaningful sense. A pathway letting an establishment register as one establishment, while leaving unresolved whether its quality system may dispose of batches as one quality unit, grants the administrative benefit without the operational one.

2. THE REGISTRATION TIMELINES ARE NOT THE INTERVAL GOVERNING SUPPLY. The Agency specifically requests comment on the 30-day and 120-day mobile-unit relocation timelines. I have no operational basis to propose different numbers and do not.

I offer a different observation. The interval deciding whether added capacity relieves a shortage is not the notification interval. It is elapsed time from the decision to add capacity to the first batch that can be released, covering qualification, validation, and the release hold. Under my illustrative inputs, sterility testing sits on that hold's critical path and dominates it: chemical and analytical testing runs concurrently, so removing it would shorten release only slightly, while incubation does not compress. A distributed unit contributes nothing before it exists as a releasing site, and that dead interval, not steady-state efficiency, most often decides whether the configuration meets a service requirement. I suggest the final rule distinguish the administrative interval from time to first released batch, rather than treating flexibility in the former as a proxy for responsiveness.

3. THE PRELIMINARY ECONOMIC ANALYSIS MAY OVERSTATE UPTAKE. My most durable finding is that a network whose saleable capacity is below demand cannot be stocked or scheduled out of the deficit. In my runs, the configurations meeting a service requirement where the incumbent plant is capacity-short are not distributed plants a sponsor builds. They buy campaigns on lines already registered and inspected, paired with positioned finished-goods inventory. Distributed capacity performed best on the product that was not capacity-short, which is where a shortage is least likely to arise. If that holds under real inputs, the likeliest users of this pathway are sponsors already operating registered lines, not new entrants building regional capacity to address shortages, and I encourage the Agency to consider whether its uptake estimate rests on the second when the first is the likelier user.

I am not asking FDA to endorse, review, or respond to my model, and nothing here should be read as a claim that it has. I seek no approval, designation, or meeting. Nobody asked me to submit this and nobody is paying me to. Every quantitative statement above describes the behavior of a computational model under illustrative inputs that are not validated or peer reviewed, and none is a measurement, a regulatory position, or a statement about any firm, product, or facility.
