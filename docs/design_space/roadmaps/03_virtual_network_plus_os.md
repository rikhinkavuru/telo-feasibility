Assignment Phase F, deliverable 20, candidate 3 of four. Prepared 2026-09-05.

**PRELIMINARY / ILLUSTRATIVE / NOT FOR EXTERNAL CLAIMS.** Every model number quoted here is behaviour of the engine under tier-5 illustrative inputs. None of it is a statement about sterile-injectable manufacturing in the world, a recommendation, or a commitment. All sixteen gates in `config/regulatory_gates.yaml` (the fifteen frozen at R002 plus G16 added under R007) stay UNCERTAIN; UNCERTAIN is never PASS, and no strategy here receives a favourable decision class. Nothing here is legal or regulatory advice. 503B is a time-varying legal state and never a durable pathway (`architecture_taxonomy.md` incompatibility 4; L503B-S02, L503B-S07, L503B-S03). No interview, partner, customer, purchaser or reviewer exists; none has occurred. Every third party named is a published organisation cited by source id.

**The honest position of this candidate.** The company contracts registered third-party US capacity, owns the application or label and the quality unit, keeps technical-transfer packages current, and runs a data layer across its own partners. The prior-art review classifies every element of that shape `already_implemented`: prequalifying capacity across existing CDMOs (F3-S11, F3-S18, F3-S1, F3-S21, F3-S28), reserved third-party capacity paid a standby fee (F3-S3, F3-S9, F7-S04, F7-S08, F7-S10, F7-S21, F10-S24), a non-manufacturer coordinating activation and allocation (F3-S10, F3-S1, F3-S23), and the asset-light operator holding only label, application or contracts, with a documented reversal, since Civica was virtual from 2018 to 2023 and then built Petersburg with contamination control among the stated reasons (F3-S23, F3-S25, F3-S29, F3-S52, F3-S31). Telo has no evidenced reason it beats Civica at 14 partners and 77 products; `falsification_register.csv` data row 14 carries that claim at status `untested`, with its threshold column recording "falsified today: no such reason is in the evidence", and pruning rule P5 requires such a reason.

**The OS half is weaker, and it is a bridge rather than a destination.** The cross-family review puts the manufacturing operating system on the does-not-survive list in all eight sub-forms (`prior_art_review.md` section 6, closing paragraph), six of them with named incumbents. The engine agrees that its levers do not bind. A perfect-OS ablation that removes every deviation, every rejection and all yield variance moves mean fill by +0.001771 across 40 cells, flips 3 of 40 cells at q = 0.90 with all three inside 1.3 binomial standard errors, and saves at most 40,684 USD/yr against an OS charge of 600,000 USD/yr at the base price (`os_only_and_virtual_network.md` section 3.1; `results/ablation/abl_post_R008/attribution.csv`). On sodium bicarbonate S17 the OS adds 0.9601 USD to a 15.6660 USD/unit break-even against a perfect-OS operational value of 0.0283 USD/unit. So the OS here is an internal instrument that keeps qualification state, activation and allocation legible inside Telo's own network. It is not the product, it is not sold on day one, and it makes no release claim.

**What the bridge would have to lead to.** One OS-shaped position survives prior art: manufacturer-side capacity coordination across independent owners, position 6.6, `uncertain` with documented structural barriers (F2-S63, F2-S64, F3-S19). It is a contracting and antitrust question before it is a product, HDA names the Sherman Act and DOJ/FTC monitoring, and ASPR already part-funds the registry half through the DS-MRN challenge (LGOV-S17, LGOV-S51). Running Telo's own network is the only route in this package by which the qualification data that position needs would ever exist. That is the bridge: contract first, coordinate later, and only if the antitrust reading permits it.

**Which configured strategies stand for this roadmap.** S12 (contracted registered-capacity supply base) and S13 (contracted base plus rotated reserved surge plus reserve) are family 3 and are configured as the family's reference and ablation arms rather than as recommendations (`novel_architectures.md`, S12 and S13 entries). S16 (reserve-triggered campaign on contracted registered lines, family 8) and S17 (rotating campaign network across three registered CMO lines, family 7) carry the same contracted shape with an activation or rotation instrument attached. The engine does not separate S12, S13 and S16 on service at the frozen target, prefers S16 on cost, and puts S16 on both cost-service frontiers while S13 is dominated on both (`feasibility_regions.md` section 4; `inventory_capacity_hybrids.md` section 3.5). This roadmap therefore carries all three and names in section 2 the evidence that would choose among them.

## 1. What the company is on day one, and what it is not

It is an application or label holder with its own quality unit, one or more executed contracts at registered US sterile fill lines, maintained technical-transfer packages, an inventory position, and an internal data layer.

It is not a manufacturer. Under R004 a reserved line Telo does not own carries no capital, fixed operations or validation, only its reservation fee and the batches it runs (MD-14). The ledgers do not show that cleanly: S16 and S17 still carry the incumbent plant's capital of 6,703,202 USD/yr, identical to S0, because ownership is a property of the `reserved` flag rather than of the site (MD-14 residual, `os_only_and_virtual_network.md` section 2.2). No Telo-only companion design has been run, so no cost figure in this file is a Telo-only cost.

It is not asset-light on quality. Disposition is non-delegable: 21 CFR 211.22(a) (F3-S42), 21 CFR 200.10(b) treating extramural facilities as an extension of the manufacturer (F3-S43), and FDA's quality-agreements guidance stating a quality agreement cannot delegate CGMP responsibility (F3-S41). One Telo quality unit disposes every batch from every partner line, which is why S12, S13, S16 and S17 all declare `cc_quality` across every site, and why they read worse on dependence than frozen S2, which declares no quality group. S2 is not independent either: both its sites carry `cc_vial_1`, its central site carries `cc_geo_R1`, and its second source separates only the API (`src/telo_feasibility/strategies.py:294-311`).

It is not eligible for the distributed-manufacturing-establishment route: the proposed rule requires a single legal entity as registrant and excludes contract units (F3-R4, L503B-S06, `architecture_taxonomy.md` incompatibility 7). It is not a 503B. It is not a software vendor: the engine has no software revenue line, no customer entity, no seat or site count outside Telo's own network, no contract value and no churn (`os_only_and_virtual_network.md` section 2.1), and `os_integration_usd_per_site_year` (100k / 300k / 800k, `config/global.yaml:614`, tier 5, source locator "study placeholder") is a cost Telo pays, not a price Telo charges.

It is not a release-automation company. Every design-space strategy runs release scenario R0 and claims no release-assurance component, so gate G15 gains no strategy (revision R006 note in `config/regulatory_gates.yaml`). The claims register records "Telo automates the release decision" and "the operating system for sterile drug manufacturing" as contradicted (C010, C015), and the company's own deck brief says not to use the phrase manufacturing OS (`deck-prompt.md:61`, cited in C015).

## 2. First product or product class, and what would pick one

The evidence supports a class and cannot pick a presentation. `product_architecture_matching.md` states that it does not select a product; selection is reserved to the founder under HA-03 and definition-of-finished test DF03.

The class the features support: non-controlled aqueous small-molecule solution in a standard vial, not cold-chain, not cytotoxic, currently or recurrently on the FDA shortage list, with national demand inside the architecture's own ceiling. Two candidates meet it on frozen snapshot S01 (2026-09-02): sodium bicarbonate 8.4% 50 mL, 15 current of 19 total shortage rows over 9.51 years, and furosemide 10 mg/mL, 30 current of 33 rows over 6.40 years and the only candidate whose reason list carries a GMP-compliance reason (`product_architecture_matching.md` section 3.1).

The evidence does not choose between those two, and the reasons are recorded. Furosemide has no configuration and no fixed fill volume, so PG1 is UNCERTAIN and no batch size, capacity or architecture can be sized for it; its longlist status is HA-03 and its shortage status needs a primary-source check, HA-18. Sodium bicarbonate is configured, but its 1,200,000 units per year is tier 5 and this roadmap's demand ceiling sits 3.9% above it at 1,246,875 units per year for S12, S13 and S16 (`ds_post_R008/thresholds.csv`, `capacity_utilization`, n = 20, binomial standard error 0.067). Norepinephrine is a resolved comparator with 0 shortage rows and its beachhead claim is retracted internally (C008). Acetazolamide fails archetype gate PG2 because it is lyophilized. Sterile water has the most concentrated supplier structure in the set, one application and zero Orange Book applicants, with a volume mismatch the longlist itself admits.

What would pick one: HA-11 for the demand denominator and installed capacity, HA-13 and HA-21 for line capacity, batch size and fill volume, HA-34 (queue rank 13) for the sterilization route, HA-03 for the founder's longlist decision. Route matters less to this family than to family 11, but it decides whether any later release story exists at all (`product_architecture_matching.md` section 3.5).

## 3. First customer and first measurable value

The first customer is a buyer of finished vials, not a payer for readiness. Prior-art open question 17 finds no US hospital system, GPO or state ever paying a standing reservation fee for sterile generic capacity (F3-S28); Civica's 2023 Senate testimony asking Congress for grants to create reserve capacity is evidence none exists; FDA's Drug Shortages Task Force records that most generic manufacturers cannot afford redundant capacity (F7-S16). Family 7's standing instruction is to assume the answer is no until a purchaser says otherwise.

So the first customer is a health system, IDN or GPO signing multi-year committed volume for units. The shapes in the evidence are Civica's minimum viable volume, about 50% of expected volume over three to ten years (F10-S01, F8-S14), and Premier ProvideGx long-term committed buying contracts (LSIM-S19).

The first measurable value is fill against that commitment, with days of supply held and a failure-to-supply remedy that is enforceable. Under illustrative inputs at the frozen target (`results/optimization/opt_20260903T220534Z/summary.json`), on sodium bicarbonate S12 reaches 0.998257 mean fill at p_meet 0.95 and 17.8847 USD/unit, S13 0.996385 at 0.90 and 15.4782, S16 0.996974 at 0.94 and 13.1807. Those are the optimizer's own evaluations; the 40-run dominance re-evaluation of the same designs gives S16 0.9972 at p_meet 0.925 and S12 0.9976 at 0.95 (`ds_post_R008/dominance.csv`), which is the set the synthesis quotes.

The commercial threshold decides this section. At S0's own break-even cost of 14.4320 USD/unit used as a stand-in price, only S16 (0.8021 of saleable capacity committed) and S17 (0.9609) cover fixed and resilience cost on sodium bicarbonate. S17 does not meet the frozen service target on that product: p_meet 0.88 at the optimizer with status `infeasible_at_full_n`, 0.85 over 40 paired runs, feasible False, dominated by S11 and S16 (`opt_20260903T220534Z/summary.json`; `ds_post_R008/dominance.csv`), so its 0.9609 is a commercial figure attached to a design that fails the service condition on this product. On norepinephrine none covers fixed and resilience cost, with S16 at 1.0855, S13 at 1.4015 and S12 at 1.5866, and a value above 1.0 means the design cannot cover fixed cost even if every unit it can make is committed (`os_only_and_virtual_network.md` section 4). No price a purchaser will pay is known for either presentation. That is HA-24 and HA-11, and nobody has been asked.

## 4. Data-access strategy

**What the company needs.** Per-partner qualification and inspection state, per-line capacity, batch rate, changeover and minimum campaign size, reservation fee and tenancy price, deviation rate and investigation duration, presentation-level demand and its regional split, and a price.

**Who holds it.** Line owners and process engineers (HA-13, HA-21); quality, CMC and microbiology staff (HA-22); hospital pharmacy and supply chain (HA-20); GPO, wholesaler and distributor (HA-24); FDA public records for registration, listing and inspection status (frozen snapshots S16 Drugs@FDA, S17 Orange Book, the 503B facility table); ASHP for presentation-level shortage duration, which is copyrighted and needs a person (queue section B, HA-10).

**What Telo must give.** Committed purchase volume and a quality agreement. The direction of that trade runs against the market: the one disclosed US CDMO capacity agreement puts annual committed capacity obligations on the CDMO with no minimum purchase obligation on the customer (LCDM-S14). Telo would be offering the mirror image of the only disclosed instrument, and the evidence base contains no commercial instance of it.

**What the package cannot score.** Data rights, channel conflict and commercial control are not represented in the engine at all; a partner site is usable or it is not (`os_only_and_virtual_network.md` section 3.2). Three of the assignment's eight virtual-versus-owned comparison axes are therefore unscored in either direction.

**Two constraints on any layer that writes records.** 21 CFR 211.22(a) puts approve-or-reject authority in the customer's own quality unit (LPMS-S20), and FDA has applied 21 CFR 211.22(c) to AI-generated GMP records in Warning Letter 320-26-58 (F2-S52, LPMS-S21). Records a machine writes are still GMP records, so a record-writing layer transfers inspection exposure to whoever's quality unit signs (`architecture_taxonomy.md` incompatibility 15). Compliance state also persists across inspection cycles and changes of ownership (LCDM-S2, LCDM-S7), so qualification state has to be re-measured rather than stored once. That re-measurement is the one data asset the landscape records as unoccupied: every platform answers where a drug is made now, and none answers who could make it by when (LSD section 5.1, `competitive_landscape.md` 4.2).

## 5. Regulatory exposure

Gates that apply on day one, all UNCERTAIN, all listed for S12, S13, S16 and S17 in `config/regulatory_gates.yaml`: G01 application ownership or a binding CMO relationship; G02 site in the approved application with establishment registration and drug listing; G03 product, process and cleaning validation; G04 aseptic processing or terminal sterilization validated; G05 analytical methods transferred; G06 stability and container-closure; G07 change-control category and submission. G13 (per-unit registration approach) and G14 (cross-site comparability) apply to every multi-site design in the battery. G16 attaches only where a second API or container source is declared (S9, S18, S19), so not to S12, S13 or S16 as configured. G15 attaches to S6 alone, because this roadmap makes no release-assurance claim.

**The hard gate is G07.** Whether adding a third-party sterile fill site to an approved application is a CBE-30-class change or a prior approval supplement with a preapproval inspection decides the family. FDA's 2004 changes guidance permits CBE-30 only for a move into an aseptic area already making similar approved products in the same container type with a satisfactory inspection (F3-S45 VI.A, VI.B.1, VI.B.2, VI.B.4). GDUFA III gives six months for a standard PAS without a preapproval inspection and ten with, and a facility not in the original ANDA defaults to the inspection path (F9-S11, F9-S12). If the answer is PAS plus inspection, S12's 365-day leg does not exist and the family loses its central advantage over building (falsification register data rows 13 and 27).

**The second hard constraint is the quality unit**, section 1 above. A related question stays open: whether a labeler operating under a partner's ANDA has any disposition authority at all (F3-S41, F3-S42, F3-S43, F3-S52).

**Everything stays UNCERTAIN until HA-31 assigns a status with a name, date and rationale**, per section E of the human-action queue, with HA-23 supplying the professional reading. Two instruments are unavailable rather than uncertain: the DME route (F3-R4) and parametric release across more than one site or autoclave (F11-S46).

## 10. Moat, and the prior-art reason

**There is no moat in the virtual network itself, on this evidence.** Every element is `already_implemented` with the source ids in the opening paragraphs, and the incumbent is Civica at 14 partners and 77 products (F3-S25). Falsification register data row 14 carries the claim "Telo beats Civica at this architecture" at status `untested`; its threshold column reads "falsified today: no such reason is in the evidence", and the row marks S12 as a comparator rather than a recommendation. The pre-declared test is to state an evidence-backed reason Telo wins against an incumbent running 14 partners and 77 products (F3-S25); no such reason is in this package.

**Three narrow positions survive prior art, and all three belong to the standby and rotation shape rather than to plain contracting** (`prior_art_review.md` section 6): 6.3, a maintained regulator-pre-agreed switching package (F9-S37); 6.4, campaign rotation across several prequalified sites as a network scheduling policy (F7-S02); 6.6, manufacturer-side capacity coordination across independent owners (F2-S63, F2-S64, F3-S19). The competitive landscape independently names contract-linked assurance that reserved capacity will still pass an inspection when called (4.1) and a contractible registry of activatable capacity with time-to-first-qualified-batch (4.2).

**Each of the three carries its own discount.** 6.3 is capped by FDA's own carve-out at CBE-30 rather than CBE-0 and is conditioned on the receiving site's compliance state at implementation, so the asset is worth about 30 days plus predictability and not a step change (F9-S37). 6.4 requires allocation rights over several independent owners' calendars, which is the same missing contracting vehicle as 6.6. 6.6 is an antitrust question first, and ASPR is already funding the registry half (LGOV-S17, LGOV-S51). The model can value none of them: allocation rights are inert because every region's criticality weight is 1.0 and no minimum guarantee is passed (NEW-2), and take-or-pay enters only the cost ledger with no service channel (MD-24).

**The OS is not the moat.** Six of the eight sub-forms are already implemented, with Korber PAS-X at more than half the top 30 (LPMS-S01, LPMS-S02), Veeva and MasterControl in quality workflow (LQBR-S04, LQBR-S07 to LQBR-S09), and Sartorius SIMCA-online in drift monitoring (LPMS-S13). Emerson already ships the furthest an OS goes toward release, immediate release once exceptions close (LPMS-S03, LPMS-S26).

## 11. Channel conflict

The engine represents none of it. There is no competing channel for a partner's own output and no control right over a partner's calendar (`os_only_and_virtual_network.md` section 3.2). What the evidence names:

1. **The partner sees Telo as a rival for its own slots.** The priority call that makes a reservation worth anything is exactly what made industry refuse BARDA capacity (F7-S01, F3-S19).
2. **The purchaser can write the same contract itself.** Three GPOs cover more than 90% of US hospitals (LSIM-S20), both large GPOs already run committed-volume and buffer programmes, and Premier took equity in Exela with eleven health systems plus multi-year offtake (LNPM-S18, LSIM-S18, LSIM-S19). Selling outside that channel means selling against it; selling inside it means competing on price, which is what the channel optimises.
3. **A partner CDMO that holds its own ANDA competes with Telo's label** on the same shelf.
4. **Selling the OS later damages the network position.** Federated cross-owner intelligence is pre-release, and its recorded falsifier is a documented CDMO refusal, which would make the category commercially closed (F2-S32, F2-S26).

Whether any of this binds is HA-24 and HA-33. It is not decidable from anything in this package.

Compliance state persists across inspection cycles and ownership changes (LCDM-S2, LCDM-S7), and PharMEDium is the documented case of one quality organisation spanning several sterile sites failing under stress (F4-S7, F4-S8, F4-S9).

## 14. What this roadmap cannot answer with the current package

1. **Whether a software company built on this layer is a good business.** The engine has no revenue side, no customer entity, no site count outside Telo's network, no contract value and no churn. The three cents per delivered unit of operational value is an upper bound on an upper bound, because the ablation also removes batch rejection and yield variance, which no OS claims to do.
2. **Data rights, channel conflict and commercial control**, three of the assignment's eight comparison axes, are not represented in the engine in either direction.
3. **Whether readiness can be bought.** `activation_failure_probability` is a per-plan constant with no link to the exercise cadence (NEW-3), and a banked campaign batch blocks an exercise batch (MD-23). S17's norepinephrine cell shows 0 activations against 72.54 exercise batches over five years, so its service comes from routine contracted output and not from standby.
4. **How much of the contracting-versus-owning gap is architecture.** The comparators searched safety stock to 90 or 120 days with no daily base-stock review, while every design-space strategy searched to 365 days with the review as a binary (MD-12). Re-optimized over one common inventory space, added central capacity S4 becomes feasible on both products at 20.0M and 23.7M USD/yr, which corrects an earlier statement that no frozen comparator meets the target (`feasibility_regions.md` section 8; NF-9).
5. **Any clean cost ranking**, because of MD-14 residual and free opening inventory.
6. **Portfolio behaviour.** One product runs per network (MD-15) and the two configured presentations are near-clones on several axes (WB-26), so two products are not a portfolio.
7. **Affordability.** No price exists in 313 landscape sources. USP records that 74% of sterile injectables in shortage price below 15 USD per unit and 44% below 5 USD, n = 61 (F12-S14), while the model's own status quo needs about 14.3 to 14.4 USD per unit to break even, so the cost model sits at or above the top of that band and is not calibrated to a commodity vial (NF-7).
8. **Whether Telo beats the incumbent.** No evidenced differentiator against Civica exists in this package (falsification register data row 14, status `untested`).
