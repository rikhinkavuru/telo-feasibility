## 1. Scope

This family asks whether Telo can reduce sterile-injectable shortage risk by holding inventory in a less-differentiated state and finishing it to a specific presentation, label, or market only when demand is known: formulated bulk solution held before filling; a concentrated presentation diluted or repackaged downstream; a common container format and a standardized vial-and-stopper system shared across products or labelers; filled but unlabeled units ("bright stock") labeled and packed late; final market configuration at a hub; and pooled inventory upstream of the point at which product identity is fixed. In Telo's model terms the question is which of these moves changes safety stock, changeover, expiry scrap, component common-cause exposure, release timing, or contract structure, and which are blocked by hold-time, stability, sterility, labeling, and application rules.

## 3. What already exists

**Postponement as an operations concept.** Lee and Tang formalized the cost-benefit trade of delaying the point of differentiation and named three routes: standardization, modular design, and process restructuring [F5-S01]. The first documented industrial case is HP's DeskJet-Plus: the factory shipped a generic printer and distribution centers added the power supply and manual; the paper reports lower inventory at equal fill rate from risk pooling [F5-S02]. A 2025 IJPE paper models a multi-tier pharmaceutical network under demand uncertainty and finds postponement, decentralization, redundancy, and chaining each cost-optimal in different regimes and all better than the industry-standard design; it frames this explicitly as a shortage-prevention lever [F5-S03]. A 2012 ETH Zurich survey found only a few documented pharmaceutical applications and concluded that labeling and packaging postponement, enabled by packaging standardization, are the preferred forms in pharma [F5-S04].

**Late labeling and packaging of injectables ("bright stock").** This is standard practice, not a concept. 21 CFR 211.130(b) (1978 rule, amended 1993) requires written procedures for "filled drug product containers that are set aside and held in unlabeled condition for future labeling operations" [F5-S08]. Trade coverage describes bright stock manufacturing (large batches stored unlabeled, labeled just before shipment) as an established strategy whose main risk is content identity at labeling [F5-S42]. Equipment vendors sell purpose-built vial-cap coders for it: WLS marks a 2D code and time stamp on the crimp of unlabeled 5 to 500 cc vials at 150 per minute [F5-S44]; WILCO built cap coding for a customer whose vials "are shipped throughout the world prior to being labelled" [F5-S45]. Contract packagers offer it as a service: PCI's Late Stage Customization holds stock "in its lowest common denominator form" and digitally prints labels, blisters, and cartons on order [F5-S46]; Sharp cites the EU's up-to-27-SKU problem as the driver [F5-S47]. Amgen Manufacturing Limited "has been utilizing postponement for several emerging markets for a number of years"; the thesis gives no motive for that existing use [F5-S05]. What the thesis proposes is broadening postponement to syringe drug product, and its stated rationale for the proposal is that FDA-driven requirements had raised the likelihood of non-conformances, which "increases the production lead time variability" [F5-S05]. The proposed design holds "unrestricted IDP inventory" (product that has moved through Formulation, Filling, and Inspection including NC resolution) so that packaging, release, and shipment take about three weeks instead of more than three months [F5-S05]. A 2021 MIT capstone for a rare-disease injectable company modeled "naked vials" held at a 3PL and labeled to country on order, cutting the finished-goods safety-stock horizon from 104 days to the 5-day labeling process [F5-S06].

**Common container formats and standardized vial-stopper systems.** ISO 8362-1:2018 "specifies the form, dimensions and capacities of glass vials for injectable preparations" and was confirmed unchanged in 2024 [F5-S36]. The fetched ISO page carries the abstract only and lists no format designations, so the familiar nR designations are not sourced here. Ready-to-use nested vials are sold as an industry-standard tub format: SCHOTT adaptiQ, offered in format "2-50R" and "meeting ISO 11040-7 and ISO 21882 industry standards" [F5-S37], and Stevanato EZ-fill (2R to 50R in nest-and-tub or tray, snippet only) [F5-S38]. Both RTU platforms stop at 50R. Container qualification is compendial: USP <1207> for package integrity of sterile products [F5-S39] and USP <1660> for glass inner-surface durability and delamination [F5-S40]. The US government has treated vial supply as a strategic bottleneck: BARDA paid Corning $204M in June 2020 to expand Valor Glass vial capacity with priority access for designated partners [F5-S34], and worked with DoD's JPEO-CBRND and SiO2 Materials Science to scale domestic vial capacity, SiO2 having "invented a hybrid material which combines the benefits of both glass and plastic" used for its vaccine vials [F5-S58]. The $143M value of that award is carried only by the company press release, whose URL bot-blocks automated fetch (snippet only) [F5-S35].

**Bulk before fill, and pooled inventory before differentiation.** In vaccines this is the government's standing design. BARDA's National Pre-Pandemic Influenza Vaccine Stockpile (since 2005) holds "pre-pandemic influenza virus bulk antigen and final containers of vaccine" plus adjuvants (AS03, MF59), with continuous stability monitoring and contracts in place for fill-finish on demand [F5-S28, F5-S29]. The 2016 BARDA contract with Protein Sciences (Sanofi) has separate line items for commercial-scale bulk lots, storage and stability of bulk lots and final containers for up to 120 months, and formulation and fill into vials, syringes, or multidose vials at concentrations "determined by HHS" [F5-S30]. At the API level, the 2020 BARDA-Phlow contract (CLIN 0004) funds a Strategic Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient Reserve facility to "store and maintain Strategic Reserve of Essential APIs, Precursor Chemical Ingredients, and Select Finished Products" under a validated CGMP warehouse system [F5-S31, F5-S32]. Phlow's own white paper says API has a 3-to-5-year retest period against about two years of finished-product shelf life, estimates $596M per year for an API reserve against $1.3B for the equivalent finished-product stockpile, and states that "Legislation may be needed" for FDA to let multiple product manufacturers use the reserve's API (snippet only) [F5-S33]. In vaccine networks the KU Leuven simulation found that keeping batches at the drug-substance site longer improves allocation flexibility across drug-product sites [F5-S07].

**Concentrate versus ready-to-use.** Concentrates are the historical default presentation for many injectables; safety bodies have spent decades moving them out of care areas. The Joint Commission defines concentrated electrolytes as solutions "manufactured and distributed with the intention of being diluted prior to administration" and, as a general rule, keeps them out of patient care areas [F5-S23]. FDA's 2022 label guidance reserves the black closure system for Potassium Chloride for Injection Concentrate and recommends affirmative statements such as "Must Dilute Before Use" [F5-S22]; Health Canada's label-practices guide, whose preamble frames its content as recommendations, advises that for liquid products requiring dilution sponsors "include a relevant warning statement (such as 'Dilute Before Use') on the principal display panel of both inner and outer labels," and names potassium chloride concentrate as a product requiring the warning on the container closure (snippet only) [F5-S50]. The repository's own verified finding is that the market pays about 3x per mg for RTU over the same molecule in a vial and that the RTU niche is being genericized where Telo aimed [F5-S51, F5-S52]. The repository's drug-selection work records the same occupancy one molecule over: "Dopamine's RTU niche is occupied twice (Baxter NDA019615, Hospira NDA018826)," which is why its 40/80 mg/mL concentrate, not its premix bag, is the tractable target there [F5-S56]. In the other direction, ASHP's shortage-conservation guidance tells hospitals to move to IV push, ready-to-administer syringes prepared in pharmacy, and 503B admixtures, and to check stock compatible with "proprietary bag-and-vial systems" (Vial2Bag, ADD-Vantage, Mini-Bag Plus) [F5-S24, F5-S25]. Those docking systems are point-of-care postponement devices, and the <797> FAQ splits them in two. "Docking and activation of proprietary bag and vial systems in accordance with the manufacturer's labeling for immediate administration to an individual patient is not considered compounding and may be performed outside of an ISO Class 5 environment" (FAQ 13). "Docking of the proprietary bag and vial systems for future activation and administration is considered compounding and must be performed in an ISO Class 5 environment in accordance with <797>," and BUDs for those systems "must not be longer than those specified in the manufacturer's labeling" (FAQ 14) [F5-S26]. The shortage-conservation use ASHP describes is immediate administration, so it falls on the not-compounding side; only stockpiling docked units ahead of demand, which is the postponement move, crosses into compounding [inference].

**503B repackaging and compounding from approved product versus bulk.** FDA's 2017 repackaging guidance defines repackaging as moving a finished product to a different container without manipulation and sets BUDs for outsourcing-facility repackaging by reference to the 503B CGMP guidance [F5-S19]. The 503B CGMP revised draft (January 2020, Revision 2) splits release testing by starting material for non-sterile products only: Appendix A "Table A. Conditions Regarding Batch Release Tests for Non-Sterile Drug Products" carries rows 1a "Starting from FDA-approved product" and 1b "Starting from bulk drug substance," where 1b expects identity and strength testing even below 60 units. The sterile table, "Table B. Conditions Regarding Batch Release Tests for Sterile Drug Products," has no starting-material split at all; under its condition 2, "Batch size <60 units, if omitted tests are performed once 60 units are produced," identity and strength are both marked as tests FDA generally does not intend to act on, whichever the starting material. In Appendix B Table D the same guidance gives the default BUD for an aseptically processed, unpreserved product released without a completed sterility test as 6 days at controlled room temperature, 9 days refrigerated, 45 days frozen, for aggregate batch size at or below 1,000 units [F5-S20]. FDA's October 2024 temporary policy for certain parenteral products repeats that table structure for aggregate batches up to 5,000 units (snippet only) [F5-S21].

## 4. What was tried and failed or discontinued, and the documented reasons

**Mislabeling of held unlabeled stock.** The documented failure mode of late labeling is content-label mismatch. Bayer's July 2019 US recall of two lots of Kogenate FS 2000 IU arose because "a batch of Jivi 3000 IU expired unlabelled vials were erroneously mislabelled as Kogenate FS 2000 IU during a production run"; about 990 vials reached the US market and the letter reports one myocardial infarction under monitoring [F5-S48]. Viatris recalled a batch of insulin glargine-yfgn in April 2022 "due to the potential for the label to be missing on some vials" [F5-S49]. Trade coverage frames the entire bright-stock engineering problem as making sure "the contents of each and every bottle match its label on application" [F5-S42]. These are reasons for unit-level coding before hold (F5-S44, F5-S45), not reasons the practice was abandoned.

**Bulk-solution holding as inventory.** No case was found of an aqueous small-molecule sterile injectable held as bulk solution as a demand buffer. The documented reason is regulatory: hold times must be defined and validated, and EMA expects re-filtration if a sterile-filtered bulk is not filled within 24 hours [F5-S16]; Annex 1 requires a maximum permissible time between preparation and filling and a validated maximum for each aseptic stage [F5-S15]; FDA expects time limits from start of bulk compounding through sterilization and filtration [F5-S10, F5-S13]. Absence of a case is not proof it was tried and failed; it is consistent with the rule set making it unattractive [inference].

**Postponement in pharma generally.** The ETH survey (2012) and Pharma Manufacturing (2021) both record that pharma adoption has been rare relative to electronics and apparel, attributing it to complexity and limited awareness rather than to failed pilots [F5-S04, F5-S43]. No discontinued named program was found.

**SAPIR-to-finished-product conversion.** Phlow's white paper concedes the pooled-API model needs regulatory change ("Legislation may be needed") and capacity contracts with product manufacturers before the reserve can supply multiple ANDA holders (snippet only) [F5-S33]. Whether the SAPIR facility funded under CLIN 0004 has been used to convert API into shortage products is not established by any source fetched here; the repository's USASpending probe records obligations through 2027 but no such use [F5-S57]. Treat as an unconfirmed lead (section 9).

**Concentrates as the "flexible" presentation.** The 25-year patient-safety campaign against concentrated electrolytes in care areas [F5-S23, F5-S22] and the market's 3x RTU premium [F5-S51] are documented reasons the industry moved differentiation earlier (to the manufacturer's premix), not later. A postponement architecture that reintroduces concentrates at the hospital runs against both [inference].

## 5. Regulatory basis and constraints

- **Holding unlabeled filled units is expressly contemplated.** 21 CFR 211.130(b) requires identification and handling procedures for containers "held in unlabeled condition for future labeling operations"; identification "need not be applied to each individual container" but must suffice to determine name, strength, quantity, and lot [F5-S08]. 211.122(d) requires separate storage of labels by product and strength; 211.122(g) requires, for cut labeling on immediate containers, one of four special control procedures: dedication of lines to each strength of each product; 100-percent examination by electronic or electromechanical equipment; 100-percent visual examination of hand-applied labeling by one person and independently verified by a second; or "Use of any automated technique, including differentiation by labeling size and shape, that physically prevents incorrect labeling from being processed by labeling and packaging equipment" [F5-S09]. The fourth option is the one most available to a bright-stock hub, since cap coding applied before hold is exactly such an automated technique [inference]. Unit-level cap coding (F5-S44, F5-S45) is the industry's implementation of 211.130(b) and 211.122(g)(2) [inference].
- **A late-labeling hub is a registered establishment.** 21 CFR 207.1 defines "relabel" and "relabeler," and defines "manufacturer" to include "contract packers, contract labelers"; a registrant is anyone who "manufactures, repacks, relabels, or salvages a drug" [F5-S12]. Whether a hub owned by Telo labeling another firm's ANDA product registers as a relabeler or as a contract manufacturer, and which listing obligations follow, is UNCERTAIN.
- **The labeling site must be in the application, but the change category is light.** FDA's 2004 guidance places "A move to a different manufacturing site for labeling" and "for secondary packaging" among minor changes, provided the new site has a satisfactory CGMP inspection for that operation; a move of primary packaging or of aseptic processing is a Changes Being Effected in 30 Days supplement or, for some sterile operations, a prior approval supplement [F5-S14]. The repository already records 314.70 as the governing rule and leaves the category to a reviewer (gate G07) [F5-S55]. Reading: adding a labeling or secondary-packaging site is the cheapest site change in the whole family, and primary packaging (filling) is not [inference; category assignment UNCERTAIN].
- **Expiry runs from manufacture, not from labeling.** 211.137 requires an expiration date determined by stability testing [F5-S11]. WHO's hold-time guidance is explicit that shelf life "irrespective of hold times" is measured from the time actives are mixed with other ingredients [F5-S17]. Postponement therefore consumes shelf life while product waits in the undifferentiated state; it cannot extend it [inference from F5-S11, F5-S17].
- **Bulk-solution holds are time-limited.** 211.111 [F5-S10]; FDA 2004 section VIII (time limits from start of bulk compounding through filtration, supported by bioburden and endotoxin data) [F5-S13]; EU Annex 1 8.5 (maximum permissible time between preparation and filling) and 8.18 (validated maximum for each aseptic stage, including holding time for sterilised product prior to filling) [F5-S15]; EMA sterilisation guideline (re-filter if not filled within 24 hours unless justified; holding and filling times over 24 hours need justification and risk assessment) [F5-S16]. Hold-time studies are expected at development and confirmed in validation (WHO TRS 992 Annex 4, scoped to non-sterile solids with principles applicable to liquids and excluding biologicals) [F5-S17]. The ICH Q1 Step 2 draft (April 2025) adds a new section 9 on holding times for intermediates "including bulk drug products," says intermediates should not be used beyond established holding times, and says packaged intermediates stored outside the process may instead carry a re-test period or shelf life [F5-S18]. Q1 was a draft at access; check Step 4 status before citing as final.
- **Repackaging and 503B BUDs.** Repackaging by an outsourcing facility follows the 503B CGMP guidance for BUDs [F5-S19]. Under the revised draft, the default BUD without a completed sterility test for an unpreserved aseptic product is 6 days at room temperature (Table D) [F5-S20]; the October 2024 temporary policy uses the same structure at a 5,000-unit aggregate threshold (snippet only) [F5-S21]. The repository's 6-day at-risk figure was secondary-sourced (HA-17) [F5-S54]; this dossier supplies the primary text. Two corrections to the repository: release-constraints.md attributes the 6-day table to "Appendix A.B" [F5-S53]; in the revised draft it is Appendix B, Table D. The "90-day assured BUD" in the same memo is a QuVa company claim, not a value in the FDA table; the FDA default with a completed passing sterility test is 28 days at room temperature and 42 days refrigerated for an unpreserved product, and 42 days at either condition for a preserved one (F5-S20 Table D, third processing row, read in the fetched text), so a 90-day BUD requires product-specific stability data under Appendix B section C or a full 211.166 program. The same guidance requires stability samples to be evaluated "with the same or representative label and adhesive that will be affixed to the container," so a late-labeling design must carry the label stock through the stability program rather than treat labeling as inert [F5-S20]. UNCERTAIN: whether the revised draft's Table D or the 2024 temporary policy governs a given facility today.
- **503B from bulk versus from approved product.** The starting-material split (identity and strength expected below 60 units from bulk drug substance, relaxed from FDA-approved product) is an Appendix A Table A provision and governs non-sterile drug products only [F5-S20]. For sterile products, Table B condition 2 relaxes both identity and strength below 60 units irrespective of starting material, so this constraint does not bind a sterile postponement pool [F5-S20]. The Table B provision that does sit on this family's topic is condition 5, which for solutions and total parenteral nutrition only relaxes finished-product identity and strength where "Bulk solution but not finished drug product is tested for identity and strength immediately before filling into final and prelabeled drug product containers" [F5-S20]. That is a codified bulk-test-then-fill release scheme; note that the containers it contemplates are prelabeled, which cuts against the same design holding the filled units unlabeled [inference]. The essentially-a-copy and bulks-list constraints already mapped in the repository (gates G09, G10) still apply [F5-S54]. A postponement pool of 503B product made from approved product is repackaging or dilution and is limited by the source product's expiry and in-use time (F5-S19 condition 8). UNCERTAIN pending reviewer.
- **Concentrates carry labeling and closure rules.** FDA reserves the black cap and ferrule for potassium chloride concentrate and recommends affirmative warnings such as "Must Dilute Before Use" [F5-S22]; Joint Commission standards restrict where concentrated electrolytes may be stored [F5-S23]. A concentrate-based postponement design inherits these as hard constraints on presentation and hospital handling [inference].
- **Common vial and stopper across applications.** Each application names its container closure system; changing vial glass or stopper supplier is a post-approval change assessed against USP <381>, <660>, <1207>, and <1660> under 314.70 (FDA CCS change guidance, snippet only) [F5-S41, F5-S39, F5-S40]. A standardized vial-stopper system therefore does not transfer across ANDAs by itself; every holder must qualify it in its own application. UNCERTAIN on category per change.
- **EU angle.** Annex 1 paragraphs 8.5 and 8.18 [F5-S15] and the EMA sterilisation guideline [F5-S16] are the binding references for any EU-supplied node. The distributed manufacturing proposed rule (FR 2026-14073) excludes, per the repository's secondary read, "unaffiliated contract manufacturers and third-party relabelers/repackers under a different quality system" from a DME [F5-S59]; this bears on whether a labeling hub could sit inside a DME. The repository has the primary text frozen but unanalyzed [F5-S55, F5-S57]. UNCERTAIN.

## 6. Adjacent-industry analogues

**Consumer electronics (HP DeskJet-Plus, 1993).** HP redesigned the printer so the power-supply module and manual were added last, shipped a generic unit by sea to European and Asian distribution centers, and localized there; the paper reports lower safety stock at equal fill rate and lower transport cost because the generic unit is less bulky, against a one-time cost of product redesign and DC capability [F5-S02]. The transferable structure is a cheap, fast, low-risk final step performed after demand is known [inference]. Late labeling of vials matches that shape; bulk-solution holding does not, because the final step (aseptic fill) is the expensive, slow, high-risk step [inference].

**Vaccines (pandemic influenza stockpile).** The NPIVS is a government-owned pooled inventory held as bulk antigen and separate adjuvant, with fill-finish contracted in advance and stability tracked for up to 120 months [F5-S28, F5-S29, F5-S30]. It works because bulk antigen is stable for years under controlled storage and because the buyer, the payer, and the release owner are the same entity [inference]. Neither condition holds for an aqueous small-molecule injectable sold to hospitals [inference from F5-S16, F5-S17].

**API reserves (SAPIR).** The Phlow contract is the only US instance found of a funded, CGMP-managed pooled inventory upstream of product differentiation for essential medicines [F5-S31, F5-S32]. It belongs to family 9 (upstream reserves) more than to family 5, and its conversion path to finished product is, by its sponsor's own account, not yet enabled (snippet only) [F5-S33].

## 8. Implications for the model

Telo's simulation (config keys read from `feasibility/config/`) carries per-product `shelf_life_months`, `target_safety_stock_days`, `changeover_days`, `units_per_batch`, `release_time_days`, `production_cycle_days`, `material_lead_time_days`, `expiry_scrap_fraction`, `delivery_days`, `variable_conversion_usd_per_unit`, `distribution_usd_per_unit`, `fixed_qa_labor_usd_per_site_year`, a `standard_vial` flag, and network keys `n_regions`, `common_cause_*`, `supplier_disruptions_per_supplier_year`, `second_source_qualification_days`, `reserved_capacity_*`, `qa_review_days`, `sterility_incubation_days`, `inventory_carrying_rate`, `emergency_transport_days`. The current products (`norepinephrine_1mgml_4ml`, `sodium_bicarbonate_8_4_50ml`) each have one presentation, so the model as configured cannot show any postponement benefit; the benefit in Lee and Tang scales with the number of variants sharing the common stage and with how imperfectly their demands are correlated [F5-S01]. Mechanism by mechanism:

- **Capacity.** Late labeling does not change fill capacity. It reduces label-driven changeovers if the line currently switches label variants inside a campaign; the parameter is `changeover_days` (per label variant, not per molecule) and `units_per_batch` (larger common batches). Source for the direction: F5-S42, F5-S05. No source gives a generic-injectable magnitude.
- **Commissioning delay.** A labeling or secondary-packaging site is a minor site change once CGMP-inspected for that operation [F5-S14]; this is a shorter lead time than gate G02/G07 assumes for a fill site. Replace: a separate `labeling_site_change_days` (not present today) instead of reusing the fill-site supplement lead time. Category assignment UNCERTAIN.
- **Common-cause groups.** A common vial-stopper system across products creates a shared-component common-cause group: one supplier event hits every product in the pool. The 2020 vial-capacity contracts show the government treats vial supply as a single point of failure [F5-S34, F5-S35]. Replace: apply `supplier_disruptions_per_supplier_year` and `supplier_disruption_duration_days` to a component group shared across products rather than per product, and let `second_source_qualification_days` reflect that a qualified alternate vial requires a 314.70 change per application [F5-S41].
- **Release components.** Bright stock does not shorten `sterility_incubation_days`, `assay_days`, `endotoxin_days`, `environmental_monitoring_days`, or `qa_review_days`; the sterility clock still starts at fill (repository finding, F5-S53). It adds a post-hold step: labeling, packaging, and a second disposition. The MIT capstone puts the labeling process at 5 days [F5-S06]; Amgen's proposal put pack-release-ship at under three weeks [F5-S05]. Replace: add `postponement_finish_days` (base 5 from F5-S06) on the path from pool to customer. The 21-day upper endpoint suggested by F5-S05 is an extrapolation: it is pack-release-ship for prefilled biologic syringes at one site, not a generic-injectable measurement, and no source gives a finish time for a US generic sterile injectable [inference]. Whether the unlabeled pool is held as released or quarantined product is a design variable with different `qa_review_days` consequences.
- **Inventory policy.** The undifferentiated pool becomes a second echelon. Risk pooling lowers `target_safety_stock_days` for the finished tier; the pool's own safety stock covers only the finish lead time plus demand over it [F5-S06, F5-S02]. Shelf life is consumed in the pool: `shelf_life_months` counts from manufacture [F5-S11, F5-S17], so `expiry_scrap_fraction` falls only if pooled units turn faster than labeled units did. A bulk-solution pool for aqueous products should be modeled with a hold cap of about one day [F5-S16] and therefore contributes no inventory. A frozen or API-level pool belongs to family 9 and uses API retest periods of 3 to 5 years (Phlow claim, snippet only) [F5-S33].
- **Contract terms.** Late labeling makes "bright stock supply" a contractable object: one filler, several application holders or private-label distributors, each with a quality agreement and its own registration (207.1 roles) [F5-S12]. The 503B route is bounded by repackaging BUDs (source expiry or in-use time) and the 6-day default when releasing without sterility results [F5-S19, F5-S20]. Replace: a `bright_stock_offtake_fraction` per labeler and a BUD cap for any 503B-pooled variant; the model currently has no labeler dimension.
- **Cost structure.** Unlabeled units carry lower value in inventory (HP's observation, F5-S02); labeling and pack cost moves to the hub (`variable_conversion_usd_per_unit` split into fill and finish); the hub adds fixed cost (coding, vision, serialization equipment: F5-S44, F5-S45) and QA labor. The repository's RTU premium (about 3x per mg, F5-S51) is the revenue-side reason to differentiate late into RTU rather than concentrate, if a molecule with an open RTU niche exists [F5-S52].

Net reading for the design space: this family changes inventory and changeover parameters, not the binding release and sterility parameters that the illustrative runs identified. It is a complement to a multi-product or multi-labeler node (family 6) and to upstream reserves (family 9), not a standalone shortage intervention [inference].
