# Family 4 prior art: hub-and-spoke manufacturing

Prepared 2026-09-02 for the adversarial design-space review (Phase C). Adversarial stance: the question is whether any sub-idea in this family is unoccupied, not whether it rescues the microplant thesis. All regulatory readings are preliminary and not legal advice; items marked UNCERTAIN need a qualified CMC/GMP reviewer (HA-31 class). Inferences are marked "[inference]".

## 1. Scope

Family 4 covers architectures in which one central site (the hub) owns a step or a function and several regional sites (spokes) own the rest: centralized formulation with regional fill-finish; a centralized quality laboratory serving distributed production; centralized release oversight over regional inventory; centralized bulk production with regional packaging or final configuration; and a centralized sterile core with distributed nonsterile or downstream operations. In Telo's terms the family asks whether the fixed quality cost, replicated validation cost, and release-queue burden that sink S1-S6 under illustrative inputs can be pooled at a hub while spokes keep the geographic proximity the thesis wants. The price of pooling is transport, hold time, stability, container, validation, and chain-of-custody burden, plus a new common-cause group (every spoke depends on the hub).

## 3. What already exists

**Drug-substance hub with separate drug-product (formulation plus fill) sites.** This is the standard biologics and vaccine topology and was run at pandemic scale in 2020-2021. Pfizer's own July 2020 infographic describes plasmid DNA at St. Louis, mRNA drug substance at Andover, and formulation, fill and finish at Kalamazoo, with Puurs for Europe (F4-S2). Pfizer authors report the initial EUA network as one plasmid node, two mRNA drug-substance nodes, and four LNP/drug-product nodes, growing to 20 facilities on 4 continents, and report a 50 percent reduction in manufacturing-and-release time from DNA template start to completion of filling and testing (F4-S3). Moderna split drug substance to Lonza (Portsmouth, NH and Visp) and fill-finish to Catalent Bloomington under separate agreements (F4-S4, F4-S5). Note what this precedent is and is not: it is a drug-substance hub feeding sites that each do formulation, sterile filtration, fill, inspection, packaging, and release testing (F4-S2). It is not a formulated-sterile-bulk hub feeding fill-only spokes. [inference] The hub-to-spoke intermediate was a frozen, non-sterile-critical drug substance, which removes most of the Annex 1 hold-time burden that a formulated aqueous injectable would carry.

**Government-prequalified fill-finish spokes.** BARDA established the Fill Finish Manufacturing Network on 2013-09-27 with four contract sites (Cook Pharmica, Patheon, Par, Nanotherapeutics), added two in 2016, and describes it as supporting an additional 117 million influenza doses in a pandemic; the 2010 HHS review that motivated it found that vaccine makers "lacked the capacity to rapidly fill vaccine vials and finish packaging vaccine in sufficient quantities for the nation" (F4-S1). GAO reported in 2021 that three industry groups described a shortage of fill-finish facilities as a production bottleneck (F4-S6). The FFMN is the closest government precedent for "centralized bulk, regional fill" as a preparedness asset, and it predates Telo by more than a decade.

**Centralized QC laboratories serving distributed production.** Pfizer states that QC testing for Comirnaty "was supported by central multipurpose labs in Chesterfield and Andover, as well as the local labs supporting each facility" (F4-S3). The regulatory architecture for a hub lab has existed since 21 CFR 207.1 named "control laboratories, contract laboratories" as manufacturers (Lreg-S5) and since 21 CFR 211.22(a) made the owner's quality unit responsible for products "held under contract by another company" (Lreg-S3, Lreg-S9). In blood banking, Creative Testing Solutions runs five high-volume donor testing laboratories that test collections from many regional centers, each sited near a major airport (F4-S24). Cell-therapy literature has modeled partial decentralization of QC as financially feasible (Lpeer-S2).

**Centralized release with forward inventory.** EU Annex 16 §4.1 permits an uncertified batch to be "shipped under quarantine to another site which has been approved for that purpose by the relevant Competent Authority" (Lreg-S14), and 21 CFR 211.142(a) requires quarantine before quality-unit release without specifying where (Lreg-S4). Quva operates two dedicated forward distribution centers (Phoenix, 40,000 sq ft; Sugar Land, 20,000 sq ft) fed by its 503B production facilities (F4-S12), and states that all release testing is performed in on-site laboratories with every batch sterility-tested by ScanRDI (F4-S11). FDA's 2026 proposed distributed manufacturing establishment rule would formalize "a single quality unit" at a hub over several units (Lrepo-S2, Lrepo-S6).

**Centralized bulk with regional packaging, labeling, or final configuration.** Contract packaging networks are mature: PCI describes US Packaging Centers of Excellence in Philadelphia and Rockford with capacity for 75 million vials per year alongside sterile fill-finish campuses in San Diego and Bedford (F4-S22). FDA's 2004 changes guidance treats labeling, secondary packaging, and testing site changes as a lower reporting category than moves of aseptic processing (Lreg-S11). For 503B outsourcing facilities, FDA's 2017 repackaging guidance sets the conditions under which finished products may be repackaged into a different container (Lreg-S12). Civica's model is a labeling-and-NDC hub over contract ANDA holders (Xellia made vancomycin and daptomycin under Xellia's ANDA with Civica labeling and NDC) plus one owned plant in Petersburg (F4-S17, F4-S18).

**Centralized sterile core with distributed downstream.** Contract terminal sterilizers already sterilize filled vials and syringes for pharmaceutical customers (F4-S21). Nephron runs blow-fill-seal production with automated packaging and distribution at a single 840,000 sq ft campus (F4-S16). The nuclear pharmacy network (130 pharmacies including 3 central pharmacies and more than 30 PET sites, Cardinal Health) is the largest US example of a sterile hub feeding regional dose preparation and delivery (F4-S23).

**503B networks.** Multi-site 503B operators exist: Quva (Sugar Land and Bloomsbury production, plus forward DCs; FDA list Lreg-S17), Fagron Sterile Services (Wichita and Boston/Canton), SCA (Windsor and Little Rock), Leiters (Englewood plus a Buena, NJ site listed as "Not yet inspected" on the FDA list). None of them describes a hub laboratory serving another site; Quva, Fagron, and SCA each describe on-site QC labs at each production site (F4-S11, F4-S13, F4-S14). Fagron describes a centralized eQMS across sites, which is a centralized release-oversight function without a centralized lab (F4-S13).

## 4. What was tried and failed or was discontinued

**PharMEDium (AmerisourceBergen), 2013-2020.** A four-site 503B network (Memphis, Cleveland MS, Sugar Land, Dayton) with a corporate headquarters in Lake Forest, IL (F4-S7). FDA inspections from 2013 to 2018 produced a warning letter for insanitary conditions; a consent decree of permanent injunction was entered 2019-05-22 that recorded the Cleveland closure and gated resumption at Memphis on FDA authorization, with independent cGMP audits required at Dayton and Sugar Land (F4-S7, F4-S9). AmerisourceBergen closed the business in 2020, giving as reasons "increased regulatory and commercial challenges, coupled with the difficulty of making progress on remediation and continued financial burden" after paying $2.6 billion for it in 2015 (F4-S8). No larger closure of a multi-site sterile-compounding network was located in this pass; no source here ranks it against others. [inference] The failure mode was not the network topology; it was replicated aseptic-quality failure across sites under one corporate quality organization, which is exactly the "single quality unit over many spokes" risk this family relies on.

**CAPS (B. Braun) 503B exit, 2026.** CAPS, which the repository cites as an incumbent that quarantines all 503B product (Lrepo-S1 §1), now states on its home page that it "has made the decision to exit the 503B sterile compounding business" while keeping its 21-site 503A regional network (F4-S10). The stated reason is "a strategic refocus on its core operations and long-term sustainability" (F4-S10). FDA's registered outsourcing facilities list, current to 2026-09-02, shows a "Regulatory Meeting Held 5/5/2026" for both CAPS 503B sites (Lreg-S17). [inference] The temporal proximity is noted; causation is not established by any source here. Decision relevance: the repository's picture of a stable 503B incumbent set is out of date, and a regional 503A network with THR-style test-hold-release survived while the two-site cGMP 503B layer did not.

**BARDA CIADM program.** BARDA states that of the three 2012 centers, two are "Former CIADM partner" and "HHS is actively engaging with one remaining CIADM partner", and that lessons from CIADM and COVID-19 informed the newer BioMaP initiative (F4-S1). The FFMN itself is described in the present tense on the same page; whether task orders are still being placed is not confirmed (see section 9).

**Hub concentration events (not hub-and-spoke failures, but the risk the family creates).** Pfizer's Rocky Mount site made 25 percent of Pfizer's US hospital sterile-injectable product, about 8 percent of US consumption, when the site was damaged in a July 2023 storm; FDA's statement ends by saying redundancy of manufacturing locations is important (F4-S19). Baxter North Cove, "one of the largest manufacturers of intravenous and peritoneal dialysis solutions in the United States", was hit by Hurricane Helene in 2024, and FDA said further products could be added to the shortage list if total supply, "including importation of product from other Baxter facilities outside the U.S.", dipped below national demand (F4-S20). The bulletin gives no count of foreign facilities, and does not use the word "flooded". [inference] A hub-and-spoke network concentrates the formulation or QC step at a single site; both events show what a hub outage looks like. This is the mechanism the model must add as a common-cause group.

**Cell-therapy decentralization.** The literature that most directly analyzes hub-and-spoke QC for regulated sterile products concludes that decentralization brings "a reduction in oversight, development of new business and manufacturing models, decision-making and control by central management" (Lpeer-S1). No implemented hub-lab network for ATMPs was found in this pass; the field's published position is modeling, not operation.

## 5. Regulatory basis and constraints

Labels: law_or_regulation, final_guidance, draft_guidance, proposed_rule, expert_interpretation. UNCERTAIN marks readings a qualified reviewer must confirm.

**Bulk hold time and transport (sub-ideas 1, 4, 5).**
- 21 CFR 211.111 requires time limits for each phase of production when appropriate (Lreg-S1; law_or_regulation). FDA's CGMP Q&A gives holding a bulk liquid "for several months until it is filled" as an example where a holding time limit should be set (Lreg-S7; explanatory). FDA's 2004 aseptic guidance section VIII says time limits "should include, for example, the period between the start of bulk product compounding and its sterilization, filtration processes, product exposure while on the processing line", and section on processing from early stages says transport of sterile bulk tanks "should be simulated as part of the media fill" (Lreg-S8; final_guidance).
- EU Annex 1 (2022) §8.5 requires "a maximum permissible time between preparation and filling"; §8.18 requires validated maximum times for the interval from start of preparation through filtration to end of aseptic filling (iv) and "the holding time for sterilised product prior to filling" (v); §8.11(iii) says bulk solutions that cannot be filtered "should be sterilised"; §8.127-8.130 allow closed systems in a lower-classified area if integrity is shown at every use (Lreg-S13; law-adjacent EU GMP guideline).
- EMA's finished-dosage-form guideline states that hold-time validation for intermediates is normally a GMP matter not routinely presented in the MAA, "However, some specific types of products (e.g. sterile products, biological products) may require presentation of data"; that prolonged storage "means more than 30 days for solid oral dosage forms and more than 24 hours for sterile products"; and that transport of bulk between manufacturing sites is covered by Annex 15 (Lreg-S15; EU scientific guideline). Annex 15 itself was not fetched (section 9).
- Reading, UNCERTAIN: a formulated sterile-filtered bulk shipped from a hub to a fill spoke will need a validated hold time covering transport, a bulk container-closure justification, and media-fill simulation of the transport; an aqueous small-molecule injectable held more than 24 hours before filling is "prolonged storage" in EU terms and will need dedicated bulk-stability data. Whether FDA would accept a multi-day hub-to-spoke hold for a sterile-filtered bulk of a simple aqueous generic is an expert_interpretation question with no located precedent.

**Testing at a site other than manufacturing (sub-idea 2).**
- 21 CFR 211.22(a) and (b): the quality unit must have "adequate laboratory facilities" available to it, and it is responsible for products "held under contract by another company" (Lreg-S3). 21 CFR 211.165(a) requires laboratory determination "prior to release" (Lreg-S2). 21 CFR 207.1 defines "manufacturer" to include "control laboratories, contract laboratories" and "establishment" to include "independent laboratories that engage in control activities for a registered drug establishment" (Lreg-S5). A hub lab is therefore a registered establishment in its own right (law_or_regulation).
- FDA's quality agreements guidance: the owner's quality unit "is legally responsible for approving or rejecting drug products manufactured by the contract facility, including for final release", and quality agreements should state that services "provided by contract facilities (including laboratories) will comply with CGMP" (Lreg-S9; final_guidance). FDA's 503B CGMP draft says the outsourcing facility's quality control unit remains responsible for drugs tested by a contractor and recommends ISO 17025 accreditation for contract testing facilities (Lreg-S10; draft_guidance).
- Testing-site changes for an approved application are a lower reporting category than aseptic-process moves (Lreg-S11 section VI; final_guidance with binding effect on categories).
- Sterility test environment: FDA 2004 says the sterility testing laboratory "should employ facilities and controls comparable to those used for aseptic filling operations" (Lreg-S8). Reading, UNCERTAIN: nothing found requires the sterility lab to be at the fill site, but the sample-transit penalty (repo: 1-3 days ahead of the 14-day incubation, Lrepo-S1) applies, and Annex 16 §1.5.3 requires the QP to consider "conditions of storage and transport for the batch and the sample, if sent separately" (Lreg-S14).

**Release oversight and quarantine inventory (sub-idea 3).**
- 21 CFR 211.142(a) requires quarantine before quality-unit release (Lreg-S4). Annex 16 §4.1 allows uncertified batches to be shipped under quarantine to an approved site (Lreg-S14). Annex 16 §1.4.1-1.4.3 allow partial-manufacturing sites to confirm their steps by a local QP with responsibilities shared under a written agreement (Lreg-S14).
- FDA's proposed distributed manufacturing establishment rule (FR 2026-14073, 91 FR 42888) would let a hub plus units register as one establishment with a single quality unit and a unified pharmaceutical quality system, and excludes unaffiliated contract manufacturers and third-party relabelers/repackers (Lrepo-S2, Lrepo-S6; proposed_rule). Base case must not depend on it (protocol 4.3). Reading, UNCERTAIN: under current law each spoke is a separately registered establishment (Lreg-S6 allows a parent to file for all), and whether a spoke can operate with no resident quality-unit staff under a hub quality unit is an expert_interpretation question. The repository's dme-rule.md Q2 asks an adjacent but narrower question: whether a real-time-release decision made by a locked model at a DMU, under the hub's quality unit, satisfies the single-quality-unit requirement, and where the release decision legally occurs (Lrepo-S2). The staffing question is not posed there.
- State wholesale distributor licensure for forward distribution centers holding unreleased or released product: not researched here; UNCERTAIN (Lrepo-S5 flags state boards).

**Registration, listing, repackaging (sub-idea 4).**
- 21 CFR 207.17: every domestic establishment that manufactures, repacks, or relabels must register; a parent may register all establishments under common ownership (Lreg-S6). 21 CFR 207.1 excludes "internal or interplant transfer between registered establishments under common ownership and control" from "commercial distribution" (Lreg-S5). Reading, UNCERTAIN: hub-to-spoke transfer of bulk under one owner is not "commercial distribution" for listing purposes; the same transfer between unaffiliated firms is.
- FDA's 2017 repackaging guidance: repackaging is placing a finished drug into a different container "without further manipulation"; repackaged drugs "are not subject to sections 503A and 503B" and so get no compounding exemption; outsourcing facilities may repackage under enumerated conditions including BUD assignment per the 503B CGMP guidance (Lreg-S12; final_guidance, enforcement discretion). Reading: regional "final configuration" of an approved vial into syringes is repackaging with a BUD, not manufacturing under the ANDA, unless the spoke is a site in the application (Lreg-S11 VI.B).

**Chain of custody and serialization (all sub-ideas).**
- DSCSA "transaction" excludes "intracompany distribution of any product between members of an affiliate or within a manufacturer" (Lreg-S16 §581(24)(B)(i)); "product" means a prescription drug "in a finished dosage form" and excludes "a drug compounded in compliance with section 353a or 353b" (§581(13)). Reading, UNCERTAIN: bulk solution and in-process intermediates are not DSCSA "product"; intracompany hub-to-spoke transfers of finished product are not "transactions"; 503B compounded products are outside DSCSA product tracing; but a contracted (unaffiliated) spoke that takes ownership of finished product becomes a trading partner and the transfer is a transaction. The repository's note that DSCSA enforcement is "staggered through Nov 2026" (Lrepo-S5) was not re-verified here.

**Adding a spoke to an approved application.** A move of aseptic processing to a newly constructed or refurbished facility, or to a facility without similar approved products, is a prior approval supplement (Lreg-S11 VI.B.4; 21 CFR 314.70). Each new fill spoke for an ANDA product is therefore a PAS with inspection exposure; labeling, secondary packaging, and testing sites are lower categories. This matches the repository's gate G07 (Lrepo-S4) and is the lead-time term a hub-and-spoke strategy must carry per spoke.

## 6. Adjacent-industry analogues

**Blood donor testing (centralized QC lab, distributed collection).** Creative Testing Solutions owns and operates five high-volume donor testing laboratories, each "strategically located near a major metropolitan airport", all FDA registered, serving many regional blood collectors; a separate special testing laboratory in Phoenix handles confirmatory work (F4-S24). This is the most mature US example of a centralized regulated laboratory serving distributed production of a sterile product, and its siting rule (airports, plus a separate confirmatory laboratory) is a design constraint Telo would inherit. [inference] A hub lab on this pattern needs overnight sample logistics and a backup lab; F4-S24 states the five-lab count, the airport siting and the Phoenix special testing lab, not this design conclusion.

**Nuclear pharmacy (sterile hub, regional dose preparation).** Cardinal Health reports 130 nuclear pharmacies including 3 central pharmacies and more than 30 PET sites, dispensing 12 million patient-specific doses per year with real-time tracking (F4-S23). The model works because short half-lives make regional preparation mandatory and release-before-sterility is permitted for short-lived radiopharmaceuticals (21 CFR 211.165(a), Lreg-S2). [inference] The analogue does not transfer to long-shelf-life generics, where the regulatory door it uses is closed (Lrepo-S1 §1).

**Beverage concentrate (centralized bulk, regional final configuration).** The Coca-Cola Company runs "two lines of business: concentrate operations and finished product operations"; bottling partners combine concentrate with water and sweeteners, package in authorized containers, and sell in an identified territory under bottler's agreements that oblige the bottler to buy all concentrate from the Company (F4-S25). The commercial structure (territorial spokes, hub-controlled formula and specifications, bottler-owned capital) is the clearest non-pharma template for "central formulation IP, regional capital". Its transfer to injectables is blocked by sterility and hold-time rules (section 5), not by economics.

**Contract terminal sterilization (distributed sterile core as a service).** Sterigenics offers terminal sterilization of "API-filled vials or syringes, which can be powder-filled, solution or suspension, gel or ointment" and external EO or NO2 sterilization of prefilled syringes (F4-S21). For terminally sterilizable products, the sterile step itself can be outsourced to a regional contract sterilizer, which inverts this family: the sterile core becomes the spoke. The product-selection consequence belongs to Family 11.

## 8. Implications for the model

The simulation currently treats each node as complete (formulate, fill, test, release) with per-node fixed quality cost, per-node validation, and per-site failure rates (config/global.yaml). Hub-and-spoke changes seven mechanisms.

| mechanism | what changes | parameter to add or replace | source that lets the model set it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Spoke capacity becomes fill-line capacity only; hub formulation capacity is a shared upstream constraint; hub lab throughput becomes a queue ahead of release | new: `hub_formulation_capacity_units_per_day`, `hub_lab_batches_per_day`; existing `node_scale_fraction` applies to spokes only | F4-S1 (FFMN 117 million doses across 4-6 sites, an order-of-magnitude anchor), F4-S22 (PCI vial capacities), F4-S17 (Civica 90 million vials per year) |

Two further model consequences. First, `deviation_rate_per_batch` and `batch_rejection_rate` should be allowed to differ between hub and spoke steps, because PharMEDium's history shows aseptic failure replicating across spokes under one quality organization (F4-S7, F4-S8) and the cell-therapy literature predicts the same oversight loss (Lpeer-S1). Second, the strategy configuration must carry the regulatory owner per step (application holder at hub, spoke as site in application, hub lab as registered testing establishment), because the same physical network is a different legal object under an ANDA, under 503B, and under the proposed DME rule (Lrepo-S3, Lrepo-S4, Lrepo-S2).

Where the repository is wrong or stale, found in this pass:
- release-constraints.md §1 uses Quva's "on average 14-18 days" as the 503B release-time anchor, but §1a of the same file already records the reversal, citing ScanRDI as an FDA-accepted alternative to USP <71> returning results in as little as 1 business day. The rapid-method branch is therefore not a new correction; the file self-corrects.
