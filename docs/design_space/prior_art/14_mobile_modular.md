# Family 14 prior art: mobile or modular units through a regulatory and operations lens

**Prepared 2026-09-02 for the adversarial design-space review (ASSIGNMENT.md Phase B family 14, Phase C novelty review). PRELIMINARY; NOT LEGAL ADVICE. No statement here has been reviewed by a qualified sterile-manufacturing, CMC, or regulatory professional. Regulatory readings are labelled UNCERTAIN where a qualified reviewer would be needed. Inferences are marked [inference].**

Absence from Telo's repository is not novelty. Every physical sub-idea in this family (prefabricated cleanroom pods, trailer cleanrooms, container-based process modules, relocatable aseptic filling suites, mobile continuous small-molecule units) has a named vendor, a dated installation, and in several cases a published cost or timeline. The regulatory sub-ideas (single registration for a fleet of movable units, reduced requalification after a move) are the subject of an FDA proposed rule, an FDA discussion paper and action plan, a PDA Points to Consider, and an EFPIA reflection paper. What does not exist anywhere in the public record found here is an FDA-approved sterile injectable manufactured in a unit that has been relocated and requalified under a single registration. That is the open question, not the hardware.

---

## 1. Scope

This family covers designs in which the production node is a prefabricated, transportable, or self-contained unit (a cleanroom POD placed in a warehouse, a semi-trailer or ISO-container cleanroom, a skid-mounted process line in a shell building) rather than a stick-built plant, and in particular designs in which the unit is moved between sites during its life. In Telo's model the family touches `node_commissioning_days` (build, qualification, submission), `node_capital_scale_exponent` and `node_fixed_cost_scale_exponent` (capital and quality-unit cost per node), `os_integration_usd_per_site_year` (which already bundles cybersecurity), the common-cause parameters (a fleet of equivalent units shares a design and a vendor), and the release components, which mobility does not shorten. The review is organised around the fourteen operating questions in the family title: installation, utilities, cleanroom and environmental control, qualification after movement, site registration, inspection, personnel, contamination control, material flow, analytical testing, cybersecurity, physical security, commissioning time, revalidation. It ends with a verdict on whether movement destroys the response-time advantage.

## 3. What already exists (implemented, by whom, at what scale, since when)

### 3.1 Prefabricated cleanroom PODs placed inside a shell building (G-CON, since 2009 priority; installations since ~2015)

G-CON's patent family on the "modular, self-contained, mobile clean room" has a 2009 priority date and remains active [F14-S58]. Documented installations: Pfizer's PCMM oral-solid-dose line at Groton, CT (modules landed early 2015, assembled in days, then commissioning and validation with GMP operation targeted for end-2015 [F14-S29]; ISPE award page: "positioned and installed into a grey space warehouse within one week upon receipt" [F14-S28]; a coating POD added in 2019 "installed and connected to the existing PCMM PODs within a week" [F14-S27]); Just-Evotec Biologics, Redmond, WA (12 PODs placed in under a week, one year from kick-off, 2021 [F14-S22]); FUJIFILM Diosynth, College Station (2021, search result only, not fetched, not cited); NIIMBL's Project Blackhawk, a "transportable" GMP-like plasmid facility in a 50-year-old warehouse, funded under NIST award 70NANB21H085 [F14-S23]. Delivery lead time: "Standard PODs can be ready for delivery in three months, whereas if a client requires custom POD designs, it might take six months to a year" [F14-S24]. Cost: G-CON publishes only a generic range, "$150 and $1,000+ per square foot" [F14-S25]; a 2019 comparison table by G-CON's CEO gives modular-container CAPEX at "70 to 90 percent of traditional built" with "Time-to-run 18 to 24 months" and a caption "14 PODs built in 8 months, installed in 3 days" [F14-S26]. No per-unit price, installed-base count, or fill-finish deployment list was found in fetched material (see section 9). [inference] All PCMM evidence found in this review is oral solid dose, not sterile injectable; no source states that PCMM has never been used for a sterile injectable.

### 3.2 Trailer-based mobile cleanrooms (Germfree since 2015; Odulair since 2019)

Germfree states it has "deployed more than 100 Mobile Compounding Pharmacies globally since 2015" [F14-S32], sells or leases 450 sq ft semi-trailer units, and claims units can be "made operational in under a week" from its lease fleet [F14-S30]; its cGMP trailer platform advertises "Onsite in < 6 months" and Grade B/C layouts for aseptic filling and cell therapy [F14-S31]. Units are "factory acceptance tested before shipment and site acceptance tested onsite to support CQV efforts" [F14-S30]. Odulair reports a USP 797/800 trailer operational at a VA hospital since 2019-03-27 [F14-S52]. These are hospital compounding (503A, USP <797>/<800>) deployments, not cGMP drug manufacturing under an approved application.

### 3.3 The one operating shortage-response mobile unit in the US: ODP's Shortage Response Pharmacy on Demand at North Mississippi Medical Center (2023 to date)

Two Germfree ISO 7 trailers (636 sq ft cleanroom, unidirectional flow), completed September and November 2023 [F14-S33]. A six-month pilot compounded roughly 12,000 syringes of lidocaine, rocuronium and potassium chloride under 503A [F14-S34, F14-S35]; ODP says the partners agreed in 2024 to extend to 503B [F14-S35]. ASPR describes the PoD as "currently deployed to Northern Mississippi Medical Center to produce medicines that are in shortage in real-time" [F14-S19]. Government funding: $20 million DoD/HHS contract, 2020, for API and finished-dose capability, execution overseen by DARPA [F14-S18]. The DARPA Battlefield Medicine program that seeded the platform is marked "This program is now complete" [F14-S17]; the program's stated end-state was small-batch production that "obviates the need for individual drug stockpiling" [F14-S17]. Peer-reviewed framing from the program's own leadership is dated 2016 and conditional ("may one day") [F14-S57]. This is exactly the use case in Telo's family 14 and it is already running, under compounding law, at one hospital, with federal money.

### 3.4 Container-based process modules for biologics and vaccines (KUBio since 2016; BioNTainer since 2023; KeyPlants; Pharmadule)

KUBio (GE Healthcare, now Cytiva): JHL Biotech's Wuhan plant, 62 modules built in Germany, construction 18 months, opened May 2016 [F14-S40]; Pfizer's Hangzhou plant, chosen because it "can help slash factory construction time in half, to 18 months from three years" [F14-S38], GMP-operational 2018, sold to WuXi Biologics in 2021 [F14-S39]. BioNTainer: the solution is one drug substance module and one formulation module, each called a BioNTainer, and "Each module is built of six ISO sized containers (2.6m x 2.4m x 12m)" [F14-S70]; containers arrived in Kigali March 2023, the site was inaugurated December 2023, and process-validation batches were planned for 2025 [F14-S36]; EIB/EC blended financing of up to EUR 95 million added in October 2025 [F14-S21]; a trade report in October 2025 says "pilot production is expected to begin in 2026" [F14-S37]. KeyPlants: an aseptic filling facility for Ferring built in Sweden, commissioned, qualified and tech-transferred in Kiel, Germany, then shipped to Indonesia for production (project complete December 2019) [F14-S41]; the MADIBA fill-finish facility in Dakar delivered "in under a year" [F14-S42]. Pharmadule Morimatsu: 88 modules for EIPICO in Egypt, "installed in 21 days, followed by successful Site Acceptance Testing (SAT) and system commissioning verification" [F14-S43]. BioNTech and TCW have a pending US application on the container architecture, claiming a portable RNA-LNP production system "wherein each of the transcription module and the LNP formulation module is contained within a separate standard shipping container" [F14-S60]; it is an application, not a granted patent, and it claims the process architecture, not a relocation or requalification method. The Ferring case is the closest published precedent for "qualify here, operate there" in aseptic filling; it was a one-time relocation before launch, not repeated moves of a licensed unit.

### 3.5 Continuous small-molecule "mobile" injectable platforms (Bright Path, CONTINUUS)

Bright Path is one of four performers named in the EQUIP-A-Pharma program, a DARPA-managed program run with HHS ASPR [F14-S71, F14-S72]; the AZBio profile describes the company separately as a DARPA Embedded Entrepreneur Initiative company [F14-S47]. Bright Path reports end-to-end production of lidocaine HCl and carboplatin injectables between May 2025 and February 2026 "Operating an integrated mobile cleanroom paired with robotic filling systems" and the filing of two ANDAs [F14-S47, F14-S48]. Whether either ANDA names a mobile unit as the manufacturing site, and whether the claimed August 2026 AMT designation exists, could not be verified (section 9). CONTINUUS described a "MoP" (Mobile Pharmaceuticals) concept of lines "easily transported between locations" in 2021 [F14-S49].

### 3.6 Regulatory scaffolding that already exists

FDA's 2022 discussion paper identified mobile-unit registration as a gap [F14-S03]; the 2023 action plan recorded stakeholder proposals and an asserted blood-collection precedent that is not in fact codified [F14-S04, F14-S68]; the July 2026 proposed rule creates the DME/DMU/hub structure with an explicit mobile branch [F14-S01, F14-S02]. PDA published Points to Consider No. 15 on mobile manufacturing in November 2025, with G-CON, Pfizer, Genentech, Emergent, ODP and ex-FDA authors [F14-S54]. EFPIA's 2021 reflection paper framed "Autonomous & Portable" PODs for regulators [F14-S55]. ISPE's 2025 review by Pfizer and G-CON authors concludes adoption "remains limited, primarily due to persistent regulatory and economic barriers" [F14-S53]. DEA's 2021 rule shows a US regulator already operating a "mobile component under the parent registration" model for controlled-substance dispensing [F14-S12].

## 4. What was tried and failed or discontinued, and the documented reasons

**Orgenesis OMPULs (mobile cGMP cell-processing units), 2019 to 2024.** Orgenesis sold five OMPULs plus its quality-system framework to Germfree for $8.34 million in April 2024 under a sale-and-leaseback structure with a ten-year exclusive manufacturing licence [F14-S44]. The FY2024 10-K states: "On November 5, 2024, Germfree notified us of its intention not to lease any OMPULS back to us." It also reports cash of "approximately $0.1 million" at 2024-12-31, going-concern doubt, subsidiary liquidations in Korea, Israel, Australia and Belgium, and a court-appointed trustee for its Israeli processing subsidiary after unpaid salaries [F14-S45]. The documented reason is customer and corporate funding failure, not a technical failure of the units; the units themselves were absorbed into a vendor's lease fleet. [inference] The lesson for Telo is that a mobile-unit fleet whose customers are pre-revenue developers has no durable demand; the hardware survived, the operator did not.

**Pfizer Hangzhou KUBio, 2016 to 2021.** Built as a modular biosimilars plant, GMP-operational 2018, sold to WuXi in 2021 after Pfizer "made a difficult decision to halt its biosimilars programs in China" [F14-S39]. The modular asset was resalable, which the vendor counts as a benefit [F14-S24]; the documented cause of exit was product-market strategy, not the facility.

**CONTINUUS Woburn GMP facility, 2021 to 2025.** The 2021 contract announcement promised a plant "fully operational within two years" [F14-S49]; a 2022 trade report gave mid-2022 completion for a $125 million, 50,000 sq ft site producing sterile injectables and tablets [F14-S50]. In November 2025 the company's board discussion still framed the immediate goal as launching a GMP facility for clinical materials, and stated: "We’ve tried applying this model to generics, and it just doesn’t work at their price points." The CEO added that a continuous facility "would cost about $50 million, which just doesn’t work for a product that sells for a few dollars per kilogram" [F14-S51]. This is the most direct published statement that a compact continuous platform, mobile or not, fails on generic-injectable economics.

**Univercells (Belgium), 2016 to 2025.** The equipment subsidiary was sold to Donaldson in June 2023 for EUR 136 million on roughly EUR 10 million revenue [F14-S46]; the parent announced a restructuring of 73 of 400 jobs at Gosselies in January 2025, citing "the current economic situation as well as an overcapacity within the sector" [F14-S20]. The original small-footprint vaccine microfacility vision was not fetched (section 9); what is documented is that the surviving business is an equipment supplier, not a distributed-plant operator.

**"Prevalidated, modular GMP-compliant facility" patent, 2005.** WO2007067656A2 claimed container cleanrooms built "in proximity to the approval authorities" then shipped to the customer; its status is Ceased [F14-S59]. The idea of pre-approving a unit and moving it without further review is two decades old and did not survive as intellectual property.

**BioNTainer schedule.** Not a failure, but a slip: containers arrived March 2023, validation batches were planned for 2025 [F14-S36], and pilot production is now "expected to begin in 2026" [F14-S37]. Three or more years from module arrival to first product, in a project with roughly USD 150 million of sponsor investment and CEPI plus EIB/EC support [F14-S21]. Modular construction compressed the build; it did not compress qualification, training, and licensing.

**DARPA Battlefield Medicine.** Program complete [F14-S17]. Its commercial descendant operates one pilot under compounding law [F14-S35]. No FDA-approved product manufactured on a Battlefield Medicine platform was found.

## 5. Regulatory basis and constraints

### 5.1 Site registration of movable units

- `law_or_regulation` 21 CFR 207.1: "Establishment means a place of business under one management at one general physical location." [F14-S07]. 21 CFR 207.29(a): expedited update within 30 calendar days of "Changing an establishment's name or physical address" [F14-S08]. FDA's own reading in 2022: current rules "do not contemplate the mobility of manufacturing units" [F14-S03].
- `proposed_rule` FR 2026-14073 (Docket FDA-2025-N-6075, comments close 2026-09-11, confirmed from the API record [F14-S01] and the frozen text [F14-S02, F14-S65]). Key provisions read from the primary text [F14-S02]: (a) a DME must include "at least one DMU capable of mobility (i.e., capable of manufacturing in multiple locations) or at least two DMUs if none of the DMUs are capable of mobility"; (b) "Proposed § 207.21(b)(1) would require a DME to register no later than 5 calendar days after the first domestic DMU begins to manufacture a drug for commercial distribution"; (c) relocation notice: "When a DMU moves within the United States or to a U.S. location from a foreign country, the registrant must provide FDA notification at least 30 calendar days prior to the relocation (see proposed § 207.29(b)(4)(i)(1)), whereas a move to any foreign country or to a different location within the same foreign would require at least 120 calendar days advance notice (see proposed § 207.29(b)(4)(i)(2))"; (d) "The proposed 30 and 120 calendar day timelines reflect the amount of time needed to prepare for a domestic versus foreign inspection, if needed"; (e) equivalence: "The term “equivalent” is not intended to mean identical, which is a stricter standard"; (f) a DME requires an approved NDA, ANDA, NADA or BLA and a preapproval inspection covering an application that "describes the use of a decentralized manufacturing strategy"; (g) single legal entity: "each contract manufacturing organization would need to register and list separately under the existing regulations". The text contains no occurrence of "503B", "outsourcing facility", "cybersecurity" or "security" (string search of the frozen XML). Corrections to the repository memo [F14-S62]: the rule says "capable of mobility", not "movable"; the advance-notice periods (30 and 120 days) were unquantified in the memo; the repo's Q4 (what requalification FDA expects after a move) is partly answered by the preamble, which says pooled DMU data "could reduce the extent of validation activities that would be needed when adding subsequent DMUs to the DME or following relocation of an existing DMU" and that this "would be especially important for DMUs capable of mobility that are commissioned for their ability to move to new locations quickly to manufacture drugs in response to need (e.g., during an emergency)" [F14-S02]. UNCERTAIN: the rule is a registration rule; it does not itself set the validation evidence standard, and it is not final. The model must not depend on it (protocol 4.3, [F14-S64]).
- Precedents for "mobile component under a parent registration": DEA's 2021 rule for mobile narcotic treatment programs [F14-S12, F14-S69]. The blood-collection precedent invoked by FDA's stakeholders [F14-S04] does not survive checking. 21 CFR Part 607 was fetched in full and contains no occurrence of the word "mobile"; § 607.3(c) uses the same formula as § 207.1, "Establishment means a place of business under one management at one general physical location", and § 607.25 requires "the name and street address of the establishment" [F14-S68]. Whatever FDA does administratively for mobile blood drives, it is not a codified registration exception, so it is not a regulatory precedent a sponsor can cite. UNCERTAIN: whether an FDA guidance or BER system practice creates the exception outside Part 607; no such document was found.

### 5.2 Inspection

- FDA 2022: "Further, FDA may have to consider the need to evaluate and/or inspect units after they move to new locations." [F14-S03]. The proposed rule ties the 30/120-day notice to inspection preparation [F14-S02]. The DME requires a preapproval inspection reviewing the unified quality system [F14-S02]. [inference] Every relocation is therefore a potential inspection trigger, and the notice period, not the transport time, is the floor on relocation latency for a licensed unit.

### 5.3 Cleanroom, environmental control, contamination control, material flow

- `law_or_regulation` 21 CFR 211.42(a) requires buildings "of suitable size, construction and location" and 211.42(c)(10) lists aseptic-processing design features [F14-S09]. 21 CFR 211.113(b) as cited in the repository maps [F14-S64].
- EU Annex 1 (2022) §2.3: a Contamination Control Strategy "should be implemented across the facility in order to define all critical control points"; §4.1: sterile manufacture in cleanrooms entered through airlocks; §4.24: "Cleanrooms and clean air equipment should be qualified using methodology in accordance with the requirements of Annex 15"; §9.4 environmental monitoring programme [F14-S14]. Trailer and POD vendors advertise the same design features. Germfree's cGMP trailer page lists "Process driven HVAC design", "Seamless Interior Walls & Coved Flooring", "Low wall returns create a particle reducing air turnover" and "Integrated active pass-through for material handling" [F14-S31, re-verified on the live page 2026-09-03]; the ODP case study describes unidirectional flow [F14-S33]. Nothing in Annex 1 or Part 211 is mobile-specific; a trailer is a cleanroom with a smaller envelope and a self-contained air handler. UNCERTAIN: whether a trailer's HVAC, reconnected at a new site with different make-up air, ambient conditions and utility quality, is a "significant modification to ... facilities, services or equipment" under Annex 1 §9.38 is a case-by-case QA judgement.

### 5.4 Qualification after movement and revalidation

- Annex 15 §4.1 and §4.2: requalification "at an appropriate frequency" with a justified period; the IQ definition covers facilities "as installed or modified"; §5.5 allows a bracketing approach to reduce validation batches for site transfers [F14-S15]. ISPE Baseline Guide Vol. 5 (2nd ed.) is the industry C&Q method [F14-S61]; its content is paywalled and was not read.
- Annex 1 §9.38: APS "as part of the initial validation, with at least three consecutive satisfactory simulation tests ... and after any significant modification to operational practices, facilities, services or equipment which are assessed to have an impact on the sterility assurance of the product (e.g. modification to the HVAC system, equipment, changes to process, number of shifts and numbers of personnel, major facility shut down)"; and the sentence that names the case directly: "Consideration should be given to performing an APS after the last batch prior to shut down, before long periods of inactivity or before decommissioning or relocation of a line." [F14-S14]. FDA 2004: three consecutive successful runs for initial line qualification, semi-annual thereafter, and "Incubation time should not be less than 14 days." [F14-S13].
- PDA Points to Consider No. 15 (Nov 2025) defines the target as a process that "once validated, can be relocated and operated in a different location for the same process, without the requirement for regulatory preapproval or revalidation" [F14-S54]. That is an industry aspiration document, not an FDA position. UNCERTAIN whether FDA would accept a move without new APS; Annex 1 treats relocation as an APS trigger by name.
- [inference] Arithmetic for a licensed aseptic unit after a move, assuming FDA treats the move as a facility change requiring line requalification: HVAC re-verification and cleanroom re-classification (days), three APS runs with 14-day incubations (overlapping runs still leave roughly 3 to 5 weeks to last read-out), environmental-monitoring requalification, personnel gowning requalification if operators change (Annex 1 §7.4, §7.5 [F14-S14]), then the first commercial batch's own release path of 14 to 18 days from the repository [F14-S63]. Earliest released product after a move is on the order of 6 to 10 weeks, before any regulatory notice or supplement, and the 30-day advance notice runs in parallel only if the move is planned. If FDA accepts pooled-fleet data and a reduced package, as the proposed rule's preamble contemplates, the floor is the release path plus re-verification, roughly 3 to 4 weeks. Both numbers are inferences for a qualified reviewer to replace.

### 5.5 Personnel

- Annex 1 §7.1 requires "sufficient appropriate personnel, suitably qualified, trained and experienced"; §7.4 gowning qualification at least annually; §7.5 "Unqualified personnel should not enter grade B cleanrooms or grade A in operation." [F14-S14]. FDA's action plan records that "new personnel may need to be trained and qualified to operate DM units at each new location" [F14-S04]. [inference] A mobile unit therefore either travels with its qualified crew (which caps fleet size at crew count and removes the "local staffing" economy) or requalifies host-site staff at each stop (which adds APS participation and gowning qualification to the relocation clock).

### 5.6 Analytical testing and release

- Method validation and transfer obligations (21 CFR 211.165, 211.194 as cited in [F14-S64]). FDA 2022 flagged that each new location may require "analytical comparability, method transfer and validation, and stability data" if a move is treated as a facility change [F14-S03, F14-S04]. The repository's release-time decomposition applies unchanged: a "~7-day floor from environmental monitoring" [F14-S63]. In-line spectroscopy on a mobile continuous unit is published (Miyai et al. 2021, cited in-repo [F14-S66]); the repository's own conformal work notes that relocation is an instrument-and-environment change requiring recalibration to restore coverage [F14-S62]. Nothing found suggests mobility shortens release; it adds a recalibration step.

### 5.7 Cybersecurity

- 21 CFR 211.68(b) on controls over computer systems and authorised changes to records [F14-S10]; 21 CFR Part 11 (not fetched). The EU Annex 11 revision (consultation closed 2025-10-07) "strengthens controls related to ... system security" [F14-S16]; the draft's section 15 covers security management, patching, penetration testing and encrypted remote access (seen in search excerpts of the draft, not separately fetched; treat as UNCERTAIN detail). Neither the FDA discussion paper nor the proposed rule mentions cybersecurity. [inference] A hub-and-spoke DME with mobile units and remote BMS access (Germfree's BMS offers "remote access (requires client IT interface)", seen in the tech sheet excerpt, not fetched) is an OT-security surface that current cGMP addresses only through 211.68 and Part 11 generalities. Telo's `os_integration_usd_per_site_year` already names cybersecurity as a cost component.

### 5.8 Physical security

- No FDA cGMP provision specific to physical security of a non-controlled drug manufacturing unit was found beyond 211.42's design-and-flow requirements [F14-S09]. For controlled substances, DEA's 21 CFR 1301.72 requires Schedule I/II storage in a safe or cabinet that, "if it weighs less than 750 pounds, is bolted or cemented to the floor or wall in such a way that it cannot be readily removed" [F14-S11], and the mobile-NTP rule applies "the security requirements of 21 CFR 1301.72(e) and 1301.74(j)-(n) ... to the mobile components of NTPs" and requires the mobile component to return to the registered location each day, waivable only on a showing of "exceptional circumstances" (full rule text fetched [F14-S69]; abstract [F14-S12]). None of Telo's current candidates (sodium bicarbonate, furosemide, acyclovir, sterile water, acetazolamide, norepinephrine) is controlled, so 1301.72 bites only on portfolio expansion into ketamine, opioids or benzodiazepines. UNCERTAIN: state board of pharmacy and state manufacturing licences for a trailer parked on hospital property vary by state (Germfree notes state licensure varies; seen in page excerpt, not fetched).

### 5.9 Advanced Manufacturing Technologies designation (FDORA 3213, FD&C 506L)

- `final_guidance` December 2024 [F14-S05]. Designation gives priority interaction; it is not approval, and near-graduated technologies are "likely not eligible". As of 2025-10-16 FDA had received 18 requests and granted 3, and "CDER has received 5 designation requests from 5 separate entities, and granted 0 designations" [F14-S06]. All three grants were CBER (automated cell-therapy manufacturing). Bright Path's August 2026 designation claim [F14-S47] would be the first CDER grant if true; FDA does not publish a designee list in the fetched material, so it is unverified (section 9).

### 5.10 The 503B branch

The proposed rule never mentions outsourcing facilities [F14-S02]. The only running US mobile shortage-response unit operates under 503A with a stated plan for 503B [F14-S35]. The repository's 503B map already records that 503B "is never a bridge to an ANDA and never a permanent supply source" and that it "does not say 503B production is lawful or unlawful for any product" [F14-S67]. [inference] A mobile 503B trailer is legally the ODP/Germfree model, and it is bounded by the bulks-list and shortage-list predicates the repository already encodes as a time-varying state.

## 6. Adjacent-industry analogues

**Mobile narcotic treatment programs (DEA, 2021).** A federal regulator already registers mobile components as a coincident activity of a fixed registrant rather than as separate establishments, on condition of pre-approval by the local field office, in-state operation and physical-security controls [F14-S12, F14-S11]. It is the closest legal analogue to the proposed DME "mobile DMU" branch. [inference] It suggests the shape FDA may adopt (notice, approval of procedures, geographic limits) and the cost of that shape (a daily-return rule and a state boundary); no FDA source states that FDA will follow the DEA model.

**Mobile blood collection (refuted as a legal precedent).** FDA stakeholders pointed to the registration of mobile blood-collection establishments under a parent establishment as a working model [F14-S04]. Part 607 was fetched in full for this review and has no mobile provision; its establishment definition is the fixed-location formula and registration requires a street address [F14-S68]. The analogue is therefore an administrative practice at best, not a rule FDA has already written. This matters because it leaves the DEA mobile-NTP rule as the only codified US precedent for a movable unit under a parent registration, and that rule governs dispensing of controlled substances, not manufacturing.

**Hospital mobile compounding pharmacies (USP <797>/<800>).** More than 100 Germfree trailers since 2015 [F14-S32], plus Odulair's VA units since 2019 [F14-S52], are the largest such fleet of relocatable sterile-processing rooms found in this review; no source comparing fleet sizes was found, so the superlative is bounded by what was fetched. The vendor markets the trailers as letting hospitals keep compounding online, "serving as a swing space during facility renovations, addressing surge capacity", and states that its "mobile facilities are factory acceptance tested before shipment and site acceptance tested onsite to support CQV efforts towards commissioning of the facility" [F14-S30]. That is evidence about first installation, not about a subsequent move. [unverified] No CETA certification-guide, state board of pharmacy licensure, or per-move recertification cost or duration source was found (section 9), so this dossier does not claim that requalifying a moved Grade C/ISO 7 aseptic space is routine at the compounding tier. The fleet also does not show cGMP batch release under an approved application.

**Field pharmaceutical manufacturing for defence (DARPA/DoD/ASPR).** Battlefield Medicine (complete) [F14-S17], the DoD/HHS contract to ODP [F14-S18] and ASPR's 2025 framing of a deployable PoD network for disasters [F14-S19] define the customer for mobility as a government preparedness buyer, not a hospital paying generic prices. [inference] This is consistent with Telo's family 13 (public-private infrastructure) and inconsistent with a commercial hospital-customer model for mobile units.

**Mobile biocontainment laboratories.** Germfree cites facilities "for shipment by military aircraft" and BSL-2/BSL-3 trailers [F14-S31]; the same vendor base builds the pharmacy trailers. This is the engineering lineage of self-contained HVAC and pressure cascades on a chassis.

**PET radiopharmacy network.** Already in the repository as the structural analogue of a distributed sterile network with release-before-sterility under 21 CFR 212.70(e) [F14-S63]. Those sites are fixed cyclotron facilities, not mobile.

## 8. Implications for the model

Parameter names below are the ids in `feasibility/config/global.yaml` and the strategy design variables in `feasibility/config/strategies/illustrative_baseline.yaml` (`sites`, `capacity_factor`, `safety_stock_days`, `release_time_factor`, `delivery_days`, `common_impact_factor`).

**Commissioning delay (`node_commissioning_days`, illustrative 365/730/1095).** The sources split this into three components that modular construction affects unequally. Build and install: 3 to 12 months for POD delivery [F14-S24], days for placement [F14-S22, S26, S27, S28, S43], under six months to site for a Germfree cGMP trailer [F14-S31], 18 months for a KUBio plant [F14-S38, S40], under a year for a modular fill-finish facility in Dakar [F14-S42]. Qualification: three APS with 14-day incubations plus cleanroom qualification [F14-S13, S14]; the Ferring facility took mid-2018 to December 2019 including tech transfer and validation [F14-S41]. Submission and licensing: BioNTainer has run from March 2023 to a 2026 pilot without product [F14-S36, S37]. Recommendation: replace the single parameter with `node_build_days` (modular low bound 90 to 180), `node_qualification_days` (low bound 60 to 120 for aseptic), and `node_submission_days` (unchanged; not addressed by modularity), and keep the base near 730 because the third term dominates. A vendor quote is the tier-2 evidence for the first term; FDA review-time data for the third.

**Relocation (new parameters).** Add `relocation_transport_days` (days; trailers), `relocation_notice_days` (30 domestic, 120 foreign, from the proposed rule [F14-S02]; base-case must not assume the rule is final), and `relocation_requalification_days` (inference 20 to 70; section 5.4). Add `relocation_events_per_year` as a strategy design variable, default 0. A strategy that relocates units should be simulated with the unit unavailable for transport plus requalification, and with a probability of an inspection hold.

**Common-cause groups (`common_cause_events_per_year`, `common_cause_duration_days`, `common_cause_capacity_impact`, `common_impact_factor`).** A fleet of equivalent units under one quality system is exactly what the DME rule requires [F14-S02] and exactly what creates a new common-cause group: one design defect, one vendor recall, one hub IT outage, one adverse inspection of the unified quality system takes down every spoke. The assignment forbids assuming nominally separate sites are independent. Recommendation: add a `fleet_design_common_cause` group with its own rate and impact, and elicit the impact from the Orgenesis case (a single quality-system framework sold with the units [F14-S44]) and from the vendor concentration (Germfree now holds both its own and the former Orgenesis units).

**Release components (`sterility_incubation_days`, `environmental_monitoring_days`, `assay_days`, `endotoxin_days`, `qa_review_days`, `release_time_factor`).** Nothing in this family changes them. The 7-day EM floor [F14-S63] and the 14-day media-fill incubation [F14-S13] are the same in a trailer as in a plant. `release_time_factor` should stay at 1.0 for mobile designs; a move adds an instrument recalibration step to any in-line release component [F14-S62].

**Inventory policy (`safety_stock_days`, `backorder_window_days`, `emergency_transport_days`).** Mobility competes with shipping inventory, not with building a plant. A move-plus-requalify cycle of weeks (section 5.4) is longer than `emergency_transport_days` by construction. The repository's frozen shortage data show furosemide listed since 2020 and sodium bicarbonate since 2017 [F14-S67 context]; for shortages that persist for years, the difference between a fixed modular node and a movable one is irrelevant, because a fixed node commissioned in 12 to 24 months still arrives inside the shortage. Mobility pays only for short, local disruptions (the ASPR hurricane and wildfire framing [F14-S19]) and only if requalification is waived.

**Contract terms (`reserved_capacity_fee_fraction`, `reserved_capacity_activation_days`).** Vendors offer lease fleets [F14-S30, S44]; a leased trailer held in reserve is a warm-standby asset (family 7) with an activation time equal to transport plus requalification plus staffing. The Orgenesis leaseback collapsed when the lessee lost funding [F14-S45]; model lessee default explicitly.

**Cost structure (`node_capital_scale_exponent`, `node_fixed_cost_scale_exponent`, `os_integration_usd_per_site_year`).** Published cost evidence is thin: $150 to $1,000+ per sq ft for cleanrooms generally [F14-S25]; modular container CAPEX at 70 to 90 percent of stick-built with 18 to 24 months time-to-run [F14-S26]; KUBio "costs 25 percent to 50 percent less than building a traditional factory" per Cytiva's own page [F14-S38] (vendor marketing, no basis published; treat the number as a claim, not evidence); a continuous small-molecule facility at about $50 million, which its own board says fails generic economics [F14-S51]. The single-quality-unit structure of a DME supports a fixed-cost exponent below 1.0 for the hub, but per-location personnel qualification [F14-S04] pushes per-site labour back up. Cybersecurity is already inside `os_integration_usd_per_site_year`; the Annex 11 revision [F14-S16] argues for raising its base rather than adding a parameter.

**Regulatory gates.** G13 (unit/site registration supportable, proposed_rule) stays UNCERTAIN; the rule is not final and excludes CMOs and is silent on 503B. G14 (cross-site comparability) is unchanged: the rule demands equivalence and says nothing about how to prove it; the repository's per-site conformal validity remains a candidate instrument [F14-S64]. Add a gate G16 "relocation requalification package accepted" with treatment `lead_time`, default status UNCERTAIN, resolved by an FDA meeting minute or a published precedent.

**Verdict on mobility.** Physical movement does not destroy the response-time advantage; transport is days and installation is days for every vendor found. What removes the advantage is the qualification and release clock that restarts after the move: Annex 1 names relocation as an APS consideration [F14-S14], FDA expects three consecutive media fills with 14-day incubation for a line qualification [F14-S13], the proposed rule requires 30 days' notice and treats a move as an inspection trigger [F14-S02], and the release floor is unchanged [F14-S63]. The earliest released sterile product after a move is weeks, not days, even under the most favourable reading, and no example of it happening under an approved application was found. Against year-long shortages that latency is immaterial; against short local disruptions it is slower than shipped inventory. Recommendation: reject mobility as Telo's mechanism. Keep modular, relocatable-in-principle construction as the build method for fixed nodes, because it shortens `node_build_days`, preserves resale value [F14-S24, S39], and satisfies the DME "capable of mobility" branch as a registration convenience without ever exercising it. Model relocation as a rare, costly event with its own common-cause exposure, not as a strategy.
