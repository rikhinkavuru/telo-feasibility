## 1. Scope

Family 2 asks whether Telo's strongest first product is software that a sterile-injectable manufacturer, a 503B outsourcing facility, a CDMO, a GPO, or a hospital would pay for, with Telo owning no plant and holding no application. The sub-ideas in the family title are: (a) a documentation and electronic batch record (EBR) wedge; (b) deviation and CAPA orchestration; (c) process scheduling and campaign optimization; (d) quality-system workflow automation; (e) sensor interoperability and data lineage; (f) drift detection and mandatory-hold decision support; (g) cross-site process intelligence without owning manufacturing; (h) capacity and shortage-response coordination software. In this project's terms the question is which of these, if any, changes a shortage-days outcome in the feasibility model, and whether any is unoccupied. The repository already concluded that the horizontal manufacturing OS is entrenched (F2-S80 section 5.3) and that the release layer compresses tests that are not the binding constraint for aseptic injectables (F2-S78 section 1). This dossier tests the eight sub-ideas against vendors, regulators, patents and documented failures.

## 3. What already exists

### 3.1 Electronic batch records and MES (sub-idea a)

The category is mature and consolidated. Körber's PAS-X states it is used by more than 50 percent of the top 30 pharma companies (F2-S01); the same page announces a "PAS-X Neo" product "purpose-built ... for small and mid-sized life science manufacturers and sites" as "coming soon" (F2-S01), which is the segment a microplant or 503B site would sit in. Körber acquired Werum, which it describes as "the leading provider" of pharma MES (F2-S02); a Werum release dated October 2019 already places Werum inside Körber (F2-S03, snippet). Siemens (Opcenter Execution Pharma) markets review by exception for faster release (F2-S05) and reports Gartner Leader placement six years running through 2023 (F2-S06). Emerson sells DeltaV MES EBR with a Quality Review Manager add-on for process exceptions (F2-S04) and holds a granted US patent on configurable exception rules for review by exception, priority 2018 (F2-S74). Rockwell sells FactoryTalk PharmaSuite (F2-S07; Gartner Market Guide representative vendor 2025 per F2-S08, snippet). MasterControl dates FDA acceptance of EBR systems to Part 11 in 1997 (F2-S12). Tulip sells no-code EBR apps (F2-S11). Apprentice reports $207 million raised, customers including Moderna, Catalent and Gilead, and a January 2026 acquisition of Ganymede.bio (F2-S10); its MES page claims "Go live in 90 days" and "One-Click Tech Transfer" across sites (F2-S09). Leucine (India/US) claims 400+ GMP facilities, agentic review-by-exception, and a 30-site single-instance deployment at Cipla (F2-S13, F2-S14); these are vendor claims. L7 Informatics and IDBS sell adjacent platforms for development, tech transfer and manufacturing data (F2-S26, F2-S27). Zaether is a consultancy that implements other vendors' MES, not a product vendor (F2-S28, snippet).

Documented outcomes rather than claims: an ISPE case study from 2010 describes an MES with review by exception at a plant making about 10,000 batches a year, validated under GAMP 5 with HACCP risk assessment (F2-S70). MIT Sloan's 2026 Pfizer case reports that after reframing the program, yearly EBR deployments rose by more than 50 percent, go-live schedule accuracy rose from 40 percent to more than 80 percent, and per-site EBR implementation cost fell by 80 percent (F2-S71). A 2026 EACL industry paper from Biocon reports an LLM pipeline converting legacy paper batch records to structured JSON with composite confidence 82 to 89 percent on three records, and states plainly that many facilities still run on paper (F2-S72). A 2026 mixed-methods study posted on ResearchGate reports a 41 percent review cycle-time reduction from AI-assisted batch record review on 1,240 records [unverified]. The posting returned HTTP 403 ("ResearchGate - Temporarily Unavailable") on re-check on 2026-09-02, so neither the figure nor the sample can be checked; its journal attribution was inconsistent on the page when first seen and its peer-review status is unresolved (F2-S73, snippet). No parameter in section 8 rests on it.

### 3.2 Deviation and CAPA orchestration; QMS workflow automation (sub-ideas b, d)

Veeva QMS reports more than 300 customer companies and external-partner collaboration on investigations and CAPA (F2-S15). Honeywell paid $1.3 billion for Sparta Systems (TrackWise) in 2020; Sparta then served more than 400 customers including 42 of the top 50 pharma companies, and Honeywell said it would add AI to "triage quality events in near real time" (F2-S16). MasterControl covers QMS and EBR (F2-S12). Validation lifecycle software is its own sub-category (Kneat, F2-S23; ValGenesis iVal with AI authoring, F2-S24), as is structured CMC knowledge (QbDVision, F2-S25).

AI-assisted deviation investigation is a crowded early market, not an open one. Vendors found on 2026-09-02: Qwyn AI (founders from Lilly, Elanco, Thermo Fisher, Salesforce; "Qwyn Investigate" marked GA, other modules "coming soon") (F2-S29); Mareana Lumis (GraphRAG over batch genealogy) (F2-S30, snippet); Visium Devin (F2-S31, snippet); plus Verixa, DeviationIQ, Qualitum, Sanai and CAPA Engine, which were seen only as search excerpts, carry no source row, are marked [unverified] here, and are listed under section 9. The incumbents embed the same function: Apprentice's MES page lists agentic AI and "Built-in QMS integration" for deviation and CAPA management (F2-S09), Leucine's "Compliance Agent" "pre-populates investigation reports" (F2-S13), L7 SYNAPSE is described as "suggesting deviation responses" (F2-S26), and Veeva shows "Vault AI" custom agents for complaint intake (F2-S15).

### 3.3 Process scheduling and campaign optimization (sub-idea c)

Scheduling sits inside MES products. Werum integrated a sedApta-based detailed scheduler into PAS-X in 2019 (F2-S84, snippet). Leucine markets cross-site scheduling and capacity balancing that "considers site capabilities, regulatory status, and product-site qualifications" (F2-S14). Apprentice lists scheduling among MES features (F2-S09). No vendor was found that optimizes campaigns across independently owned sites against an external shortage signal; see section 7.

### 3.4 Sensor interoperability and data lineage (sub-idea e)

Process historians and asset frameworks are the standard layer. The AVEVA PI case at a Boehringer Ingelheim animal-health site aligned SCADA, LIMS, process control and spectroscopic data into event frames so SIMCA models could run; the data scientist quoted says 80 to 95 percent of the work is managing data (F2-S21). Scitara DLX sells lab instrument connectivity as an iPaaS with an audit-trailed event stream (F2-S22). Aizon Unify is described in the Curia case on Aizon's home page as "a scalable, comprehensive lakehouse and data analysis solution" (F2-S17); "historian" does not appear on that page, and calling Unify a production historian was the author's characterization of its functional role [inference]. Leucine lists OPC-UA, Modbus, MQTT and PI connectors at the edge (F2-S13). L7 standardizes ontology at data capture (F2-S26). The ISPE Pharma 4.0 program names data integrity by design and digital maturity as enablers (F2-S69, snippet).

### 3.5 Drift detection and mandatory-hold decision support (sub-idea f)

Multivariate real-time monitoring with fault detection and deviation alerts is a shipped product: Sartorius SIMCA-online with green/yellow/red batch trajectories, fault detection, root-cause contribution and "automatic corrective recommendation" (F2-S20). Aizon's home page lists continued process verification, rapid deviation resolution and root-cause analytics as use cases (F2-S17), and its data sheet lists multivariate anomaly and out-of-trend detection (F2-S85, snippet). Basetwo sells soft sensors that "predict deviations" and claims an 80 percent deviation reduction (F2-S19; vendor claim). Quartic markets real-time batch-outcome prediction (F2-S18). None of these is a release decision. A hold decision that affects product disposition is a "critical application" in the draft Annex 22 sense (F2-S57); see section 5.

### 3.6 Cross-site process intelligence without owning manufacturing (sub-idea g)

Within one owner's network this exists commercially: Leucine's single multi-tenant instance across 30 Cipla sites with cross-site deviation correlation and CAPA propagation (F2-S14, vendor claim); Apprentice's Connected Manufacturing Network and sponsor access for external teams (F2-S09); Aizon's "industrialize insights cross-site" onboarding (F2-S17); Veeva QMS partner access (F2-S15). Across independent owners (sponsor plus several CDMOs, or several unrelated manufacturers) the products are pre-release: Axio BioPharma's Lattice is "in active development" as a federated system where "data stays local" and "insight travels" (F2-S32); L7 EXCHANGE, for sponsor-CRO-CDMO handoffs, carries an "Estimated release date: 2027" (F2-S26). Pfizer describes its own network-wide data infrastructure as the payoff of the MES foundation, inside Pfizer (F2-S71).

### 3.7 Capacity and shortage-response coordination software (sub-idea h)

Hospital side, paid and at scale: Vizient sells shortage analytics, the Novaplus Enhanced Supply committed-inventory program with supplier warehousing in the US, and a Pharmacy Reserve; it founded the End Drug Shortages Alliance (F2-S33, F2-S34). Premier's ProvideGx and PremierProRx use long-term committed buying contracts with manufacturers, cover more than 150 shortage drugs, and claim 15 FDA delistings (F2-S35); Premier's own survey reports manufacturers rank unpredictable demand among their top supply challenges (F2-S36). Bluesight ShortageCheck forecasts shortages from data across more than 3,250 healthcare organizations and consolidates RFID and cabinet inventory (F2-S37). LogicStream's shortage app was acquired and now sells as Quva BrightStream Shortage Navigator, citing more than 3,000 hospitals (F2-S38; KLAS spotlight 2024, F2-S39 snippet). Wolters Kluwer Simplifi 797 is USP compounding compliance software, not shortage software (F2-S40). Lumere was absorbed into GHX in 2020 (F2-S41) and Trulla into SpendMend in 2022 (F2-S42, snippet). USP sells the Medicine Supply Map, an AI shortage-risk platform that maps finished forms to API to key starting materials, and claims 100 percent of 2024 new injectable shortages were flagged high-risk in advance (F2-S43; vendor claim, monthly accuracy reporting offered). Angels for Change's Project PROTECT funds 503B facilities by grant to pre-build vulnerable medicines, using prediction technology to choose them (F2-S44).

Government side: FDA receives 506C interruption notices, requires redundancy risk management plans under 506C(j) (draft guidance 2022, F2-S66), and collects annual per-NDC manufacturing volumes under 510(j)(3) (F2-S65, F2-S67 snippet). ASPR's Office of Supply Chain Optimization and its National Medical Supply Chain Center monitor "production capabilities" (F2-S61). The Supply Chain Control Tower aggregates voluntary distributor data covering about 90 percent of pharmaceutical distribution but "does not facilitate two-way communication" and does not collect from API manufacturers (F2-S63; F2-S62 snippet; F2-S64). In the EU, EMA's ESMP has been mandatory for marketing authorisation holders and national authorities since 2 February 2025 (F2-S60).

Manufacturer-side coordination of reserved or surge capacity across independent firms was not found as a commercial software product. The closest instruments are contracts (Premier, Vizient), grants (Project PROTECT), and one-way government reporting. Whether that gap is unoccupied by design (antitrust and confidentiality) or by neglect is addressed in section 5.

## 4. What was tried and failed or discontinued, and the documented reasons

1. "Paper on glass" MES, industry-wide. MIT Sloan's Pfizer case states the industry "struggled to capture that value" for nearly two decades and that attempts "mostly produced what practitioners dismissively called 'paper on glass'" with disconnected equipment and siloed data (F2-S71). The case authors, not Pfizer executives, describe Pfizer's own program as perpetually behind schedule; one large site demanded a restart with a new vendor; regulatory refiling costs made one-size-fits-all standardization "essentially impossible" (F2-S71). The documented remedy was organizational (site partnership, templated "MES-lite"), not new software. Implication: an EBR wedge sold to small sites competes against this accumulated implementation know-how and against Körber's announced small-site product (F2-S01).

2. Standalone hospital shortage-analytics vendors did not stay standalone. LogicStream was acquired by Quva (F2-S38), Lumere by GHX (F2-S41), Trulla by SpendMend (F2-S42, snippet). None is documented as a failure; each is documented as absorption into a larger pharmacy-services or supply-chain platform. [inference] A shortage-analytics point product for hospitals reaches an exit as a feature, not as a company.

3. The federal Supply Chain Control Tower is documented as one-way, voluntary and at risk of losing data after the pandemic. NASEM in 2022 called federal monitoring "haphazard and inconsistent" (F2-S64); HDA in 2025 reports no two-way communication, no technology platform for data submission ("requests are fielded by email and phone"), and voluntary agreements that "will end" with the COVID response (F2-S63). HDA also lists the Sherman Antitrust Act and DOJ/FTC monitoring as policy barriers to inter-company data collaboration (F2-S63, snippet portion). This is the documented reason cross-manufacturer coordination platforms are scarce.

4. AI-generated GMP documentation without quality-unit review. FDA Warning Letter 320-26-58 (2 April 2026) records a firm that used AI agents to write specifications, procedures and master production records, was cited under 21 CFR 211.22(c), reported that the agent "never told you it was required" when asked about process validation, and has ceased drug production (F2-S52; F2-S77 snippet). The firm was a small homeopathic manufacturer, not a software vendor, so this is a documented failure mode rather than a product failure.

5. No OS-only pharma software vendor shutdown was found in the 2024 to 2026 window. Searches for discontinued pharma MES or QMS products returned CDMO closures rather than software closures. AmplifyBio, an Ohio contract development and manufacturing organization, closed in April 2025 after failing to find investment or a buyer (F2-S90); it is not in this family. A second CDMO closure lead (Exothera, Belgium) could not be confirmed from a fetchable page and is recorded in section 9 instead. Absence of a found failure is not evidence that none occurred; consolidation (Körber/Werum, Honeywell/Sparta at $1.3 billion, Apprentice/Ganymede) is the documented pattern (F2-S02, F2-S16, F2-S10).

6. Repository-level: the repo's own release-layer analysis found that chemical real-time release removes roughly zero days from an aseptic injectable's hold because sterility incubation is the pole (F2-S78 section 1). A documentation or review-by-exception wedge attacks the "QA batch record review + QP disposition" component, which the repo estimates at 0 to 4 days beyond the last result (F2-S78 table in section 1). Vendor claims of batch review falling from "20 days to 1 day" (F2-S13 family) describe paper-based baselines, not the model's baseline.

## 5. Regulatory basis and constraints

Labels follow the repository protocol: law_or_regulation, final_guidance, draft_guidance, agency_initiative, industry_guidance, unresolved_question. Interpretations are UNCERTAIN unless a qualified reviewer confirms them.

### 5.1 United States, drug CGMP

- 21 CFR Part 11 (law_or_regulation; source 62 FR 13464, 1997) governs electronic records and signatures; 11.10 (controls for closed systems), 11.50 and 11.70 (signature manifestation and linking), 11.100 to 11.300 (electronic signatures) are the sections an EBR, QMS or decision-support tool must satisfy (F2-S53; section numbers cited from the Part 11 table of contents on the fetched page). Every vendor in section 3 advertises Part 11 conformance (F2-S09, F2-S11, F2-S12, F2-S13). [inference] Part 11 conformance is therefore table stakes, not a moat.
- 21 CFR 211.188 requires batch production and control records with "complete information relating to the production and control of each batch" (F2-S54). 211.192 requires that "All drug product production and control records, including those for packaging and labeling, shall be reviewed and approved by the quality control unit to determine compliance with all established, approved written procedures before a batch is released or distributed", and that any unexplained discrepancy "shall be thoroughly investigated, whether or not the batch has already been distributed"; 211.22(c) gives the quality control unit "the responsibility for approving or rejecting all procedures or specifications impacting on the identity, strength, quality, and purity of the drug product"; 211.68 governs "Automatic, mechanical, and electronic equipment" and at (b) requires that "A backup file of data entered into the computer or related system shall be maintained", subject to a stated exception for data eliminated by computerization; 211.100(a) requires written production and process control procedures that are "drafted, reviewed, and approved by the appropriate organizational units and reviewed and approved by the quality control unit" (F2-S55, part 211 fetched on re-check). Correction to an earlier draft of this dossier: 211.100 is "Written procedures; deviations", not a process-validation requirement. Interpretation: review by exception does not remove the 211.192 review; it changes what the reviewer reads. UNCERTAIN in the sense that the acceptable scope of "exception" is set by the firm's validated system and the inspector, not by regulation.
- FDA's 2018 Data Integrity Q&A (final_guidance) defines audit trails, states who reviews them (personnel responsible for record review, and the quality unit under 211.192), and ties review frequency to 211.188(b) (after each significant step) and 211.22 (before batch release) (F2-S51; questions 7 and 8 by number, seen in the fetched PDF's table of contents; an earlier reference to a question "1c" is dropped because that string was not found in the extracted text). Any EBR or lineage product must expose audit trails for human review at those frequencies.
- Computer Software Assurance (final_guidance, September 2025) is scoped to "medical device production or the quality system" under 21 CFR 820 (F2-S50). Applying CSA thinking to drug CGMP software is common industry practice through GAMP 5 second edition (F2-S68, industry_guidance), but it is an unresolved_question whether FDA drug inspectors accept CSA-style reduced testing for a drug MES. UNCERTAIN.
- FDA's January 2025 AI draft guidance (draft_guidance, docket FDA-2024-D-4689) excludes AI used "for operational efficiencies (e.g., internal workflows, resource allocation, drafting/writing a regulatory submission) that do not impact patient safety, drug quality" (F2-S48). Interpretation: EBR authoring aids, scheduling and shortage-response coordination tools sit outside the credibility framework; drift detection that triggers a hold, deviation classification that changes disposition, and any release-related model sit inside it and follow the seven-step credibility assessment (F2-S49). No final version was located on 2026-09-02; a trade status table dated August 2026 still lists it as draft (F2-S76, snippet). UNCERTAIN until a final is published.
- Warning Letter 320-26-58 (April 2026) is the first documented enforcement applying 211.22(c) to AI-generated GMP documents: "any output or recommendations from an AI agent must be reviewed and cleared by an authorized human representative of your firm's QU" (F2-S52). Interpretation: any deviation-drafting or record-generating product must be designed so the quality unit's review and clearance is recorded; the vendor cannot hold the 211.22 responsibility. Not UNCERTAIN as to the requirement; UNCERTAIN as to what level of review evidence satisfies an inspector.
- CDER's Quality Management Maturity program (agency_initiative) links mature quality systems to shortage prevention and opened a third cohort of its prototype assessment protocol evaluation program, announced in a Federal Register notice of 11 February 2026, for up to nine establishments (F2-S45). It rates manufacturers, not vendors. The empirical link between QMM and shortage avoidance is described in FDA's own white paper as resting partly on "unpublished data" (F2-S87, snippet). Treat the shortage-reduction benefit of quality software as plausible, not quantified.
- FRAME (agency_initiative) lists AI in manufacturing and distributed manufacturing as priorities; the DME proposed rule (July 2026) is the FRAME output most relevant here (F2-S46, F2-S47). The repository's reading of the proposed rule is that a DME needs "a single quality unit" under one management (F2-S79). Interpretation: an OS-only vendor cannot be the quality unit of a DME or of any registrant; it can only be a supplier to one. UNCERTAIN pending line-by-line reading of FR 2026-14073 (HA-15 in F2-S81).
- Shortage data flows (law_or_regulation and guidance): 506C notifications, 506C(j) redundancy risk management plans (draft_guidance 2022, F2-S66), and 510(j)(3) annual per-NDC volume reports through the CDER NextGen portal (F2-S65; F2-S67 snippet). These define the manufacturer-side data a coordination product would need to ingest or generate. FDA does not publish 510(j)(3) volumes back to industry [inference from the guidance's stated uses in the snippet].

### 5.2 European Union

- EudraLex Volume 4 Chapter 4 (Documentation) and Annex 11 (Computerised Systems) remain the January 2011 texts on the Commission's index; no Annex 22 appears in the index (F2-S59, observation on the fetched page). The consultation on revised Chapter 4, revised Annex 11 and new Annex 22 closed 7 October 2025 (F2-S56).
- Draft Annex 22 (F2-S57) applies to AI "in critical applications with direct impact on patient safety, product quality or data integrity"; it applies only to static models and to models with deterministic output; it states that dynamic models and probabilistic models "should not be used in critical GMP applications" and that generative AI and LLMs "should not be used in critical GMP applications"; LLMs in non-critical applications require a qualified human responsible for output suitability. Section 10 of the draft covers operation, including performance and input drift monitoring and human-review records (F2-S56 summary; F2-S76 snippet for section numbering).
- Status correction to the repository: F2-S78 section 2a states finalisation was "expected mid-2026". As of 2026-09-02 no final Annex 22 is listed (F2-S59), and EMA held a two-day workshop on 30 June and 1 July 2026 because its consultation "suggested support for potentially enabling" generative and adaptive models, with EMA "still considering the implications" (F2-S58). Interpretation: the repository's claim that a locked deterministic model is a competitive advantage under Annex 22 is correct against the draft and may weaken if the final text admits guardrailed adaptive or probabilistic models. UNCERTAIN.
- Annex 16 (QP certification) and Annex 17 (RTRT and parametric release) constrain any hold or release decision support in the EU; the repository already carries the Annex 17 sections 3.10 and 4.18 one-way ratchet (F2-S78 section 2).
- EMA states that "MAHs of centrally authorised products must use the platform to report medicine shortages regularly", and that this follows EMA's extended mandate, laid out in Regulation (EU) 2022/123 (F2-S89). The news item carrying the 2 February 2025 mandatory date does not itself name the regulation (F2-S60). A shortage-response coordination product for EU manufacturers would have to interoperate with ESMP templates rather than replace them.

### 5.3 Hospital-side software

Simplifi 797, ShortageCheck, Quva BrightStream and Vizient analytics are governed by USP <797>/<795>/<800>, state boards of pharmacy and Joint Commission expectations (F2-S40), not by 21 CFR 211. Their buyer, contract and validation burden differ from manufacturer software. This matters for the model: hospital-side software changes buyer inventory behavior; it does not change manufacturer capacity.

### 5.4 Antitrust and data-sharing

HDA identifies antitrust law and DOJ/FTC monitoring as barriers to inter-company supply-chain data collaboration (F2-S63, snippet portion of the same fact sheet). A cross-manufacturer capacity coordination platform would need a legal structure (GPO, government convening, or a neutral nonprofit such as EDSA) before it needs software. unresolved_question; needs counsel.

## 6. Adjacent-industry analogues

Process historians and asset frameworks. AVEVA PI (formerly OSIsoft) originates in general process industries; the Boehringer Ingelheim animal-health case shows a pharma-adjacent site using PI asset framework event frames to make SIMCA monitoring reproducible across sites, with the quoted observation that most of the work is data management (F2-S21). The lesson for sub-idea (e) is that lineage tooling is a cost center bought from incumbents, not a differentiator.

Cross-industry MES analyst coverage. Gartner's MES definition quoted by Siemens describes a cross-industry software class that synchronizes execution with ERP and PLM and supports genealogy and process history (F2-S06); the 2025 Market Guide selected vendors on "inquiry volumes, market presence and the ability to cover the core MES use cases" (F2-S08, snippet). Pharma MES is a vertical of a larger, consolidated market.

Process-control patents. Emerson's exception-rules patent (F2-S74) was filed by Fisher-Rosemount, the DCS business, and is written for "manufacturing plants" generally. Review by exception is process-industry intellectual property that pharma inherited, not the reverse.

National supply-chain awareness frameworks. NASEM's 2022 framework treats awareness as transparency plus analytics plus communication for all medical products, and documents the SCCT's reliance on four distributors covering about 90 percent of pharmaceutical distribution (F2-S64; F2-S63). Shortage-response coordination in other critical-goods sectors is out of scope here and was not searched.

Facility risk scoring from public text. Pharm3r holds a granted patent on NLP classification of public documents into production-issue labels and facility risk scores (F2-S75). This is the analytics analogue of USP's Medicine Supply Map and of Bluesight's market signals: risk prediction from external data rather than from plant-floor data.

Summary answer to the family's question. No sub-idea is scientifically novel. Six of eight are already implemented at scale by incumbents with more than a decade of validated deployments. The two that are not yet occupied, (g) across independent owners and (h) on the manufacturer side, are unoccupied for reasons that are documented and structural: data confidentiality between competing manufacturers, antitrust exposure, and the fact that the paying customer for shortage response today is the hospital or GPO, whose money already flows to Vizient, Premier and Bluesight. [inference] The strongest OS-only company in this family is not a new EBR or QMS vendor. It is either a supplier of a narrow validated component to an incumbent platform (drift monitoring, per-site conformal validity as an equivalence instrument for the DME single-quality-unit requirement) or a neutral coordination layer that first needs a contracting vehicle. Neither is a software-first company in the sense of the assignment's option (3); both are contract-first.
