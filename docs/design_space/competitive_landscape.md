# Competitive landscape: synthesis across nine categories

Prepared 2026-09-03 for the adversarial design-space review. This file selects, compares and concludes from nine verified landscape dossiers. It introduces no new sources. Every claim carries the source id of the dossier that verified it. Markers `[inference]` and `[unverified]` are carried forward from the note that made them. A citation audit on 2026-09-03 found that most of them had been dropped in drafting and reinstated them; a synthesis sentence here that carries no marker has been checked against its note, but the notes remain the authority.

Nothing here is a recommendation. Nothing here has been reviewed by a regulatory or commercial professional. Absence of an idea from this file is not evidence that the idea is novel.

### 1.2 Blind spots, stated plainly

These are the gaps that most limit what this synthesis can conclude.

- **No price exists anywhere in the evidence base.** No published rate card, minimum order value, reservation fee or per-unit price was obtained for US sterile fill-finish (LCDM §6), for 503B product (L503B §6), for enterprise QMS (LQBR §6), for MES (LPMS §6), for shortage-data platforms (LSD §6), or for modular hardware (LAMD §6). The only public prices in 313 sources are SimplerQMS at USD 17,500 per year for 15 users (LQBR-S14) and an ASHP individual subscription at USD 7 to 9 per month (LSD-S7). Every economic comparison below is therefore directional.
- **Contract terms are private.** Committed-volume, failure-to-supply and reservation terms are not disclosed in either Premier release (LSIM-S18, LSIM-S19), in Vizient's FAQ (LNPM-S19), or in the public SEC exhibit of the largest US capacity reservation, where the per-batch price is redacted (F7-S04).
- **Enforcement data is not a capability measure.** Every inference drawn from warning letters records where FDA looked and found problems. Inspection frequency is not uniform and no base-rate correction was applied (LSIM §6.3).
- **Eight named manufacturers were not established.** Dr. Reddy's, Gland, Teva, Viatris, Cipla and Meitheal carry no verified US sterile footprint; American Regent and WG Critical Care are only partially verified (LSIM §6.2, LSIM-S29, LSIM-S30).
- **Vendor scale claims dominate the software categories.** Only two audited figures exist across LPMS and LQBR: Kneat's FY2025 revenue and ARR (LPMS-S10) and Veeva's FY2026 results (LQBR-S04). Analyst quadrant positions are paywalled and unread (LQBR-S22).
- **Not covered at all:** wholesaler allocation systems at McKesson, Cencora and Cardinal, which probably hold the real-time availability data (LSD §6.10); state-level programs other than CalRx; UK shortage-response instruments beyond the capital grant scheme (LGOV §6).

## 3. Recent structural events 2023 to 2026, and what they imply for entry

**The merchant sterile capacity pool shrank while total capacity grew.** Catalent Bloomington became captive Novo Nordisk in December 2024 (LCDM-S1, LCDM-S31). Emergent sold four commercial aseptic fill lines and shut two more sites (LCDM-S10, LCDM-S30). Sanofi's Ridgefield arrived at Thermo Fisher already committed to a Sanofi portfolio (LCDM-S9, LCDM-S33). Additions are real but format-specific and aimed at GLP-1, ADCs and biologics, not hospital generics (LCDM-S3, LCDM-S5, LCDM-S26). *Implication* **[inference]**: any capacity model that counts US aseptic units without separating merchant from captive, and vials from syringes and cartridges, overstates what is available to a shortage intervention (LCDM §5.2).

**Four firms with capital and distribution already in place exited 503B in six years.** PharMEDium under AmerisourceBergen after a consent decree, Fresenius Kabi by sale, CAPS under B. Braun by wind-down, and Nephron's 503B division as reported but unconfirmed (L503B-S15, L503B-S20, L503B-S18, L503B §6). B. Braun kept the 21 patient-specific 503A pharmacies and dropped the batch business. *Implication* **[inference]**: the batch 503B model has been tested repeatedly by better-capitalised entrants and abandoned. Four exits is the count of cases assembled here, not a base rate against the number of entrants, which no source gives.

**The enforcement wave of 2024 to 2026 is about facility and equipment design, not analytics.** Par Health's lines "lacked fundamental RABS design elements" (LSIM-S5); FDA asked Eugia whether it plans "a more extensive redesign or replacement of your aseptic processing lines" (LSIM-S4); FDA told Jubilant the same aseptic simulation deficiency was cited in 2023 and 2024 and "You have failed to adequately correct this deficiency" (LCDM-S7); FDA told the Bloomington site that contamination releases continued "well after the Novo Nordisk acquisition" (LCDM-S2). *Implication* **[inference]**: compliance state persists across inspection cycles and survives changes of ownership. A network that prequalifies capacity once and activates it years later is qualifying a moving target (LCDM §5.6).

**The federal government funded the exact thesis.** EQUIP-A-Pharma launched 2025-05-15 with four performers, eight drugs and an explicit goal of "a real-time digital regulatory approval framework" (LGOV-S12, LGOV-S15, LAMD-S04). ODP has a modular unit deployed inside a hospital producing shortage drugs (LGOV-S14, F14-S34). DEKA sent a point-of-care sterile unit to FDA for review (LGOV-S16). JPEO-CBRND made "Technologies that Reduce Product Release Time" a technical area of an RFI (LGOV-S36, LGOV-S37). BARDA's own solicitation names "distributed manufacturing systems with a special focus on vertical integration of the systems with fill and finish lines" as an example platform (LGOV-S23, LGOV-R01). *Implication* **[inference]**: distributed sterile manufacture with an AI release layer is the government's stated preference, which makes it crowded rather than novel (LGOV §5.1).

**The demand narrative moved against the pitch.** FDA reports 55 new shortages in CY2023, 15 in CY2024 and 4 in CY2025 (LSIM-S21). GAO's surviving finding is that existing shortages last longer and concentrate in sterile injectables (LSIM-S22). Meanwhile hospital shortage-management labour roughly doubled (LSD-S11B). *Implication* **[inference]**: a pitch premised on a rising count of new shortages contradicts the regulator's own number. The defensible framing is duration and response cost, not incidence.

**Federal purchasing finally moved, and it bought units, not readiness.** USAntibiotics said in 2024 it needed a government customer to survive (LNPM-S27) and received a $29,998,678 firm-fixed-price twelve-month order in May 2026, one offer received, placed against a standing VA Federal Supply Schedule vehicle (LNPM-S26). In April 2026 the Army began paying MCCPDC to build the ANDA CMC package that would lift the shortage gate on its own production (LNPM-S26). *Implication* **[inference]**: the binding constraint that cleared was contractable demand, not capability, and the instrument was a unit purchase order. This is one case, not a pattern.

**The largest industrial-policy lever does not reach shortage drugs.** The April 2026 Section 232 proclamation applies to patented pharmaceuticals; clause (5) states generics "shall not be subject to tariffs pursuant to section 232 at this time" (LGOV-S21). *Implication* **[inference]**: the instrument moving the most private capital into US pharmaceutical construction is aimed at the segment that is not short (LGOV §5.3).

**Regulatory acceleration arrived as a free government program.** FDA PreCheck received over 80 requests and selected seven, none a merchant sterile CDMO and none a distributed or modular network (LCDM-S22, LAMD-S11). *Implication* **[inference]**: a startup cannot sell what FDA is giving away, and a shortage-focused entrant with an ANDA plan fits the stated eligibility with little competition from CDMOs (LSIM §5.7, LCDM §5.7).

**Consolidation removed independent counterparties.** Premier taken private at $2.6B (LSD-S13, LSD-S13B); Sharp absorbed the main US small-batch isolator CDMO (LCDM-S12); QuVa bought its shortage-analytics vendor (LSD-S15); Siemens bought Dotmatics at $5.1bn (LPMS-S18); Gartner published its first QMS Magic Quadrant in January 2026 (LQBR-S22). *Implication* **[inference]**: buyers now have a shortlist mechanism a new entrant is not on, and several natural partners are now owned by competitors or by private equity.

## 4. Unoccupied or weakly occupied intervention points, each with its counter-argument

Ranked by how well the evidence supports the vacancy. None is a recommendation and none has been priced.

**4.1 Buyer-facing, contract-linked assurance that reserved capacity will still pass an inspection when called.** *Evidence.* Demand certainty is occupied by GPOs, nonprofits and federal programs (LSIM §5.4). **[inference]** What nobody sells is continuous, contract-linked, buyer-visible assurance over the life of the contract. Pfizer's own policy paper proposes long-term contracting requirements, notes quality scorecards "have been discussed", and adds "this may not be sufficient to move purchasing away from considerations based solely on price" (LSIM-S20). FDA's QMM program exists precisely because "a root cause for many drug shortages is the absence of incentives for manufacturers to strive for more than simply meeting CGMP regulations" (LQBR-S01, LQBR-S32). Every QMS product in the category is a single-tenant system of record for one firm (LQBR §5.3). *Counter-argument.* Three GPOs cover over 90% of US hospitals and "often focus on securing the lowest-price product as the sole criterion" (LSIM-S20). The natural aggregator of cross-firm reliability data is a purchaser consortium or a regulator, not a vendor. FDA may fill the assessment side itself and the QMM disclosure policy is unknown (LSIM §6.1). Certifying a site that later fails carries the same liability that sank the release thesis.

**4.2 A verified, contractible registry of activatable qualified capacity, with per-product time-to-first-qualified-batch.** *Evidence.* **[inference]** Every platform maps where a drug is made now; none publishes who could make it by when (LSD §5.1). Activation latency is real and large: Leiters bought a 110,000 ft2 plant in February 2022 that remains "Not yet inspected" 4.2 years later (L503B-S01, L503B-S21). The Emergent reservation fee explicitly "does not include tech transfer, process and analytical development, process development, raw materials and lot release testing of a batch" (F7-S04). **[inference]** Nobody in 503B sells or measures time-to-qualified-output (L503B §5.3). *Counter-argument.* ASPR has already named the concept and put money behind it: the DS-MRN challenge is "a secure digital infrastructure that stores manufacturing specifications and connects them with a network of vetted domestic manufacturers ready to produce critical medical products during shortages" (LGOV-S17), Phase 1 closed 2026-08-28 (LGOV-S51). The registry is only worth building if someone pays for activation, and the only parties who pay for activation are the two GPOs (LSD §5.1). The underlying data is held by the parties who benefit from opacity.

**4.3 The microbiological half of release at a small node.** *Evidence.* None of the four EQUIP-A-Pharma performer descriptions mentions sterility testing, rapid microbiological methods or environmental monitoring; they name chemical synthesis, in-line metrology and real-time quality assessment (LAMD-S03, LAMD-S04). The equipment vendors sell contamination control inside the isolator, not a faster release decision on microbiological attributes (LAMD-S14, LAMD-S16). **[inference]** That no entity is visibly working the microbiological half is the note's reading of an absence of evidence in its own search, not a checked absence (LAMD §5.1). Environmental monitoring review is a mandatory element of the batch release decision regardless of sterilization route (F11-S04, F11-S01). *Counter-argument.* JPEO-CBRND is already soliciting "mechanisms to enable real-time release testing or dramatically accelerate release timelines" (LGOV-S36, LGOV-S37), so this is a stated government requirement rather than an unclaimed idea. RMM sterility in 1 to 2 days is already sold commercially for compounded products (F11-S36). FDA's only RMM sterility guidance was withdrawn unfinalised in 2015 and never replaced (F11-S17, F11-S18). And CDER has granted zero AMT designations from five requests, with both determinations negative (LAMD-S01).

**4.4 Statistical demonstration of cross-unit equivalence for a distributed manufacturing establishment.** *Evidence.* **[inference]** Vendors sell multi-site instances and central templates, but none markets the demonstration that unit N performs equivalently to unit 1 (LPMS §5.3, LPMS-S02, LPMS-S07, LPMS-S08). The DME proposal conditions the pathway on a single quality unit running a unified pharmaceutical quality system across units and leaves equivalence undefined (LQBR-S25, LAMD-S25). Every funded actor is proving a platform at one site (LAMD §5.2, marked [inference] there). *Counter-argument.* The rule is proposed, comments close 2026-09-11 and are still open as of this file's date, and no DME exists to sell to (LQBR §5.5). The preamble states "the registrant must be a single legal entity" and rules out CMO-operated units, so any architecture relying on prequalified third-party capacity cannot use the pathway at all (LPMS-S22, L503B-S06). **[inference]** Veeva or MasterControl can ship equivalence monitoring inside their existing multi-site models the day the first applicant needs it (LQBR §5.5). This is an evidence method and a filing artefact, not a company.

**4.5 Instrumented, verifiable aseptic intervention counting and retrospective product-impact assessment.** *Evidence.* FDA is now quantifying interventions and catching under-reporting: "Your data reported maximum aseptic intervention counts per batch of over (b)(4)", of which "[a]pproximately 30% of these interventions involved lubricating productions lines" (LSIM-S5); Eugia Unit III showed "a pattern of inadequate documentation and underreporting of interventions" (LSIM-S3). Intervention logging and video review are already records obligations under 21 CFR 211.180(a) (LSIM-S3). Separately, FDA rejected the firm's own impact assessment in every 2025 to 2026 letter read (LSIM §5.3). *Counter-argument.* The buyer is a firm already in enforcement, which is distressed, episodic and reputationally awkward, and in at least one case remediation runs through a third-party consultant review (LSIM-S5). Line-vision vendors and equipment OEMs are the plausible incumbents and no competitive check was run, so the vacancy is [unverified] (LSIM §5.1, §5.2). A tool that produces impact conclusions inherits liability for them.

**4.6 Payment for standing ready, separable from units shipped, for civilian generic injectables.** *Evidence.* Every funding mechanism found prices units or reimburses cost. Civica sells against minimum-volume commitments (LNPM-S03). ProvideGx and NES Reserve pay for produced inventory (LNPM-S18, LNPM-S19). Phlow is cost-plus-fixed-fee (LNPM-S11). The decisive case is USAntibiotics: what it eventually received was a firm-fixed-price order for a stated quantity of capsules over twelve months (LNPM-S26). **[inference]** Every federal program funds capital, none funds operations (LGOV §5.4), and GAO's CIADM finding names the failure this produces: sites were built, had no regular manufacturing work, and could not hold the capability (LGOV-S11, LGOV §5.4). *Counter-argument.* This is a policy gap, not a company-shaped one. Standby is not cheap: read per year, EU FAB costs roughly what one BARDA large-volume-parenteral solicitation ceiling costs once, for vaccines only (LGOV-S28, LGOV-S44, LGOV-S45). DLA concluded expansion of Warstopper was feasible and needed no new statutory authority, and three years later it has not happened, blocked by manufacturer reluctance to hold volumes they may not sell (LGOV-S07). Whether an availability payment untied to any ordered quantity is even procurable under FAR Part 16 is uncertain (F10-S27). **[inference]** PJM's experience is read in the note as showing that availability payments without performance penalties produce non-delivery when it matters; F7-S28 states only that Capacity Performance added investment incentives and non-performance penalties, and that resources assume the financial risk of not meeting their obligations (F7-S28).

**4.7 Independent, prospective evaluation of prediction and release claims.** *Evidence.* Every accuracy claim in the shortage-data category is self-reported by the vendor being evaluated, on a threshold the vendor chose (LSD-S5, LSD-S14). No AI feature in the quality category publishes an error rate, a risk-coverage curve, or a stated behaviour under distribution shift (LQBR §5.2). **[inference]** No federal program funds a party whose job is to test whether a release model holds under shift (LGOV §5.2). *Counter-argument.* This is a paper, not a company (LSD §5.4). The buyer for a statistical guarantee inside a quality workflow is a quality unit that must defend it to an inspector, and no evidence was found that such a buyer exists. FDA's own review function partially covers it. Incumbents can add a calibration layer to their own agents without changing distribution.

**4.8 Buffer-stock-as-a-service against the CMS essential-medicines payment.** *Evidence.* CMS already pays eligible hospitals for establishing and maintaining a six-month buffer stock, explicitly permitting the stock to be held through a manufacturer, distributor or intermediary (LSD-S3). **[inference]** No vendor was found selling that service, or the cost-report documentation the payment requires (LSD §5.3). *Counter-argument.* Eligibility is limited to hospitals with 100 beds or fewer that are not part of a chain organisation (LSD-S3), and the payment excludes medicines already in shortage. The absolute market is small. Vizient already removes the inventory cost for its members at claimed 131 million units of scale with no up-front investment or program fee (LNPM-S19), which is the incumbent offer for everyone else.

## 5. What a new entrant must not underestimate

**Observed build timelines, not first-principles estimates.** **[inference]** That these observed instances, rather than first-principles estimates, are the right comparator for a distributed-microplant timeline is the notes' reading (LSIM §5.7, LAMD §6). Nexus: greenfield US sterile plant, >84,000 ft2, isolator technology, passing inspection announced May 2023 (LSIM-S16). Leiters: a bought, pre-existing 110,000 ft2 pharmaceutical plant, registered July 2022, still not inspected in August 2026 (L503B-S01, L503B-S21). Civica Petersburg: operational but had not begun commercial manufacture as of the last confirmed public statement in May 2025, with market entry slipped from 2025 to 2026 (LNPM-S07, F10-S34). BioNTech Kigali: inaugurated December 2023 with process-validation batches only planned for 2025 in the last verified company statement (LAMD-S21). Apiject: approximately five years from award to NDA submission (LGOV-S27, LGOV-S39). White Raven is the fastest documented case at 18 months from project kickoff to GMP certification on a purchased SA25 workcell, in Europe, for a startup CDMO (LAMD-S15). Purchase-to-GMP for the fill hardware alone is 12 to 15 months for an SA25 and 6 to 9 for a Microcell (LAMD-S14).

**Capex scale.** Incumbent expansions in this window: Hikma $1B by 2030 on top of >$4B over 15 years (LSIM-S11); Baxter >$2.5B across the US network over a decade (LSIM-S10); Fresenius >USD 250M at Melrose Park (LSIM-S12); Simtra $250M+ (LCDM-S3); Vetter ~$285M for a clinical-only US site (LCDM-S17); Curia $200M (LCDM-S8); Jubilant $132M (LCDM-S6); GRAM $100M (LCDM-S5). Even expanding an already-validated 503B costs tens of millions: $25M validated at Wichita, ~$39M more by 2027, ~$29M at Las Vegas (L503B-S19). Adding a small amount of aseptic capacity is the most crowded activity in the CDMO category (LCDM §5, closing paragraph).

**The channel, and switching costs inside it.** Three GPOs cover over 90% of US hospitals (LSIM-S20). Vizient serves more than 65% of US acute care providers on a $140 billion contract portfolio (LSD-S11). Premier is guided by health systems with more than 1,600 hospitals (LNPM-S18). Both GPOs already run committed-volume and buffer-inventory programs, and the Premier co-investment in Exela shows a GPO will fund an ANDA when it wants supply (LNPM-S18). Selling outside that channel means selling against it. Selling inside it means competing on price, which is what the channel optimises.

**Contract asymmetry.** **[inference]** That the shape disclosed in Lifecore's own filing is the standard shape of a US CDMO capacity relationship is the note's reading of one filing, not a surveyed fact (LCDM §5.3). The filing states: the agreement "includes annual committed capacity obligations on the Company without minimum purchase obligations on Alcon", and both agreements permit sourcing from third parties (LCDM-S14). A shortage intervention needs the mirror image and the evidence base contains no commercial instance of it. On the buyer side, HHS records that "the erosion of failure-to-supply clauses in GPO contracts may be associated with drug shortages" and that low-price clauses let intermediaries walk away (F10-S10), and under 15 percent of one GPO's members' generic injectable purchases went through its shortage program (F10-S15).

**Regulatory time is not schedulable.** CDER has granted zero AMT designations from five requests, with two determinations both negative, and no application anywhere has yet referenced a designated AMT (LAMD-S01). PreCheck took over 80 requests and selected seven, none of them a distributed or modular network (LCDM-S22). The DME pathway requires an approved application, a preapproval inspection, one management, a unified quality system, and a single legal entity as registrant (L503B-S06, LPMS-S22). For 503B there is still no facility-specific CGMP regulation thirteen years after the DQSA, only a draft marked "Not for implementation" (L503B-S04, L503B-S05).

**Incumbents are already moving into the adjacent software wedge.** QuVa, the largest 503B by hospital count, bought a predictive-analytics company and now holds two national Premier agreements, one of them for software (L503B-S30, L503B-S31, LSD-S15). Rockwell publicly concedes MES is "expensive, slow to implement and lacking flexibility" and shipped a containerised release aimed at that (LPMS-S05). Korber advertises a purpose-built MES for small and mid-sized manufacturers. Tulip and Leucine already sell configuration-speed deployment (LPMS §5.7). **[inference]** A wedge premised on "no MES fits a small sterile node" has a shrinking window and at least four vendors moving into it (LPMS §5.7, LQBR §5.4).

**Federal money is already committed to the same architecture, sole-source and long-dated.** Phlow's contract runs to a potential 2030 with roughly $697M obligated and its holder not yet profitable after six years (LGOV-S18, LNPM-S09). **[inference]** A new entrant asking ASPR or BARDA for the same function is proposing to duplicate an incumbent contract (LNPM §3, Phlow row). BioMaP entry requires Manufacturing Readiness Level 6 with mandatory cost share, which excludes anything pre-prototype (LGOV-S23, LGOV-R01).

**Exit is common in this sector.** No source here gives an entrant denominator, so no base rate is computed and none should be read into this list. Akorn (LSIM-S2), Athenex (LSIM-S28), PharMEDium (L503B-S15), Fresenius Kabi's 503B (L503B-S20), CAPS (L503B-S18), Emergent's CDMO business (LCDM-S10), Ajinomoto's US sterile fill-finish (LCDM-S11), and California's own in-state plant funding (LNPM-S20). One of these, Emergent, is documented here as having held large federal capacity money before exiting: a $542,750,000 Task 1 Capacity Reservation (F7-S04, LCDM-S10). No second case among the exits above is established by these notes.

## 6. Open questions

Ordered by how much the answer would change the conclusions above.

1. **Does any GPO, health-system or federal capacity contract contain a quality or capability covenant, as distinct from a volume commitment with failure-to-supply penalties?** This single fact decides whether §4.1 is an opportunity or a misreading. Terms are private in every source (LSIM §6.3, LNPM §6, LCDM §6). Closing it requires a GPO contracting interview, SAM.gov review or FOIA.
2. **What is the status and disclosure policy of FDA's Quality Management Maturity ratings?** If ratings become purchaser-visible, §4.1 closes. GAO describes QMM as an effort FDA "started developing" as of April 2025 (LSIM-S22); CDER opened a third prototype year for up to nine establishments in 2026 (LQBR-S01).
3. **Does any US CDMO currently manufacture a drug on FDA's shortage list under a third party's ANDA?** This determines whether a virtual-network architecture has a live precedent or would be a first. Nothing found answers it; it requires establishment-data search or direct enquiry (LCDM §6).
4. **Did RPP 26-2-AM-LVP produce awards, and to whom?** A ~$200M solicitation preferring antimicrobials and large-volume parenterals closed 2026-03-16 with no award announcement located (LGOV-S23, LGOV §6).
5. **What is the current status of EO 14336 implementation?** No published list of the ~26 critical drugs, no statement that the SAPIR repository is operational, no procurement notice for API purchases (LGOV-S20, LGOV §6). A sustained federal API purchase program would be the first durable demand instrument on the US side.
6. **Will the ASPR amoxicillin order recur, and at what annual volume?** It rides a standing VA FSS vehicle with no unexercised options, so recurrence turns on appropriations and requisitions rather than a new procurement (LNPM-S26, LNPM §6). The answer generalises to every "the government will buy domestic capacity" assumption.
7. **Has Civica's Petersburg site passed a pre-approval inspection or obtained approval for any product made there?** The last confirmed evidence is 16 months old (LNPM-S07, LNPM §6). This is the most important open fact about the leading comparator.
8. **What does a US sterile campaign actually cost, and what does an MES or eQMS cost a two-to-five-person node?** No price exists anywhere in 313 sources (LCDM §6, LPMS §6, LQBR §6). Every economic conclusion in this file is unpriced.
9. **Did the 2026 enforcement wave produce FDA-listed shortages?** Par Health suspended aseptic fill and release from affected lines, Eugia paused certain injectables, and an IMS epinephrine lot was implicated, but none was checked against the shortage database (LSIM §6.1). The check is cheap and directly relevant to product selection.
10. **Is the 2024 to 2026 aseptic-design finding cluster a real shift in FDA emphasis or an artifact of which sites were inspected?** No base-rate correction was applied to any warning-letter inference in this file (LSIM §6.3).
11. **What can be built from 503B product reports?** Twice-yearly unit counts exist, the only published analysis against the shortage list is six years old, and whether FDA publishes the underlying reports in usable form is [unverified] (L503B-S23, L503B-S25, L503B §6).
12. **Do any of the remaining EQUIP-A-Pharma performers have ANDAs on file or approved, and was Bright Path's reported August 2026 AMT designation real?** The designation is single-sourced to a trade-association write-up with no FDA or company confirmation (LAMD-S08, LAMD §6).
