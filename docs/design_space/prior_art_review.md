# Cross-family prior-art review

Synthesis of the fourteen prior-art dossiers in `docs/design_space/prior_art/` for the adversarial design-space review. Prepared 2026-09-03. This file cites only source ids that appear in those dossiers and only claims those dossiers support. No new material was fetched. Nothing here is legal or regulatory advice. `[inference]` marks reasoning, `[unverified]` marks a claim no fetched source supports, UNCERTAIN marks a reading that needs a qualified reviewer.

Source-id prefixes map to families: F1 and LR (Telo architectures), F2 (OS-only), F3 (virtual network), F4 and Lreg/Lpeer/Lpat/Lrepo (hub-and-spoke), F5 (postponement), F6 (multi-product), F7 (warm standby), F8 (inventory-capacity hybrids), F9 (upstream-first), F10 (contracts and procurement), F11 (terminal sterilization and release), F12 (product selection), F13 (public-private), F14 (mobile and modular).

## 1. Method

### 1.1 What was searched

Fourteen families, each covering five to seventeen named sub-ideas, were searched independently on 2026-09-02 with citation audits on 2026-09-03. Each dossier was required to answer the same six questions per sub-idea: who has built it, at what scale, under which registration and release regime, what happened, which rule governs it, and what would falsify the classification. Search targets across the fourteen were: US primary law and guidance (govinfo CFR annual-edition XML, eCFR versioner API, federalregister.gov API and full-text XML, uscode.house.gov, fda.gov guidance and warning letters); EU and UK primary text (EudraLex Volume 4 Annexes 1, 11, 15, 16, 17 and draft 22, EMA guidelines, EUR-Lex, legislation.gov.uk); oversight bodies (GAO, HHS OIG, HHS ASPE, CRS, NASEM); federal procurement records (USAspending API, SAM.gov, KEI-hosted FOIA contract copies, HigherGov as an aggregator); company filings (SEC EDGAR 8-K, 10-K, redacted supply agreements); patents via Google Patents; peer-reviewed literature via DOI, PubMed, PMC and publisher pages; vendor and trade material.

### 1.2 Verification protocol

`fetched_quoted` means the page was fetched in-session and the quote is verbatim from the fetched text. `snippet_only` means only a search-index excerpt was seen and no quote is asserted. `in_repo_file` means a repository file was read locally. Thirteen of the fourteen dossiers record a citation audit or verification pass on 2026-09-02 or 2026-09-03; family 2 records none. Only families 3, 6 and 13 state that every row was re-fetched. Those passes found no fabricated source, company, patent number, contract, DOI or URL anywhere, and found instead a recurring defect class of **misattribution**: load-bearing numbers cited to sources that do not contain them. Family 3 states seven wrong claim-to-source mappings and eight uncited claims; family 6 lists thirteen corrections; family 8 states nine findings; family 10 states twelve defects corrected and four sources added; family 12 tabulates twenty-two. Families 1, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 and 14 record audits without stating a count, so no count is given for them here. [inference] The reading this review takes from that record is that the sources are real and that the mapping from claim to source was not reliable until audited.

### 1.4 Known blind spots

- **Paywalled compendia.** USP `<71>`, `<72>`, `<73>`, `<1071>`, `<1207>`, `<1211>`, `<1222>`, `<1223>`, `<382>` and Ph. Eur. 5.1.1, 5.1.2, 5.1.6 were not read; only public FAQ and chapter-introduction pages were. The 14-day sterility incubation figure that anchors the whole release model is corroborated by three commercial sources and one roundtable, not by a fetched copy of `<71>` (F11 §9).
- **Paywalled industry standards.** PDA TR30 and TR33, PDA Points to Consider No. 15 (the single most relevant unread document on relocation requalification), ISPE Baseline Guide Vol. 5, ISPE Risk-MaPP body text, KLAS reports.
- **Non-English sources.** Effectively none. Germany's ALBVVG was read through a ministry summary in German; Finland through Fimea's English pages. No Japanese, Chinese, Indian or Brazilian regulatory material was searched, except one machine-translated Chinese patent application recorded as a lead and not cited (F11 §9).
- **Confidential industry data.** Batch sizes, changeover distributions, reservation fees, CDMO quotes, per-product fixed quality costs, container-closure commonality, per-region injectable utilization. Every reservation fee and shortfall formula in the SEC exhibits is redacted (F3-S53, F3-S54, F3-S55). Sterilization route per marketed presentation is not disclosed on US or EU labels.
- **Approved-application content.** Module 3.2.P.3 site tables, chemistry review sections and inspection reports are not public, which is why the single most decision-relevant question in family 4 (does any approved aqueous injectable list separate bulk-preparation and fill sites) could not be answered.
- **Hosts that refuse scripted fetch.** gao.gov, sec.gov, businesswire.com, cardinalhealth.com, pubmed, USP's edge, justice.gov, congress.gov legislation search, and fda.gov press announcements variously returned 403 or 404 to non-browser agents while live; ecfr.gov and federalregister.gov redirect automated clients to a CAPTCHA. Several were reached only through browser-equivalent routes, and one (justice.gov, on Ben Venue) could not be reached at all.
- **Patents.** Google Patents keyword search only. No USPTO full-text or Espacenet CPC-scoped search was run in any family, so no absence-of-patent claim is safe.
- **No interviews.** Every willingness-to-pay, cycle-time and cost figure originating with a vendor or a purchaser is self-reported.

## 3. Failed and discontinued attempts, grouped by documented cause

### 3.1 Economics

- **CONTINUUS Pharmaceuticals.** $69.3M DoD/HHS contract in January 2021 with a plant "fully operational within two years"; the Woburn build was cancelled in June 2023; in 2025 the board still described the first commercial GMP facility as awaiting a partner decision. Its own board says: "We've tried applying this model to generics, and it just doesn't work at their price points", and a continuous facility "would cost about $50 million, which just doesn't work for a product that sells for a few dollars per kilogram" (F1-S45, F1-S59, F1-S23, F1-S46, F14-S50, F14-S51).
- **USAntibiotics.** Capacity for 100% of US amoxicillin demand, serving about 5% of the market; unit production cost about $4 against a $5 buyer price that does not cover overhead; survival required a Walmart and McKesson channel deal (F3-S36, F3-S37, F3-S38, F13-S31).
- **Ben Venue Laboratories.** Ceased production in 2013; the company's own reason is "The magnitude of continued investment and time required to overcome the systemic manufacturing challenges is not viable" (F6-S33).
- **Akorn.** Chapter 7 in February 2023, all US operations shut, and a recall of all within-expiry product because "The discontinuation of the Quality program means the company will not be able to support or guarantee that the products will meet all intended specifications" (F6-S24). [inference] The products were not defective; the quality system that vouched for them stopped existing.
- **DoD Copperhead layaway.** Planners "decided not to maintain a production capacity ... because the round is expensive, requirements are low, the cost of maintaining a production line in layaway status would be prohibitive, and there are substitute items being developed" (F7-S19). [inference] Low volume plus high readiness cost plus an available substitute is the profile of many shortage-prone injectables.
- **DLA Warstopper expansion.** Manufacturers declined: "if the medications were not ordered on a Warstopper contract, they would not be able to sell the increased volume ... before the medications expire", and at least two said they would not participate (F7-S07, F10-S25).
- **SNS vendor-managed inventory.** All contracts terminated in 2017 as not cost-effective or not meeting preparedness goals; rotation works only where the commercial market can absorb the reserve before expiry (F8-S03).
- **Orgenesis mobile cGMP units.** Five OMPULs and the quality-system framework sold to Germfree for $8.34M in a sale-and-leaseback; Germfree then declined to lease any back; the parent reported ~$0.1M cash and going-concern doubt (F14-S44, F14-S45). [inference, carried from family 14] The hardware survived; the operator did not.
- **The structural statement.** FDA's own root-cause report: "Most generic manufacturers cannot afford to support redundant capacity and must make decisions about how to make the best use of their available capacity" (F7-S16). HHS locates the cause in "prices for generic drugs that are driven to levels so low that they create insufficient incentives for redundancy or resilience-oriented manufacturing" (F6-S18).

### 3.2 Regulation and quality systems

- **PharMEDium.** A four-site 503B network with one corporate quality organization; Memphis voluntarily suspended December 2017; a 2019 consent decree left only two sites operating; AmerisourceBergen closed the business in 2020 citing "increased regulatory and commercial challenges, coupled with the difficulty of making progress on remediation and continued financial burden", five years after paying $2.6 billion for it in 2015 (F4-S7, F4-S8, F4-S9, F6-S44). [inference] The failure mode was not the network topology; it was replicated aseptic-quality failure across sites under one quality organization, which is exactly the risk the hub-and-spoke and DME structures rely on.
- **Nephron.** 2022 warning letters to both the outsourcing facility and the conventional plant; repeat 483 observations through 2025; FDA records shared blow-fill-seal and sterilizing equipment across 503B and conventional operations, and warns the firm not to "introduce risks through overlap" (F1-S14, F1-S15, F1-S60).
- **Intas Sanand.** Data-integrity failures across production and laboratory departments; Import Alert 66-40 on 1 June 2023; one site's failure removed an oncology portfolio (F6-S22, F6-S23).
- **Teligent.** A four-to-eight-million-unit injectable line built inside an existing plant; the 2019 site warning letter blocked the pre-approval inspection for it; Chapter 11 in 2021 (F6-S35). [inference] This is the closest documented analogue to a Telo-scale node in the material collected, and site compliance, not line capacity, decided whether any product could launch.
- **CAPS (B. Braun).** Exited the 503B sterile compounding business in 2026 while keeping its 21-site 503A network; FDA's list shows regulatory meetings at both 503B sites on 5 May 2026; no source links the two (F4-S10, Lreg-S17).
- **FDA's only RMM sterility guidance.** The 2008 draft on growth-based rapid microbiological methods was withdrawn unfinalised in 2015, in a bulk withdrawal whose stated reason was resourcing, and has never been replaced (F11-S17, F11-S18).
- **CCIT as a release substitute.** FDA drew the line inside its own 2008 guidance: it "does not apply to sterility testing methods for product sterility testing prior to release" (F11-S14).
- **AI-generated GMP documentation.** Warning Letter 320-26-58 (2 April 2026) cites a firm under 21 CFR 211.22(c) for using AI agents to write specifications and master production records; the firm has ceased drug production; FDA states "any output or recommendations from an AI agent must be reviewed and cleared by an authorized human representative of your firm's QU" (F2-S52).
- **Terminal sterilization's real failure mode is chemical.** Sodium bicarbonate autoclaved at 121 °C formed sodium dawsonite from aluminium leached out of the glass; dilute norepinephrine autoclaved without stabilisers generates 3,4-dihydroxybenzaldehyde (F11-S31, F11-S32).
- **The "prevalidated modular GMP facility" patent** (WO2007067656A2, priority 2005), which claimed building cleanrooms near the approval authority and shipping them to the customer, is Ceased (F14-S59). The idea of pre-approving a unit and moving it is two decades old and did not survive as intellectual property.

### 3.3 Demand and contracting

- **CIADM, the most fully documented failure in the material collected.** A 2018 joint review found two of three sites with none of their capacity used and the third at 7 percent. BARDA spent about $4M per year on task orders against a stated ready-state requirement of $30M to $60M per year, and "avoided officially designating sites as ready to provide core services, as that would have required the agency to pay sustainment fees". When activated, the sites "were not ready to produce high-quality countermeasures reliably at a commercial scale ... and were learning in real time". Emergent Bayview stopped production April to July 2021 after cross-contamination; the contract was terminated in November 2021; none of the sites met the surge goal (F7-S01, F8-S09, F3-S7, F3-S8).
- **Why co-tenancy could not fund the standby.** "officials from two sites said that industry partners were concerned about the risk of being forced out of the sites when the government later needed the capacity for other reasons" (F7-S01). [inference] The priority right that gives reserved capacity its value suppresses the commercial demand meant to keep it warm.
- **BARDA's own 2018 diagnosis.** Preparedness work is intermittent and low-revenue, which "has been a deterrent to USG partnering in the past" (F3-S19).
- **Fill Finish Manufacturing Network.** About $40M to four companies in FY2013 against a 117-million-dose target, six contractors by 2016, roughly $62.6M obligated over the vehicle's life per an aggregator; no documented activation for a US shortage and no published retrospective (F7-S02, F13-S42, F3-S13, F7-S34). [inference] This is the closest federal precedent to Telo's thesis in the material collected, and its outcome is unknown.
- **UK VMIC.** About £200M of public money for a facility expected to deliver 70 million pandemic doses; it "never opened its doors" and was sold to Catalent before completion, because "the initial 2020 rationale for the expansion of VMIC into a factory no longer held" (F3-S59, F3-S60, F7-S24).
- **503B as a shortage response.** The statutory door exists but the measured overlap is 18% of unique shortage-list APIs; the attributed cause is that shortage unpredictability makes the investment unattractive (F1-S65).
- **Hospital buffer-stock payment.** CMS solicited comment on paying all hospitals and did not adopt it; the finalised policy reaches only hospitals of 100 beds or fewer not in a chain, about 500 hospitals, under 10% of hospitals and under 5% of hospital pharmaceutical usage (F8-S16, F8-S17, F10-S13, F10-S16).
- **Legislative payment reform.** The Senate Finance discussion draft drew detailed objections (340B interaction, DEA quota collision, entry barriers) and, on the evidence collected, was not enacted; a 2025 National Academies workshop records that such proposals "have stalled or remain too limited in scope" (F10-S11, F10-S18, F10-S17, F10-S45).
- **Commitments erode in practice.** HHS records that "the erosion of failure-to-supply clauses in GPO contracts may be associated with drug shortages"; HealthTrust's own comparison implies 30-50% compliance in typical shortage programmes; under 15% of HealthTrust members' generic injectable purchases went through SIMS (F10-S10, F10-S09, F10-S15).
- **Onshoring instruments exclude the product class.** Proclamation 11020: "I have further determined not to adjust imports of generic pharmaceuticals" (F13-S09). DPA Title III: HHS made two investments in FY2018-2024 against DOD's 208 (F13-S07).

### 3.4 Execution and commissioning

- **Civica Petersburg.** Construction underway early 2021; "largely complete on the $140 million facility" by January 2023; "in the process of testing and regulatory approval" in August 2025; "* Site is pending FDA approval" on the 2025 fact sheet. More than 1,600 days with pre-committed hospital demand, $140M of capital and experienced leadership (F13-S41, F13-S27, F13-S28, F1-S57, F1-S58).
- **BioNTainer Kigali.** Containers arrived March 2023, validation batches planned for 2025, pilot production now expected 2026. Modular construction compressed the build; it did not compress qualification, training or licensing (F14-S36, F14-S37).
- **On Demand Pharmaceuticals.** Roughly 12,000 prefilled syringes over six months under 503A; no ODP or Tupelo entry on FDA's outsourcing-facility table as of 8/28/2026; no ANDA approval or DMF number found (F1-S40, F1-S43, F1-S16).
- **California declined to build.** HCAI's own report: "$100 million to $300 million initially with ongoing costs between $20 million to $50 million" annually, plus a technical-expertise gap and regulatory exposure, and the finding that "financial viability in the generic drug manufacturing space may require a facility to manufacture 20 to 40 products". The $50M earmarked for an in-state plant was eliminated for budget savings (F13-S18, F13-S19).
- **Texas.** The 2023 enabling chapter expired on its own terms on 1 September 2025; a 2025 reauthorization extends the sunset to 2031 and requires a business plan; no facility exists (F13-S20).
- **Manufacturing software, industry-wide.** Two decades of "paper on glass"; at Pfizer the fix was organizational (site partnership, templated MES-lite), not technical, after which yearly EBR deployments rose more than 50%, schedule accuracy went from 40% to over 80%, and per-site cost fell 80% (F2-S71).
- **Advanced manufacturing adoption generally.** GAO found few drugs made by advanced manufacturing and FDA without performance goals; stakeholders cited regulatory uncertainty as weakening the business case (F1-S18). The AMT designation programme had 18 requests and 3 grants as of 16 October 2025, with zero CDER grants (F14-S06).
- **Concentration events that define the hub risk.** Pfizer Rocky Mount, a July 2023 tornado: production areas undamaged, about 40,000 pallets lost from a warehouse, and all roughly 50 medicines placed on allocation; FDA's statement ends by saying redundancy of manufacturing locations is important (F6-S25, F4-S19). Baxter North Cove after Hurricane Helene (F4-S20).

## 5. Where prior art contradicts Telo's repository

Each item names the repository file and the external source that contradicts it.

1. **`regulatory/release-constraints.md` §1 contains a quotation that is not in the cited document.** The memo attributes to FDA's 2004 aseptic processing guidance the sentence "the sterility test is an essential element". The full guidance was downloaded and searched: "essential" occurs twelve times, three of them in section XI attaching to sterility-test media, deviation handling and the investigation of positives; the phrase "essential elements" occurs once, in section XII, about environmental and personnel monitoring data. Section XI.B says the opposite of what the paraphrase implies: "Sterility tests are limited in their ability to detect contamination because of the small sample size typically used" (F11-S01 versus F11-S41). This sentence sits in the memo's load-bearing paragraph and should be struck.
2. **The same file truncates CPG 490.200.** It quotes "It does not apply to products sterilized by filtration." The full sentence is "It does not apply to products sterilized by filtration, radiation, dry heat, or ethylene oxide", and the omission hides that the US and EU differ on radiation and dry heat (F11-S02).
3. **The same file §4a describes USP `<73>` as elevating ATP bioluminescence to a compendial standard.** USP's own FAQ says `<72>` and `<73>` became official in August 2025 "but are not applicable until they are referenced in a monograph", are treated as alternative methods, and need primary validation plus verification; the `<1071>` briefing is also removing "Release" from the chapter title (F11-S24, F11-S27).
4. **The same file treats the roughly seven-day environmental-monitoring wait as an unconditional floor.** Annex 1 §10.10 gives a narrow relief for short-shelf-life products, under which release proceeds on "a review of the most recent available data" (F11-S04).
5. **The same file never mentions the 503B Table D schedule**, which prices the aseptic-versus-terminal choice at 6 days against 14 days of at-risk beyond-use dating, and 28 days with a completed passing test. The memo's own recommended strategy is to sell dating rather than cycle time, so this is a gap in its central argument (F11-S45).
6. **The same file's "90-day assured BUD" is a QuVa company claim, not an FDA value**, and the 6-day table is Appendix B Table D, not "Appendix A.B". FDA's default with a completed passing sterility test is 28 days at room temperature unpreserved (F5-S20).
7. **The same file §3 cites "pharmacy on demand" as the closest ETP-accepted analogue to a microplant.** The listing sits under biological molecules for continuous bioprocesses, not small-molecule sterile injectables (F1-S05).
8. **The same file §2a expects Annex 22 to be finalised mid-2026.** It has not been; EMA reopened the model-class question in a workshop on 30 June and 1 July 2026 and is "still considering the implications" (F2-S58, F2-S59).
9. **The same file treats CAPS as a current 503B incumbent that quarantines everything.** CAPS has exited the 503B business (F4-S10).
10. **`research/market-research.md` describes Civica as "One large plant (~$124.5M for ~140M units/yr) plus a ~6-month stockpile".** The plant is $140M and 140,000 sq ft with more than 200 million units per year across three lines, it had not produced a commercial batch as of August 2025, and the mechanism that produces Civica's shortage effect is contracted CMO supply under partners' ANDAs plus the buffer, not the plant (F13-S41, F1-S39, F3-S31, F8-S14, F3-S25).
11. **The same file carries Phlow at $812M.** The USAspending record gives $696,665,953.98 obligated and $594,810,032.34 outlaid, 2020-05-18 to 2027-01-13; the contract document gives a base of $354,256,000; aggregators give a potential $969,754,682; Phlow describes a $1.2 billion 10-year contract. No fetched record gives $812M (F13-S29, F1-S25, F1-S30, F1-S34).
12. **The same file counts "~80 503B outsourcing facilities".** FDA's table had 97 rows on 8/28/2026 (F1-S16).
13. **The same file §4.1 says 503Bs "make RTU injectables manually".** Fagron's facility page describes syringe filling, labeling and semi-automated bag filling; Nephron states "we run a completely automated manufacturing, packaging, and distribution process" (F4-S13, F4-S16).
14. **The same file treats the Strategic API Reserve as a funded policy tailwind.** It is a line item in one BARDA contract plus bills that have not been enacted across four Congresses (F8-S05, F8-S06, F8-S07, F8-S08).
15. **The same file contradicts itself on the 44%-under-$5 statistic**, tagging it verified at one line and unverified at another and listing it as needing a join that is unnecessary. USP publishes it verbatim: "74% of sterile injectables in shortage have a price point below $15 per unit, with 44% less than $5 per unit", n=61 injectable drug products, per unit rather than per vial (F12-S14).
16. **The same file §5.3 treats a real-time AI quality-release engine as a narrow open vector.** The drift-monitoring half is a shipped product from Sartorius, Aizon and Basetwo, so the open part is narrower: a validated abstention mechanism and per-site validity, which are instruments rather than products (F2-S20, F2-S17, F2-S19).
17. **`regulatory/dme-rule.md` says the primary text has not been read.** It has now been read twice. Corrections: the rule says "capable of mobility", not "movable"; the 5-day registration clock runs from the first domestic DMU's first commercial manufacture; the relocation notice periods are 30 days domestic and 120 days foreign; and the preamble contemplates pooled DMU data reducing validation for subsequent units and relocations (F1-S01, F14-S02).
18. **`research/beachhead-verification.md` contradicts itself on pricing.** Its table records "$1.47 (Big4) / $4.52 (FSS)" and its analysis uses $4.52 as the FSS price, but its prose and checklist both call $1.47 an FSS price (F12-R02, per family 12 §10.3).

## 6. Unoccupied intervention points that survive prior art

Six survive. Each is stated with the evidence that it is unoccupied, what would falsify that, and the honest reason it is unoccupied, because in four of the six the reason is a barrier rather than an oversight.

**6.1 Process-route conversion as a shortage intervention: systematically screening shortage-prone aseptic injectables for terminal-sterilizability, and taking parametric release.**
Evidence unoccupied: no source found asks the question as a design variable; the sterilization route is not published per marketed presentation in any US or EU label reviewed, so no dataset exists to screen against (F11 §9, F12-S13 area); Telo's own `feasibility/docs/regulatory/approved_generic_cmo_map.md` records the route for sodium bicarbonate 8.4% as an unresolved question (F11-S44). What is occupied: the mechanism (since 1985, F11-S02), the chemistry for one candidate (Sun Pharma's autoclavable norepinephrine, F11-S32), and the finding that heat degrades some products or their containers (F11-S31).
Falsifier: an ANDA holder, CDMO or consultancy offering route conversion as a service; or a published mapping of shortage-list injectables to sterilization route.
Why it may be unoccupied: the work is per-product reformulation and container qualification with no platform effect, and FDA's QbR FAQ suggests parametric release may be a per-site and per-autoclave asset rather than a company-level one (F11-S46). [inference] **Of the interventions collected across the fourteen families, this is the only one that acts on the binding release constraint itself rather than on components that are not binding.** The scope of that statement is what these dossiers searched, not the literature as a whole; families 1, 11 and 14 each record their exhaustive negatives as [unverified].

**6.2 Product-archetype release-component decomposition used as a design variable, both for network architecture and for product selection.**
Evidence unoccupied: family 11 found no source assembling route, release-component vector and network design into one decision (F11-S01, F11-S02, F11-S04, F11-S05); family 12 found no index, patent or vendor method ranking candidate products by the decomposition of release time (F12-S13, F12-S15, F12-S20, F12-S21). The occupied adjacent space is shortage-risk scoring, which is a shipped product and patented (F12-S24, F12-S25, F12-S26).
Falsifier: a paper, consulting framework or regulatory document doing either.
Caveat: this is an analysis method, not a company. Its commercial value is as an input to 6.1.

**6.3 A maintained, regulator-pre-agreed switching package keeping alternate sources current for shortage-prone generics.**
Evidence unoccupied: the instrument exists in regulation (21 CFR 314.70(e), ICH Q12 PACMP) and FDA's guidance explicitly allows a location change to be proposed in a comparability protocol (F9-S37); FDA publishes no approved protocols and none was found for a generic sterile injectable (F9 §9 item 2, F3 §9); the federal analogue is a vaccine programme whose outcome is unknown (F7-S02, F13-S42).
Falsifier: a single published protocol naming alternate aseptic sites for a generic; an operating service maintaining inspection-ready alternates across ANDA holders.
Why it may be unoccupied: FDA's own carve-out caps the gain at CBE-30 rather than CBE-0 and conditions even that on the receiving site's CGMP compliance state at implementation, so the asset is worth 30 days plus predictability, not a step change (F9-S37).

**6.4 Campaign rotation across several prequalified sites as a network scheduling policy.**
Evidence unoccupied: family 7 searched BARDA, DLA, HERA, CEPI and the CMO literature and found only the single-site version, which BARDA states as one annual lot per product per site (F7-S02); the regulatory cadence that rotation would satisfy is documented (F7-S13).
Falsifier: a purchaser, CMO network or government programme scheduling rotation explicitly to hold multi-site validation.
Why it may be unoccupied: it requires a party with allocation rights over several independent owners' calendars, which is the same missing contracting vehicle as 6.6.

**6.5 A hub laboratory serving several 503B outsourcing facilities.**
Evidence unoccupied: regulatorily permitted (21 CFR 207.1 recognises control and contract laboratories; the 503B CGMP draft recommends ISO 17025 contract testing facilities); none of Quva, Fagron, SCA, Leiters or Nephron describes it, and each states on-site labs (F4-S11, F4-S13, F4-S14, Lreg-S10, Lreg-S5).
Falsifier: any 503B registrant whose release testing runs at an affiliated central lab.
Why the value is bounded: two of the named operators already run rapid sterility on site (ScanRDI validated to `<1223>` and `<71>` at Quva; SCA describes its Little Rock lab as "one of the first labs to fully qualify rapid sterility testing in the 503B industry"), so a hub lab pools cost without shortening the pole (F4-S11, F4-S14). No source read in any family ranks 503B operators by size, so no size claim is made here. PharMEDium is the documented warning about what one quality organization spanning several sterile sites does under stress (F4-S7, F4-S8).

**6.6 Manufacturer-side capacity coordination across independent owners.**
Evidence unoccupied: no commercial platform found; HDA reports the federal control tower has no two-way communication and no submission platform, and names the Sherman Act and DOJ/FTC monitoring as barriers; NASEM called federal monitoring "haphazard and inconsistent"; BARDA's own 2018 RFI names government visibility into CDMO capacity as an unsolved problem (F2-S63, F2-S64, F3-S19). The pre-release private attempts are federated-intelligence products still in development (F2-S32, F2-S26).
Falsifier: an operating platform with named CDMO participants sharing capacity data; or a legal opinion establishing the structure under which it is permissible.
Why it is unoccupied: it is a contracting and antitrust problem before it is a software problem, and the party that pays for shortage response today is the hospital or GPO, whose money already flows to Vizient, Premier and Bluesight (F2-S33 through F2-S39).

**What does not survive.** Owned distributed microplants (occupied in pilot and failed in every funded federal analogue), mobility as a response mechanism (family 14's own verdict: the qualification and release clock restarts after a move, so the earliest released sterile product is weeks, which is immaterial against multi-year shortages and slower than shipped inventory), the manufacturing OS in any of its eight sub-forms, warm standby as an owned asset, upstream diversification as a standalone intervention (orthogonal to both dominant documented shortage causes), and any AI or conformal layer positioned as removing the sterility hold, which family 11 records as already refuted rather than novel.

## 7. Open questions the sweep could not resolve

**Regulatory questions requiring a qualified reviewer or an agency meeting**

1. Whether an approved parametric release programme can extend to a second site or a second autoclave, and what reporting category that change carries. FDA's QbR FAQ ties the abbreviated route to identical facility, autoclave, container-closure, parameters and load patterns but does not address portability (F11-S46). If parametric release is a per-node asset, its cost scales with node count like replicated process validation.
2. Whether a comparability protocol has ever pre-authorized an alternate aseptic site or API supplier for a generic sterile injectable, and whether an uninspected alternate site can clear the facility evaluation (F9 §9 item 2; F3 §9).
3. Whether FDA would accept a multi-day hold of sterile-filtered aqueous bulk between a hub and a fill spoke, given the EMA 24-hour refiltration expectation (F5-S16, Lreg-S13, Lreg-S15).
4. Whether any approved ANDA or NDA for an aqueous small-molecule injectable lists bulk solution preparation and aseptic filling at different sites. Family 4 calls this its single most decision-relevant gap and it requires reading Module 3.2.P.3 tables that are not public.
5. Whether a spoke can operate with no resident quality-unit staff under a hub quality unit, and where the release decision legally occurs at a DMU (Lrepo-S2, Lreg-S6).
6. Whether the DME proposed rule's affiliation language excludes contract manufacturers on a line-by-line read, and whether it addresses parametric release or sterility-assurance ownership across DMUs. Both readings currently rest on secondary reads (F3-R4, F11 §9, F14-S02).
7. Whether FDA accepts HBEL/PDE carryover limits in lieu of the historical cleaning criteria; no FDA document adopting or rejecting the EMA approach was found (F6 §9).
8. The final Annex 22 model-class boundary, and the final status of FDA's AI draft guidance (F2-S58, F2-S76).
9. Four questions for counsel that gate any readiness-payment business model: anti-kickback treatment of a resilience premium; antitrust risk after the 2023 withdrawal of the joint-purchasing safety zone; whether 340B and Medicaid inflation-rebate mechanics permit a stated reliability premium; and whether an availability payment untied to ordered quantity is procurable under FAR Part 16 (F10 §9 item 11).
10. IRS position on whether drug-sale revenue serves a charitable purpose, which five nonprofit drug companies told GAO is an unresolved threat to their exempt status (F13-S22).
