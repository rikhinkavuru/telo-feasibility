## 1. Scope

Software vendors whose product is the system of record for a regulated manufacturer's quality processes: document and change control, deviations and CAPA, audits, complaints, training, supplier quality, and in some cases the electronic batch record itself. In scope: Veeva Quality Cloud, MasterControl, TrackWise Digital (Sparta Systems, owned by Honeywell), ETQ Reliance (now inside Octave Intelligence plc), Ideagen, ComplianceQuest, Dot Compliance, Qualio, Greenlight Guru, SimplerQMS, and the small vendors that sell compounding-specific batch records to 503B outsourcing facilities.

Out of scope and covered elsewhere: manufacturing execution systems sold as MES rather than as QMS (Koerber PAS-X, Rockwell PharmaSuite, Siemens Opcenter, Tulip, Apprentice); LIMS as a standalone category; shortage-data and regulatory-intelligence platforms (Redica, Vizient, GPO analytics). The boundary is not clean. MasterControl sells an eBR product inside a QMS company, and Siemens and Rockwell appear in the same analyst quadrant as Veeva. Where the boundary matters to a conclusion it is stated.

Why this category is in the design space at all. The assignment's Phase B family 2 lists as candidate Telo architectures a "documentation and electronic-batch-record wedge", "deviation/CAPA orchestration", "quality-system workflow automation", and "cross-site process intelligence without owning manufacturing". Those are descriptions of this category's existing products. The governing question here is therefore narrow and unfriendly:

1. Is the wedge occupied, by whom, at what scale, and at what price?
2. Does occupying it reduce sterile-injectable shortage risk, by a mechanism anyone has measured?
3. Is there any position left that is defensible and that acts on a failure mode the feasibility model says binds?

Absence of an idea from Telo's repository is not novelty. Vendor marketing that says "faster batch release" is not evidence of faster batch release.

Two structural readings of this table.

First, the category is consolidated and capitalized. Two 2022 transactions alone put about USD 2.5bn of enterprise value on two of these assets (ETQ at $1,200M, Ideagen at about GBP 1.09bn), Honeywell paid $1.3bn for Sparta in 2020, and Veeva's R&D and Quality segment alone carried $1,426.6M of FY2026 subscription revenue. A new entrant does not face a fragmented market.

Second, **[inference]** "batch record" means two different things inside this category. For Veeva, Ideagen, ETQ, ComplianceQuest, Dot Compliance, Qualio and SimplerQMS it means controlled documents and quality events around the record. For MasterControl and for the 503B-specific vendors it means executing the record on the floor. A wedge aimed at documentation is aimed at the first group's home turf. A wedge aimed at execution is aimed at MasterControl and at MES vendors outside this dossier.

## 4. Events 2023 to 2026 that changed this landscape

**2026-05-22 to 2026-05-28: ETQ left Hexagon.** Hexagon's annual general meeting resolved on 2026-04-24 to distribute all shares in Octave Intelligence plc to Hexagon's shareholders. Octave holds the Asset Lifecycle Intelligence, Safety Infrastructure and Geospatial, ETQ and Bricsys businesses and trades on Nasdaq New York as OCTV and on Nasdaq Stockholm as SDRs (LQBR-S12). The two primary sources give different completion dates. Hexagon's investor-relations page states that the distribution completed on 28 May 2026, with SDRs on Nasdaq Stockholm and Class B ordinary shares on Nasdaq New York (LQBR-S12). Octave's own release states that the distribution completed on 22 May, which was also the record date, that SDRs began trading on Nasdaq Stockholm on 25 May, and that Nasdaq New York trading began on the release date of 28 May (LQBR-S28). Read 22 May as the distribution and record date, 28 May as the first Nasdaq New York trading day. An earlier version of this dossier printed 2026-05-22 as the Nasdaq New York listing date, which neither source supports. ETQ is now a life-sciences-adjacent unit inside an asset-lifecycle software company rather than inside a metrology group. Its strategic commitment to pharma is less legible than it was.

**2026-01-20: Gartner published its first Magic Quadrant for QMS Software.** The vendor list includes AssurX, ComplianceQuest, Fabasoft, Hexagon (ETQ), Honeywell, Ideagen, MasterControl, Omnex Systems, QAD, Rockwell Automation (Plex), Siemens and Veeva Systems (LQBR-S22). Three vendors published Leader claims within the following eight weeks: ComplianceQuest on 2026-01-27 (LQBR-S13), ETQ on 2026-02-03 (LQBR-S29) and MasterControl on 2026-03-11 (LQBR-S30). An earlier version of this dossier said four vendors within six weeks; MasterControl's release falls in week seven and no dated Siemens release was obtained. An inaugural quadrant is a signal that the analyst firms consider the category mature enough to rank, and that buyers now have a shortlist mechanism that a new entrant is not on.

**2025-09-24 and 2026-02-03: FDA finalized, then superseded, the computer software assurance guidance.** The September 2025 final guidance sets out a risk-based approach to validating software used in production or the quality system (LQBR-S03). It was superseded by "Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality Management System Software", whose FDA guidance page is content-current as of 2026-02-03 and states in terms that it supersedes the September 24, 2025 guidance (LQBR-S31). Both are written for medical device production and the device quality system under 21 CFR Part 820. Neither is a drug CGMP guidance. A drug manufacturer validating a QMS still works from 21 CFR Part 11 and its own GAMP-style approach. Reading the CSA guidance as a general relaxation of validation burden for pharmaceutical quality software would be wrong.

**2024 through 2026: FDA built a quality-maturity assessment rather than a quality-software requirement.** CDER ran prototype QMM assessments in 2024 and 2025 and announced a third year on 2026-02-11 for up to nine establishments, with requests accepted through 2026-04-13 (LQBR-S01). The program's own stated rationale is the shortage link: a federal task force found that a root cause of many shortages is the absence of incentives to develop mature quality management systems (LQBR-S01). CDER's 2023 white paper on the program names five practice areas: Management Commitment to Quality, Business Continuity, Advanced Pharmaceutical Quality System, Technical Excellence, and Employee Engagement and Empowerment (LQBR-S32). The FDA program page cited above does not list them. The program does not score software, and no vendor in this dossier was found to sell a QMM-shaped evidence product.

Only that one letter is in the source table. The claim that FDA issued such letters to several outsourcing facilities in 2025, which stood here previously, is [unverified]. The aseptic-practice findings dominate the letter, but it also cites 21 CFR 211.188 for failure to prepare batch production and control records with complete information relating to the production and control of each batch, and 21 CFR 211.22(d) for failure to establish and follow written responsibilities and procedures applicable to the quality control unit, and FDA's corrective-action critique turns partly on draft SOPs for Change Control, Deviations and Risk Assessment being in use and on absent retrospective batch reviews and training records (LQBR-S02). Those are record-content and procedure-control failures, not failures of document search, and an eBR would not have prevented the first-air and gowning observations that drove the letter.

## 5. Unoccupied or weakly occupied intervention points

Each item states what the evidence supports, then what is inference.

**5.1 The category's shortage-reduction claim is not supported at the mechanism level for aseptically filled injectables.** MasterControl's own launch language promises "faster batch release times" from better batch records (LQBR-S08). The repository has already tested that mechanism for this product class: chemical and documentation acceleration removes approximately zero days from an aseptically filled injectable's hold because the 14-day USP <71> sterility incubation is the critical path and every chemical test finishes inside it (LQBR-S24). Even at zero-day sterility a roughly seven-day floor remains from environmental-monitoring plate incubation, because EM data are an essential element of the release decision (LQBR-S24). **[inference]** A documentation or deviation-triage product sold on release-time reduction is therefore selling a benefit that does not exist for the beachhead product class unless it is paired with a validated rapid sterility method and a rapid EM method. This is not an unoccupied point. It is a false point, and it is currently being sold by an incumbent. The defensible version of the claim is about QA review days, not about the release wall. The feasibility model's `qa_review_days` parameter is defined in `/Users/rikhinkavuru/telo/feasibility/config/global.yaml` at low 0, base 2 and high 4 days, evidence tier 5, `validation_status: illustrative`. That file is outside this dossier's citable in-repo list, so it is named by path rather than given a source id, and the values were read from it directly on 2026-09-03. The parameter is an illustrative bound, not a measurement, and the argument should not be pushed further than that.

**5.2 Calibrated abstention and published error rates for AI deviation triage.** Every AI feature found in this category is assistive: summarize, categorize, translate, draft, trend, predict (LQBR-S06, LQBR-S07, LQBR-S10, LQBR-S13, LQBR-S26). None publishes a measured error rate, a risk-coverage curve, a distribution-free guarantee, or a stated behaviour under distribution shift. **[inference]** The weakly occupied position is not "another QMS" and not "AI in the QMS". It is the statistical layer that lets an already-installed QMS state, defensibly, how often its triage is wrong and when it should abstain. Three cautions before treating this as an opportunity. First, the buyer for a statistical guarantee inside a quality workflow is a quality unit that has to defend it to an inspector, and no evidence was found that such a buyer exists today. Second, draft EU GMP Annex 22 (2025 consultation, not final as of 2026-09) is reported to permit only static, locked, deterministic models for critical applications, which would constrain the product to periodic revalidation rather than online learning. The repository file tags this `[V, secondary, 2026-07-26]`, states that it rests on trade-press and ECA summaries of the draft, and says to verify it against the final Annex 22 text on publication (LQBR-S24). It is not a settled regulatory constraint today, and the earlier version of this section overstated it as one. Third, the incumbents can add a calibration layer to their own agents without changing their distribution, which makes this a feature risk rather than a moat.

**5.3 Cross-firm quality telemetry tied to supply continuity.** Every product in this category is a single-tenant system of record for one firm. Vendor language about connecting suppliers and partners means giving one firm's suppliers access to that firm's tenant, not pooling across firms (LQBR-S05, LQBR-S10). FDA's QMM program is the clearest statement of demand for a cross-firm, purchaser-facing view of manufacturing reliability, and its stated goal includes minimizing risks to product availability (LQBR-S01). **[inference]** No vendor found sells the artifact QMM implies: a comparable, evidence-backed reliability signal that a hospital, GPO or government purchaser could contract on. This is the most genuinely open point in this dossier. It is also the hardest. The blocking constraints are data rights, competitive confidentiality, and the fact that the natural aggregator of cross-firm reliability data is a purchaser consortium or a regulator, not a software vendor. Anyone claiming this point has to answer who supplies the data and why.

**5.4 The small-manufacturer affordability gap is smaller than an OS-first thesis needs it to be.** SimplerQMS publishes a USD 17,500 per year floor covering 15 users and including validation, migration and training (LQBR-S14). Honeywell sells QuickTrack explicitly as enterprise QMS "without the complexity or cost" for startups and small and medium businesses (LQBR-S10). Veeva sells Quality Basics to emerging biotechs with no implementation or maintenance cost (LQBR-S05). Dot Compliance and Qualio both sell preconfigured systems into the same segment (LQBR-S15, LQBR-S16). **[inference]** The claim "small sterile manufacturers cannot get a validated quality system" is not supportable. What is genuinely thin is compounding-specific and sterile-specific execution: beyond-use-date calculation under USP <797> categories, environmental-monitoring excursion linked to in-process batch impact, media-fill and garbing qualification gating operator actions. Those functions are served by small vendors such as ConsortiEX rather than by the analyst-tracked incumbents (LQBR-S18). The counterweight is that the addressable base is probably small, but the size claim does not survive checking. That registered 503B outsourcing facilities number in the low hundreds is [unverified]: no facility count was obtained from FDA's registration list in this pass and nothing in the source table carries one, so it cannot be used as a premise. What is verified is that at least one of the largest 503Bs already runs an enterprise QMS (LQBR-S17, LQBR-S27). See section 6.

**5.5 A quality system shaped for a distributed manufacturing establishment.** FDA's July 2026 proposed rule conditions DME status on "a single quality unit" operating "a unified pharmaceutical quality system" across a hub and its distributed units, with the hub responsible for directing, monitoring and controlling manufacturing to ensure equivalence, as quoted in law-firm advisories (LQBR-S25). The repository file states that the primary Federal Register text has not been read because federalregister.gov blocks automated fetch, and tags everything in it `[V, secondary]`. The quoted phrases are the advisories' quotations of the rule, not the rule as read. No vendor in this dossier was found marketing a DME-configured quality system, unified batch-record model across units, or equivalence-monitoring product. **[inference]** This is unoccupied today, but it is unoccupied for the ordinary reason that the rule is proposed rather than final and no DME exists to sell to. It is a feature that Veeva or MasterControl can ship inside their existing multi-site models the day the first DME applicant needs it. Treating it as a company-scale opportunity would be a mistake; treating it as a reason to write the specification early, and to be the party that defines what equivalence monitoring across units looks like, is defensible.

**5.6 Nothing in this category acts on the failure modes the feasibility model says bind.** The Phase A bottleneck decomposition at `/Users/rikhinkavuru/telo/feasibility/docs/design_space/bottleneck_decomposition.md` names, among its ablation levers, `capacity_shortfall`, `surge_headroom`, `api_lead_time`, `component_lead_time`, `supplier_concentration`, `contract_insufficiency`, `fixed_quality_cost` and `commissioning_delay`. That file is outside this dossier's citable in-repo list, so it is named by path rather than given a source id; the lever names were read from it directly on 2026-09-03. This paragraph carries the dossier's central negative conclusion, so the provenance is stated rather than assumed. Quality and batch-record software acts on deviation and rejection rates, on documentation cycle time, and on the fixed quality cost line, and only weakly on the last of those since the license is itself a fixed cost. **[inference]** An OS-first Telo built on this category's product surface would be selling into a crowded market a product that moves none of the model's binding constraints. If an OS-first path is taken, its justification has to be data access and a wedge into the manufacturer relationship, not shortage-day reduction, and the shortage claim must not be made.
