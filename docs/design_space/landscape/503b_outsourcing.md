# Competitive landscape: 503B outsourcing facilities

Prepared 2026-09-02. Verified citation audit applied 2026-09-03. Category: `503b_outsourcing`. Author: landscape researcher, adversarial design-space review.

## 1. Scope

FDA-registered human drug compounding outsourcing facilities under section 503B of the FD&C Act (21 U.S.C. 353b), the firms that own them, and the statutory and guidance machinery that sets what they may make and how long the product lasts. In scope: the registered-facility population as FDA publishes it, the large multi-site operators (QuVa, SCA Pharmaceuticals, Fagron Sterile Services and AnazaoHealth, Leiters Health, Nephron Sterile Compounding Center, Central Admixture Pharmacy Services, STAQ Pharma), the firms that have left (PharMEDium, Fresenius Kabi, CAPS), the 503B bulks list, the essentially-a-copy restriction, the beyond-use-date and release-testing enforcement policy, and the effect of the July 2026 distributed manufacturing establishment (DME) proposed rule on this class.

Out of scope, each having its own dossier: nonprofit and public-benefit manufacturers, approved-generic sterile injectable manufacturers and CDMOs, compounding robotics vendors, and federal onshoring programs. Mark Cuban Cost Plus and Civica appear here only where their 503B registrations bear on 503B occupancy.

Purpose. Telo's repository treats 503B as a "revenue bridge and inspection-experience generator" that "can never be the convoy" (`regulatory/dme-rule.md`), and separately identifies a "503B trap" in which the market leaders can release before sterility completes and choose not to (`regulatory/release-constraints.md`). This dossier tests both claims against primary sources, records what the sector actually is in 2026, and identifies which intervention points inside and adjacent to 503B are unoccupied. Absence of an idea from Telo's repository is not novelty.

Limits. 503B firms are almost all private. Revenue figures for QuVa, SCA, Leiters, STAQ, and Nephron are not audited public data and are excluded or labeled. Fagron is the one listed operator and the only source of audited numbers. Its IFRS operating segments are geographic (Fagron EMEA, Fagron North America - Pacific, Fagron Latin America); Note 6 Revenue then disaggregates revenue by activity category (Essentials, Brands, Compounding Services) inside each segment. There is no US-503B-only revenue line, and the Compounding Services category does not separate sterile 503B output from 503A and non-sterile compounding. FDA's registered-facility table is the population frame used throughout; it has visible data-entry errors (one Florida facility is coded CA, one New Mexico town is coded FL, one row has no city or state), so counts derived from its geography column are approximate.

Source count. 34 web sources and 4 in-repo files. That exceeds the 25 suggested for this category. The overage is in primary government documents: the release-testing and BUD policy, the bulks list, the copy-rule guidance, the DME rule, and the fee notices are each separately load-bearing for a different conclusion, and compressing them would make the conclusions uncheckable.

Verification notes.

L503B-S01 was fetched as raw HTML and the table body parsed programmatically (97 `<tr>` rows in `<tbody>`). Every count in sections 3 and 4 attributed to S01 comes from that parse, not from a summarizer. An automated summary of the same page returned "approximately 130+" facilities, which is wrong; a verifier should parse the table rather than read a summary of it.

L503B-S06 was checked for the strings "503B", "503b", and "503" case-insensitively. The document contains the token "503" once, in a context unrelated to compounding, and contains no occurrence of "503B" or "outsourcing facility". This is the primary-text confirmation of the secondary claim recorded in `regulatory/dme-rule.md` (L503B-R03).

L503B-S19 is a three-column PDF. Quoted sentences read correctly in column order but are interleaved in a naive text extraction. A verifier should read the rendered page, not a flat text dump. Two specific traps in this document. First, the EUR 424.8m Compounding Services figure is in Note 6 Revenue in the consolidated financial statements (page 225), where it is printed in thousands with a comma as `424,805`; a text search for "424.8" will not find it there and will instead land on page 288, where the same rounded number appears as an unrelated EU Taxonomy eligible-turnover line in the Sustainability Statement. Second, the Key Figures page (page 3) shows revenue per activity category as an unlabelled pie (44.6%, 40.1%, 15.3%) above a separate legend, so a flat extraction cannot pair percentage to label. Note 6 settles it arithmetically: Compounding Services 424,805 of 952,169 is 44.6%, Essentials 381,353 is 40.1%, Brands 146,012 is 15.3%.

L503B-S28 is quoted verbatim including the source's own typo, "bringing and estimated 300 jobs". The wording is the publisher's, not a transcription error here.

Audit note, 2026-09-03. An independent verifier re-fetched every source in this table. Quva acquires BrightStream Health.** The largest 503B by hospital count bought a predictive-analytics and machine-learning software company and now sells shortage-management, inventory, and diversion-surveillance software alongside compounded product. ## 5. Unoccupied or weakly occupied intervention points

Each item states what the evidence supports, then what is inference.

**5.1 Nothing in this class produces evidence about product identity or potency at release.** [inference] The failure modes named in every 2024 to 2026 letter read here are aseptic-process failures: environmental monitoring in ISO 5 areas, cleaning and disinfection, aseptic process validation, investigation of discrepancies, and component identity testing. The Leiters letter shows FDA rejecting a non-viable-particulate probe-placement justification based on "a single processing run", and separately citing bevacizumab repackaging on an unvalidated process. None of these is a chemical-assay problem. This is direct evidence for the repository's release-constraints conclusion, and it makes the point sharper than the repository states it: a chemical release model is orthogonal not only to the sterility clock but to the entire observed enforcement surface of this class. (L503B-S10, L503B-S11, L503B-S14)

**5.2 Aseptic-process evidence generation is weakly occupied and is what FDA is actually asking for.** [inference] FDA's rejection language in the Leiters letter is about insufficiency of evidence, not absence of controls: one processing run does not demonstrate "consistent performance over time or under varying operational conditions in other 'critical operating zones'". A product that produces defensible, statistically framed, site-conditional evidence of environmental control across zones and over time addresses the observation FDA actually writes. No entity in this dossier sells that. Quva sells shortage and inventory analytics, not aseptic-assurance evidence. This is the nearest defensible adjacency between Telo's measured conformal work and this market, and it is a quality-evidence product, not a release-automation product. (L503B-S10, L503B-S16)

**5.3 Capacity activation latency is unmeasured and unmanaged.** Leiters bought a 110,000 sq ft sterile plant in February 2022, registered it in July 2022, and it is still "Not yet inspected" 4.2 years later. The purchase announcement said the plant "will require further modifications to meet aseptic manufacturing FDA regulations and guidance", so this measures the cost of converting a non-aseptic pharmaceutical plant and qualifying it, not the cost of restarting an idle sterile line. That makes it the right comparator for a Telo node built from scratch, and it does not support a claim that Leiters is sitting on idle capacity. Fifteen registered addresses have gone more than two years without an inspection, which under FDA's stated practice implies they have not confirmed production and distribution. [inference] Nobody in the class sells or measures time-to-qualified-output, yet that latency is the quantity that determines whether reserved or standby capacity can answer a shortage at all. A Telo model that treats a node as available on commissioning is contradicted by the best-funded attempt in the sector. (L503B-S01, L503B-S21)

**5.4 The registry is not a capacity census, and no public instrument distinguishes the two.** 97 registered addresses, 41 never inspected, at least two belonging to a firm publicly winding down. FDA's own fee projection is 87. Product reports exist twice yearly per facility with unit counts, but the only published analysis of them against the shortage list is six years old. [inference] A maintained, current mapping of "who is actually shipping what, at what volume, against which shortage" is unoccupied. The Outsourcing Facilities Association publishes a per-firm shortage-product list, says the content comes from "the publicly available FDA website and required 6-month product reports published by the FDA", and disclaims both quantity and nature ("OFA makes NO representation to the quantity or nature of any product listed below") and pricing. So the closest thing to a current mapping is a trade-association list that will not say how much of anything is made. This is a data product, not a manufacturing product, and it is the kind of thing a GPO or a payer might fund. One dependency is [unverified]: I did not locate the FDA page that publishes the underlying product reports, or confirm the file format, so bulk access to them is an assumption rather than a checked fact. (L503B-S01, L503B-S23, L503B-S25, L503B-S34)

**5.5 Health-system ownership of sterile capacity is occupied but shallow.** STAQ Pharma is owned by health systems and is the only large operator in the table whose most recent Denver inspection produced no 483. Leiters carries health-system investors, including Kaiser Permanente Ventures, Mayo Clinic and UNC Health, alongside a private-equity majority holder. [inference] The model works and is not saturated, but it is occupied by incumbents with two plants each, and a new entrant would compete for the same consortium capital. Telo would be the fourth or fifth such vehicle, not the first. (L503B-S01, L503B-S22, L503B-S28, L503B-S32)

**5.6 The 503B trap is confirmed and is worse than the repository states.** The repository frames it as leaders declining to use at-risk release because a 6-day BUD is worse than a 90-day one. The primary text adds a constraint the repository does not record: the default-BUD table applies only to aggregate batch sizes of 1,000 units or fewer. [inference] A high-volume node therefore cannot use the default-BUD policy at all and must generate product-specific stability data, so the "convert a 6-day at-risk BUD into a long assured BUD" proposition does not apply to the batch sizes at which a shortage-relevant node would need to run. That does not kill the shelf-life framing, but it relocates it: the value is in stability and container-closure evidence generation, not in a release decision. (L503B-S04, L503B-R01)

**5.7 The DME pathway is closed to 503B, and this is now confirmed from the primary text.** The proposed rule requires an approved application and a preapproval inspection and excludes CMO-operated units. The words "503B" and "outsourcing facility" do not appear in it. [inference] Two consequences for Telo. First, the repository's conclusion that "the 503B route can be a revenue bridge but it can never be the convoy" is correct and can now be sourced to the rule itself rather than to law-firm advisories. Second, the absence is itself a comment-worthy gap: FDA has proposed a decentralized-manufacturing registration for approved products and has left the one existing class of legally decentralized sterile production entirely outside it. Nothing in the sources read here shows anyone raising this on the docket. Comments close 2026-09-11. (L503B-S06, L503B-R03)

**5.8 What is decisively occupied, and should not be re-entered.** RTA sterile injectable supply to hospitals under GPO contracts is occupied by Quva, SCA, Fagron, Leiters, and STAQ, at least three of which have multi-site footprints and national distribution. Forward distribution for compounded sterile product is occupied by Quva. Hospital pharmacy shortage analytics is occupied by Quva BrightStream. Shortage-driven bulk compounding as a durable line is foreclosed by the copy rule and by FDA's reading of "clinical need". A Telo entry at any of these points would be a late, sub-scale entry against firms whose capex on a single expansion exceeds any plausible Telo Phase 2 budget. (L503B-S16, L503B-S17, L503B-S19, L503B-S03, L503B-S07)
