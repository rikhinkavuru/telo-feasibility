## 1. Scope

Organisations whose product is information about drug supply and drug shortage risk, rather than drug substance. In scope: the two national shortage registries (FDA CDER Drug Shortage Database, ASHP / University of Utah Drug Information Service); the risk-modelling platforms (USP Medicine Supply Map, FDA's internal analytics); the GPO programmes that pair shortage information with committed volume or dedicated inventory (Vizient, Premier); the general-purpose supply-chain-risk vendors that sell into pharma and government (Resilinc, Exiger, Everstream); the hospital-side pharmacy software that carries shortage signals into a purchasing workflow (Bluesight, LogicStream, Trulla/SpendMend, Wolters Kluwer, GHX/Lumere); and the transaction-data licensors underneath all of it (IQVIA, Symphony Health). CMS is included because it is the only party in this category that pays anyone to hold inventory.

The governing questions are narrow. For each entity: what data does it actually hold, who pays for it, is a price public, and which intervention point does it already occupy. Marketing language about preventing shortages is not evidence of occupancy. Absence of an idea from Telo's repository is not novelty.

## 4. Events 2023 to 2026 that changed the landscape

1. **Q1 2024, shortage counts peak on the ASHP series and diverge from FDA's.** ASHP and Utah DIS recorded 323 active shortages in Q1 2024, the highest since tracking began in 2001 (LSD-S8). Vizient reproduces the same datum, "reached a record high in the first quarter of 2024, with 323 active shortages, surpassing the previous peak of 320 in 2014" (LSD-S11B). The heading previously read 2023, which did not match the quarter of the datum beneath it. GAO recorded 102 shortages on FDA's list as of 2024-07-31 (LSD-S2). Both numbers are correct on their own definitions. Telo's own market research quotes the 323 figure without stating that it is the ASHP count, which overstates the FDA-defined problem by roughly threefold (LSD-S24). Correct this before external use.
2. **2024-07-25, QuVa Pharma acquires LogicStream Health.** A 503B outsourcing facility bought a shortage-prediction platform outright, financed with Blue Owl debt, and stated it would enrich the model with its own purchasing data (LSD-S15). Shortage prediction moved from a standalone product to a feature attached to supply.

## 5. Unoccupied or weakly occupied intervention points

Each of these is my judgement from the evidence above, marked as an inference. Each is stated so it can be falsified.

**5.1 IP11, a verified registry of activatable qualified capacity, is unoccupied. [inference]** Every platform in section 3 maps risk or origin. None publishes, for a given product, which other facilities could make it, whether the process is already transferred, whether the site is registered and inspected for that dosage form, and what the elapsed time to first releasable batch would be. Exiger maps ingredient origin and formulation sites (LSD-S18); USP maps FDF, API and KSM sites (LSD-S4); Resilinc maps part-to-site (LSD-S19). All of these answer "where is it made now". None answers "who could make it by when". Falsification: find any commercial or government product that publishes a per-product time-to-first-qualified-batch for alternative sites. Note the counterweight: this registry is only valuable if someone will pay for activation, and section 3 shows the only parties who pay for activation are the two GPOs.

**5.2 IP8, allocation during an active shortage, is weakly occupied. [inference]** Vizient's Advanced Access Hub takes urgent inventory requests when no other supply exists (LSD-S9), which is a queue, not an allocation rule. USP has published on protective purchasing as an amplifier of shortages (LSD-S4). Nobody sells a defensible, auditable allocation mechanism across buyers. This is a mechanism-design problem rather than a technology problem, and it sits inside the GPO channel, so an outside entrant would need a GPO partner rather than a product.

**5.3 IP10, payer-funded inventory, is under-built relative to the authority that exists. [inference]** CMS already pays small independent hospitals for the cost of establishing and maintaining a 6-month buffer stock, explicitly permitting the stock to be held through a manufacturer, distributor or intermediary (LSD-S3). I found no vendor selling buffer-stock-as-a-service against this payment, and no vendor selling the cost-report documentation that the payment requires. The eligible population is narrow and the payment excludes medicines already in shortage, so the absolute market is small. It is nonetheless a real contracted revenue mechanism, and it is absent from Telo's model.

**5.4 IP12, independent prospective evaluation of shortage prediction, is unoccupied. [inference]** Every accuracy claim in this landscape is self-reported by the vendor whose product is being evaluated: USP's 98 percent and 100 percent figures use a 40 percent risk threshold that USP itself chose (LSD-S5); Bluesight's 42-day average lead time is a company statement (LSD-S14). The published academic work reports conventional retrospective metrics on a single split (LSD-S23). There is no pre-registered, third-party, prospective benchmark with calibration and risk-coverage reporting. This is a credible research contribution and it is directly adjacent to the conformal selective-prediction work Telo already has. It is a paper, not a company. Do not confuse the two.

**5.5 The demand-side data gap is structural and no platform can close it. [inference from a direct source statement]** GAO records that manufacturers must report an interruption in production caused by increased demand, but are not required to report an increase in demand that does not interrupt production, and that FDA is asking Congress for that authority (LSD-S2). Increased demand contributed to 65 percent of shortages on FDA's list as of 2024-07-31 (LSD-S2). So the dominant reported driver of shortages is precisely the category that is not reportable. Any prediction platform, USP's and FDA's included, is inferring this from proxies. The implication for Telo is negative rather than positive: an information product cannot fix a missing statutory reporting duty, and better sensors inside a Telo plant do not generate national demand data.

**5.6 What is definitively occupied, and should not be attempted.** Shortage detection and notification (FDA and ASHP; Utah DIS holds a contract with Vizient to provide shortage information, LSD-S8, and the repo records that a paid data licence exists, LSD-S25. The earlier phrasing, that ASHP's data is "already licensed to Vizient", asserted a licence and a payment direction that LSD-S8 does not state). Predictive shortage risk scoring (USP commercially, FDA internally, Bluesight and LogicStream from the hospital side). Upstream site and ingredient mapping (Exiger under a $919M federal vehicle, USP, Resilinc). Essential and vulnerable medicine list curation (FDA and USP, LSD-S5 and LSD-S18; Vizient's Essential Medications List, LSD-S9 and LSD-S11B. WHO's Essential Medicines List is named only inside LSD-S5's text, no WHO source was fetched, and WHO has been dropped from this list). Committed-volume contracting (Premier ProvideGx, Vizient Novaplus Enhanced Supply). Procurement execution (Trulla, GHX). A Telo product at any of these points enters against a funded, distributed incumbent with better data.

**5.7 The decision-relevant consequence for the design-space review. [inference]** The evidence in this category argues against an information-first or OS-first wedge aimed at shortage prediction. Detection improved measurably over the same window in which annualised hospital labour for managing shortages rose from 8.6M hours and $359M to 20.2M hours and $894M, which Vizient frames as a 2019 to 2024 change (LSD-S11B). These four figures were previously cited to LSD-S11, which does not contain them. That pairing is the strongest available evidence that the binding constraint is response capacity and contracted supply, not signal quality. It also argues that the two GPOs, not hospitals, are the counterparty that matters, because they own both the demand aggregation and the shortage data channel.
