## 1. Scope

Entities that make, or arrange the making of, prescription drugs for the U.S. market under a nonprofit, public-benefit, member-owned, or state-branded mandate rather than a shareholder-return mandate, where the stated purpose includes reducing shortage risk or price. In scope: Civica (Civica, Inc., Civica Foundation, CivicaScript), Phlow Corp., USAntibiotics, Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drug Company (MCCPDC), Premier ProvideGx, Vizient Novaplus and Novaplus Enhanced Supply, CalRx.

Two of these are not nonprofits and are included because they occupy the same intervention points. MCCPDC is a public-benefit corporation, registered in federal award data as "MARK CUBAN COST PLUS DRUG COMPANY, PBC" (LNPM-S26). No source fetched for this dossier states its state of incorporation. ProvideGx and Novaplus are for-profit GPO programs that manufacture nothing and instead buy shortage risk down with committed volume. Phlow is a for-profit CDMO whose funding is federal; it is included because it is the API and key-starting-material half of the same Virginia system Civica sits in.

The governing question for this dossier is narrow. For each entity: what does it actually own and operate, what does it contract out, what is the verifiable scale, and which intervention point does it already occupy. Marketing language about solving shortages is not evidence of occupancy. Absence of an idea from Telo's repository is not novelty.

Out of scope: 503B outsourcing facilities as a class, CDMOs as a class, and federal onshoring programs as programs. Each has its own dossier. MCCPDC's 503B facility and Phlow's ASPR contract appear here only insofar as they define these entities' occupancy.

Limits of this dossier. Nonprofit financials are available only through Form 990, which lags 12 to 18 months. USAntibiotics, MCCPDC, and Phlow file no public financials; their figures below are self-reported, press-reported, or drawn from federal award records. Two program-scale numbers used by vendors (ProvideGx "15 drug shortages" delisted, NES Reserve "131 million units") are unaudited vendor claims and are labeled as such wherever they appear.

Source count. 27 web sources and 2 in-repo files, above the 25 suggested for this category. The overage is deliberate: two federal award records located late in the pass reverse a conclusion in section 5, and dropping corroborating sources to hit a count would have made the reversal less checkable.

In the second pass only the highlights, contents, and opening letter pages were returned, so two Civica operational facts were held out as unverified and listed in section 6 as an open item. In this pass the full report text was retrieved and those pages were read. These are now used in sections 3, 4, and 5, and the open item is closed. "Has begun operating" is not a statement that commercial product has shipped, and it is not treated as one here.

Searching federal award data for "Civica" returns only CivicaActions, Inc., an unrelated government-IT contractor. Re-running the documented query on 2026-09-03 returned 36 CivicaActions prime awards and no Civica, Inc. prime award. It is not Civica, Inc. Any automated scan of this category has to exclude it.

## 4. Events 2023 to 2026 that changed this landscape

- May 2023. Astral SteriTech recalled all in-date sterile antibiotic powders from its Vadodara plant, including six Civica-labeled presentations across three products under labeler code 72572 (ampicillin-sulbactam 1.5g and 3g, cefepime 1g and 2g, piperacillin-tazobactam 3.375g and 4.5g), because "the sterility of products manufactured at Astral's facility cannot be assured" (LNPM-S22). Direct evidence that a contract-manufacturing nonprofit inherits its CMOs' quality risk under its own NDC.
- 2023. First peer-reviewed evidence of Civica's effect: 96 percent versus 86 percent order fulfillment at one member system, with the realized cost advantage shrinking to 2.7 percent once that hospital optimized its wholesaler buying across 62 non-Civica manufacturers (LNPM-S02). The access claim survives. The cost claim largely does not.
- July 2024. ASPE published an independent volume and price analysis: 64 generic sterile injectables, 350.2 million units, $255.9 million of sales over 2020-2022, with Civica's price up to 2.15 times the traditional-model price for certain drugs (LNPM-S03).
- July to September 2024. USAntibiotics, the sole U.S. amoxicillin maker, stated it would close within 18 months without federal purchase commitments, and that breaking even required either doubling commercial sales or acquiring the U.S. government as a customer (LNPM-S14, LNPM-S27).
- August to September 2024. FDA inspected MCCPDC's Dallas 503B facility over nine days and issued a seven-observation Form 483 (LNPM-S15).
- September 2024. ProvideGx signed Long Grove for norepinephrine premix IV bags, explicitly to add safety stock and a second source in a five-year availability problem (LNPM-S24).
- May 2025. GAO reported on nonprofit drug companies. It "identified seven nonprofit drug companies that met our definition as of July 2024", three of them with drugs on the market. Six described difficulty obtaining enough funding for drug development. Five expressed concern about maintaining tax-exempt status when using drug-sale revenue to support operations, and one of those five specifically framed that revenue as potentially unrelated business taxable income; IRS has taken no position and has set no threshold (LNPM-S01). GAO makes no claim that the report is the first of its kind, and none is made here. Also May 2025: Civica confirmed the Petersburg plant is operational but has not begun commercial manufacture, and that its roughly 80 medicines still come from contract manufacturers (LNPM-S07).
- June 2025. Angels for Change awarded a Project PROTECT grant to MCCPDC's 503B facility to pre-fund carboplatin and methotrexate (LNPM-S23). Philanthropy, not payers, funding pre-shortage production.
- June to December 2025. The U.S. Army funded MCCPDC $205,920 for proof-of-concept cGMP production of sterile saline and sterile water from nonsterile source water (LNPM-S26). This is a defense-funded test of point-of-need sterile drug-substance manufacture at a 503B.
- 2025, month unknown. Phlow secured a $37M private round, part of more than $90M raised privately, while still not profitable. The source, published May 2026, says only that the raise "was secured last year" and gives no month (LNPM-S09).
- August 2025. Executive Order 14336 ordered ASPR to fill the SAPIR, stating that "the SAPIR is nearly empty" after five years and roughly $700M obligated to Phlow, and directed a list of approximately 26 critical drugs plus a six-month API supply (LNPM-S12, LNPM-S10, LNPM-S26).
- December 2025. FDA approved USAntibiotics' Augmentin XR in two months under the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot, the program's first approval, explicitly on domestic-manufacturing grounds (LNPM-S13). A fast, non-monetary regulatory reward for domestic capacity now exists.
- January 2026. CalRx-branded insulin glargine went on sale, manufactured by Biocon, distributed by Civica, not made in Petersburg (LNPM-S05, LNPM-S20).
- April 2026. The U.S. Army awarded MCCPDC $1,393,259.04 through April 2028 to generate a CMC data package suitable for an FDA ANDA (LNPM-S26). The federal government began paying to convert a 503B's shortage-gated capability into an approved application, which is the exact barrier MCCPDC named publicly.
- May 2026. ASPR placed a $29,998,678 firm-fixed-price, twelve-month order with USAntibiotics for amoxicillin capsules, one offer received (LNPM-S26). The federal purchase that the company said in 2024 it needed to survive.
- May 2026. Phlow replaced its founding CEO with Tim Mayleben and confirmed it is not yet profitable (LNPM-S09).
- July 2026. The Alliance for Building Better Medicine won $15.9M from EDA for an end-to-end Occam Systems (KSM) to Phlow (API) to Civica (finished dose) project covering ketamine, midazolam, norepinephrine, and succinylcholine (LNPM-S08). Norepinephrine, the molecule Telo's own beachhead verification failed on, is now the target of a federally funded, vertically integrated domestic consortium.
- August 2026. KFF reported CalRx insulin uptake as limited, about 120,000 packs in seven months, with an academic assessment that the state-label effort is "largely symbolic at this time" (LNPM-S21).

Net effect over the period. The category consolidated around contracting, inventory, and federal purchasing rather than around new owned plants. The one new plant in it, Petersburg, had not begun commercial manufacture as of the last confirmed public statement in May 2025, and no later FDA approval or inspection outcome for the site was located (LNPM-S07). The one state that budgeted for a plant cancelled that line. The two entities that got materially stronger did so by acquiring a government buyer (USAntibiotics) or a government development contract (MCCPDC), not by adding capacity on spec.

## 5. Unoccupied or weakly occupied intervention points

Evidence first, inference second. Nothing here is a recommendation. Two of these points were assessed differently in the first pass and are marked as revised.

**1. Availability payments for standby sterile capacity. Weakly occupied, and the evidence is now stronger than in the first pass.** Every funding mechanism visible in this category prices units or reimburses cost. Civica sells against minimum-volume commitments (LNPM-S03). ProvideGx and NES Reserve pay for produced inventory (LNPM-S18, LNPM-S19). Phlow is cost-plus-fixed-fee R&D (LNPM-S11). The decisive case is USAntibiotics. It asked explicitly not for a subsidy but for purchase, and what it eventually received was a firm-fixed-price order for a stated quantity of capsules covering twelve months (LNPM-S26, LNPM-S27). That order was placed against a standing VA Federal Supply Schedule vehicle, so further orders need a requisition rather than a new procurement (LNPM-S26). [inference] No buyer in this category has been shown to pay for readiness separately from units, including the buyer that stepped in to save the only domestic source of the most-prescribed antibiotic. The schedule vehicle blunts the sharpest version of the point. A twelve-month unit order cannot by itself underwrite a facility with a twenty-year life, but the vehicle makes repeating the order cheap, so the binding constraint is stockpile appropriation and requisition rather than contracting friction. What still has not been shown anywhere in this category is a payment for standing ready that is separable from units shipped. Any architecture whose revenue depends on being paid to stand ready has to name the first buyer who has ever done that, and the honest answer from this evidence base is that none of these did.

**2. Pre-shortage production of drugs not currently on the shortage list. REVISED: now partially occupied, by the Department of Defense.** MCCPDC states it may make ifosfamide only while the drug is officially in shortage, and that "nearly $360,000 in FDA filing fees" blocks moving to an approved application (LNPM-S17). [inference] The source does not name the fee schedule; reading it as the ANDA program fee is this dossier's inference. In the first pass the only party funding pre-shortage production was a patient-advocacy nonprofit with grants (LNPM-S23). That is no longer the whole picture. In April 2026 the Army contracted MCCPDC through 2028 to generate a CMC data package suitable for an ANDA (LNPM-S26). [inference] The prevention window is no longer structurally unoccupied. The federal government has started buying its way past the exact barrier the incumbent named, at the incumbent, on a two-year clock. An intervention claiming to prevent rather than mitigate now has to differentiate against a funded program, not against an empty space.

**3. Independent, contestable verification of supply-reliability claims. Weakly occupied.** There are two published third-party evaluations of any entity in this category, both about Civica, and they partly disagree: NEJM Catalyst finds a real access gain and a nearly eliminated price gain (LNPM-S02), ASPE finds volumes comparable to traditional competitors and some prices up to 2.15 times higher (LNPM-S03). Everything else is vendor-reported. ProvideGx's "15 drug shortages" delisted appears word for word in the January 2024 and September 2024 releases with no change (LNPM-S18, LNPM-S24). NES Reserve's "131 million units" and "$538 million of inventory cost avoidance" carry no methodology and no external audit (LNPM-S19). [inference] An identical count across eight months of press releases is a marketing constant, not a tracked metric. Measured, per-NDC, per-buyer reliability accounting is weakly occupied, it is a data product rather than a manufacturing product, and it is the one place where the incumbents' own claims are least defensible.

**4. Quality risk transfer in the contract-manufacturing layer. Weakly occupied.** Civica puts its own NDC on other firms' output and inherits their failures, demonstrated across six Civica-labeled presentations in three products in the Astral recall (LNPM-S22). The exposure is structural rather than incidental: GAO records 14 manufacturer partners producing 77 drugs under the Civica label (LNPM-S01). Civica's answer has been to build its own plant, which as of the last confirmed public statement had not begun commercial manufacture (LNPM-S04, LNPM-S07). [inference] Supplier quality surveillance and rapid qualified-alternate switching for label-holders is weakly occupied and is a far cheaper response to CMO-inherited risk than vertical integration. This is closer to the OS-only family in the repo's architecture taxonomy than to a plant.

**5. Low-volume, changeover-heavy products. Weakly occupied on economics.** Civica's members participate in choosing what it makes, and the set is concentrated in high-volume hospital staples. Civica states its pipeline medications "are prioritized for production by our members" (LNPM-S04), and GAO reports that "hospital system members participate in the selection of drugs for Civica to manufacture" (LNPM-S01). Neither source describes a vote. ProvideGx and NES target the same essential-medicine lists (LNPM-S18, LNPM-S19). MCCPDC is the only actor claiming rapid product pivoting, at 1 to 2 million doses per year, and its oncology expansion is philanthropically funded (LNPM-S16, LNPM-S23). [inference] Low-volume oncology and pediatric presentations are weakly occupied because nobody can make the fixed quality overhead work there, and the entity nearest to occupying them needs grants to do it. A design requiring those products to carry per-site fixed quality cost should be treated as unfunded until a payer is named.

**6. Point-of-need sterile production of simple drug substances. Newly and thinly occupied.** The Army paid MCCPDC to demonstrate cGMP production of sterile saline and sterile water from nonsterile source water (LNPM-S26). [inference] This is the closest funded work in this category to a distributed sterile-manufacturing thesis, and it is instructive that the customer is DoD rather than a hospital or a GPO, that the products are the two simplest possible sterile items, and that the award was $205,920 for six months. The demand for point-of-need sterile manufacture that exists today is a defense logistics demand at proof-of-concept scale, not a civilian shortage demand at commercial scale.

**7. The nonprofit wrapper is not a free advantage.** Five of seven nonprofit drug companies told GAO they were concerned about maintaining tax-exempt status when using drug-sale revenue to support operations, and one of those five specifically framed that revenue as potentially unrelated business taxable income. IRS has taken no position on whether selling lower-priced drugs serves a charitable purpose, and has "no set percentage or threshold amount of unrelated business taxable income that would result in the loss of an organization's tax-exempt status" (LNPM-S01). [inference] The tax question scales with commercial success, so the wrapper is most fragile exactly when the company works. MCCPDC's public-benefit corporation and Phlow's for-profit-with-federal-contracts both avoid it.

**8. Firmly occupied. Do not re-enter without a distinguishing mechanism.** Hospital-owned committed-volume purchasing (Civica). GPO demand aggregation with safety-stock and failure-to-supply terms (ProvideGx, NES Reserve). Six-month buffer inventory (Civica, NES Reserve). Domestic API and KSM for essential medicines (Phlow, sole source, potential end 2030). State-branded low-cost labeling (CalRx). GPO co-investment in domestic ANDAs (Premier and Exela). End-to-end KSM to finished dose for ketamine, midazolam, norepinephrine, succinylcholine in Virginia (Occam, Phlow, Civica, funded July 2026). SNS finished-dose purchase from a domestic sole source (ASPR and USAntibiotics, May 2026).

### Read-across to the Telo repository

LNPM-R01 describes Civica as "One large plant (~$124.5M for ~140M units/yr) plus a ~6-month stockpile" and claims Telo's differentiator is that "We remove the single-plant fragility and the inventory cost." Three corrections.

First, capacity. Civica states more than 200 million units per year across three lines, not 140 million (LNPM-S04). The 140,000 figure in circulation is the square footage of the building (LNPM-S04, LNPM-S07).

Second, and more important, the framing is wrong about what Civica is. Civica is not currently a plant-based competitor. GAO records 14 manufacturer partners producing 77 drugs under the Civica label (LNPM-S01). As of the last confirmed public statement, May 2025, Civica supplied about 80 medicines through contract manufacturers and the Petersburg plant "hasn't yet begun to manufacture commercial drugs" (LNPM-S04, LNPM-S07). No FDA approval or inspection outcome for the site has been located since, so this is a fact last confirmed 16 months ago rather than a settled present state. It should not be stated as though Civica has never and will never ship from Petersburg; see section 6. The repo's stated differentiator attacks a single-plant fragility Civica does not currently have, and misses the fragility it does have, which is CMO-inherited quality risk realized in the Astral recall (LNPM-S22).

Third, "we remove the inventory cost" is the weakest of the three claims. Vizient already removes the inventory cost for its members with no up-front investment and no program fee, at 131 million units of claimed scale (LNPM-S19). Removing hospital-held inventory is not a differentiator against this landscape; it is the incumbent offer.

On Phlow, LNPM-R02's probe is consistent with LNPM-S26. The repository's $812M figure is correct, and the second pass of this dossier was wrong to say otherwise. The contract face prints "Total Base CLINS: $354,256,000.00", "Total OPTION CLINs: $458,174,247", and "TOTAL CONTRACT VALUE (incl. potential options): $812,430,247" (LNPM-S11). The base plus the six option periods sums exactly to the $812,430,247 printed there.

There are four quantities in play, not three:

- **$354,256,000**, base CLINs, on the contract face (LNPM-S11).
- **$812,430,247**, as-awarded total contract value including all six option periods, on the contract face (LNPM-S11).
- **$696,665,953.98**, obligated to date (LNPM-S26, LNPM-R02).
- **$708.8M current award and $969,754,682 potential award**, after later modifications (LNPM-S10).

The repository's $812M and the deck's $696.7M are therefore both correct and are measuring different things: as-awarded ceiling including options, versus obligated to date. `research/market-research.md` §9.1 flagged this as an internal inconsistency and asked for "a re-pull from the HHS/BARDA award record" (LNPM-R01). The re-pull resolves it in favour of both figures rather than against either. Neither should be deleted; each should be labelled.

Two cautions. First, the contract-face $812,430,247 is no longer the current ceiling. HigherGov records that "Since initial award the Potential Award value has increased 8% from $899,430,248 to $969,754,682" (LNPM-S10). Second, that $899,430,248 starting point does not reconcile with the $812,430,247 on the 2020 contract face, and no source fetched here explains the $87M difference. [inference] It is most likely an early modification, but that is a guess and is listed in section 6 as unresolved. Any external document should state which quantity it means and as of what date.
