# Competitive landscape: government onshoring and resilience programs

Prepared 2026-09-02. Category: `government_onshoring_programs`. Author: landscape researcher, adversarial design-space review.

## 1. Scope

Publicly funded programs in the United States, the European Union and the United Kingdom whose stated purpose is to create, restore or reserve domestic manufacturing capacity for medicines, their active ingredients or their key starting materials, plus the oversight bodies that assess those programs.

In scope on the US side: ASPR's Center for Industrial Base Management and Supply Chain (IBMSC) and its Industrial Base Expansion (IBx) portfolio; BARDA's Pharmaceutical Countermeasures Infrastructure division and the BioMaP-Consortium; the HHS Defense Production Act Title III program; DoD's Joint Program Executive Office for Chemical, Biological, Radiological and Nuclear Defense (JPEO-CBRND); DARPA's role through EQUIP-A-Pharma; the Defense Logistics Agency Warstopper program; NIST's NIIMBL institute; and the trade instruments (Section 232, Executive Order 14336) that now shape private capital in this sector. Named recipients are covered where the award defines an occupied intervention point: API Innovation Center, Phlow, On Demand Pharmaceuticals, DEKA, Apiject, Manus Bio, and the four EQUIP-A-Pharma performers.

In scope on the European side: HERA, EU FAB, HERA Invest, the Critical Medicines Alliance, the Critical Medicines Act and its legislative status, and the European Court of Auditors assessment. UK: the Life Sciences Innovative Manufacturing Fund and its successor scheme.

Out of scope: nonprofit and public-benefit manufacturers as a class, commercial sterile-injectable manufacturers, CDMOs, 503B outsourcing facilities, and state-level programs. Each has its own dossier. Civica, Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drug Company and Phlow appear here only through their federal awards.

What this dossier is for. The governing question is what intervention actually reduces sterile-injectable shortage risk. Government programs matter twice over: they occupy intervention points, and they are the only buyer for several of them. A point that a federal program already funds is not a gap. A point that four federally funded performers are already demonstrating is not novelty.

Limits. Federal award data is fragmentary. Award ceilings, obligations and outlays are three different numbers and press releases mix them freely. The ASPR IBx portfolio pages publish a base award amount for some entries and nothing for most. Award announcements are not evidence of delivery, and almost none of these programs publish an outcome measure tied to shortage duration or shortage count. Where a number is a ceiling rather than money spent, it is labelled.

Source count. 56 web sources and 4 in-repo files, above the 25 suggested. The overage is deliberate. The category spans six US agencies, three EU institutions and one UK department, and the two most decision-relevant findings (section 5) each rest on more than one independent source. Twenty-three of the 56 web sources, LGOV-S34 through LGOV-S56, were added in a citation audit on 2026-09-03. Each one carries a specific number or date that the originally cited source did not contain. See the verification notes and the last paragraph of section 6.

## 4. Recent events 2023 to 2026 that changed the landscape

**2023-10 to 2023-12, EU sets up the industrial track.** The Commission's October 2023 communication on shortages was followed by EMA's first Union list of critical medicines in December 2023 and by the Critical Medicines Alliance in January 2024. Until then the EU had no industrial policy for medicines. (LGOV-S29)

**2023-11-27, US expands DPA authority to essential medicines.** The White House Council on Supply Chain Resilience convened for the first time, HHS identified $35 million for key starting materials for sterile injectable medicines, and a Supply Chain Resilience and Shortage Coordinator post was announced. The Presidential Determination broadening HHS Title III authority, PD 2024-03, is dated 2023-12-27 and was published in the Federal Register on 2024-01-02. This is the origin of every ASPR pharmaceutical capacity award since. (LGOV-S06, LGOV-S52, LGOV-S54)

**2023-10, BARDA replaces CIADM with BioMaP.** GAO had already documented why CIADM failed: a program originally composed of three sites, one of which was shut down for cross-contamination, and an internal HHS review attributing the failure to the sites lacking regular manufacturing work. BARDA launched the BioMaP-Consortium in October 2023 with Advanced Technology International as consortium manager. GAO closed its CIADM recommendation only in 2026, after BARDA produced risk-management documentation. (LGOV-S01, LGOV-S11)

**2024-09-12, first DPA Title III essential-medicines award.** APIIC received $13,968,691 for albuterol, metoprolol succinate and lorazepam APIs, with $2.4M of its own money. (LGOV-S04, LGOV-S26)

**2024-08-13, DoD joins the Phlow contract.** JPEO-CBRND kicked off an interagency advanced API manufacturing effort at Phlow's Petersburg facility under the ASPR contract, describing it as a 10-year, $1.2 billion cost-plus-fixed-fee award. This figure does not match the obligation or the ceiling in the federal award record. (LGOV-S18, LGOV-S19)

**2025-03-27, HHS restructuring moves ASPR under CDC.** The announced plan cut HHS from 82,000 to 62,000 full-time employees, consolidated 28 divisions to 15, and moved ASPR (roughly 1,000 people) under CDC. Congressional Research Service analysis flagged that the Assistant Secretary for ASPR is statutorily required to report to the Secretary. The institutional home of every US program in this dossier is unsettled. (LGOV-S22)

**2025-04-09, GAO reports HHS has no shortage coordination mechanism.** The coordinator post created in Nov 2023 ended in May 2025 when its funding expired. HHS did not concur with GAO's original recommendation. As of a March 2026 written response, HHS said the administration was still "examining both the need and placement of a centralized coordinating function for drug shortages" and both recommendations remain Open. (LGOV-S10)

**2025-05-15, EQUIP-A-Pharma launches.** ASPR and DARPA funded four projects on AI, machine learning and informatics for agile manufacture of eight sterile injectable or oral medicines, with real-time quality assessment, in-line metrology and a digital approach to regulatory submissions. All four were expected to file ANDAs within a year. (LGOV-S12)

**2025-06-05, point-of-care sterile manufacturing goes to FDA.** ASPR announced two Caspian units, one to Walter Reed and one to FDA for review, each intended to produce up to 500 IV bags per day with automated quality controls tied to a cloud-based quality manager. (LGOV-S16)

**2025-08-13, Executive Order 14336 declares the previous approach a failure.** The order states that only about 10 percent of APIs by volume for US finished drug products are made domestically, that the Biden Administration spent billions without increasing domestic production, and that the Strategic Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients Reserve "is nearly empty." It directs ASPR to name roughly 26 critical drugs and obtain a six-month API supply, subject to available funds. (LGOV-S20)

**2025-09, European Court of Auditors finds no effective EU framework.** Record shortages in 2023 and 2024, 136 critical shortages reported to EMA between 2022 and October 2024, EMA without a mandate outside a health crisis, and uncoordinated national reshoring that "might result in duplication of effort as well as a significant cost for taxpayers." Publication was announced in the Official Journal of 18 September 2025; the report PDF carries no date of its own. (LGOV-S31, LGOV-S50)

**2025-08-07 to 2025-09-30, tariffs enter the picture and are traded for pricing.** FDA announced PreCheck, a two-phase program to speed the construction and review of new domestic drug manufacturing sites, on 2025-08-07 (LGOV-S53). On 2025-09-25 the President announced on Truth Social a 100 percent tariff on any branded or patented pharmaceutical product effective 2025-10-01, the only exemption being companies actively building a US plant (LGOV-S56). Five days later Pfizer became the first company to sign a most-favoured-nation pricing agreement and received in exchange "a three-year grace period during which time Pfizer products under a Section 232 investigation won't face tariffs, provided we further invest in manufacturing in the United States" (LGOV-S55). No source located supports a general pause of the tariff. What happened instead was company-by-company relief bought with pricing concessions, which is the mechanism later written into the April 2026 proclamation.

**2026-02-06 to 2026-03-16, BARDA solicits large-volume parenterals.** RPP 26-2-AM-LVP sought commercial-scale production of essential medicines with a stated preference for anti-microbials and large volume parenterals, an estimated total of roughly $200 million, a 24-month period of performance, a minimum entry requirement of Manufacturing Readiness Level 6, mandatory cost share, and BioMaP membership before award. The solicitation text names distributed manufacturing systems vertically integrated with fill and finish lines as an example platform. No award announcement has been located as of 2026-09-02. (LGOV-S23, LGOV-S24)

**2026-03-24, APIIC extended to propofol and metoprolol.** An additional $8.3M to expand API capacity for propofol and for metoprolol. ASPR's own claim is narrower than the first draft of this dossier reported: the release says the investment "will establish the first end-to-end domestic supply chain for metoprolol in decades" and never uses the words Title III, dosage form or tablet. Federal investment in APIIC passed $22M. (LGOV-S03)

**2026-04-02, Section 232 pharmaceutical proclamation.** A 100 percent ad valorem duty on patented pharmaceuticals and their ingredients as listed in Annex I, reduced to 20 percent for companies with approved onshoring plans (rising to 100 percent on 2030-04-02), 15 percent for the EU, Japan, Korea, Switzerland and Liechtenstein, and 10 percent for the UK, reducing to zero to the extent required by any future US-UK agreement on pharmaceutical pricing. Clause (3)(e) sets a zero rate until 2029-01-20 for companies that are eligible for the 20 percent onshoring rate and have entered into MFN pharmaceutical pricing agreements with the Secretary of Health and Human Services. A zero rate also applies to products listed in Annex IV and, subject to a trade-framework or urgent-health-need determination, to drugs whose approved indications are all orphan-designated and to several specialty categories. The Secretary's findings concern patented pharmaceuticals specifically and contrast them with generics approved under section 505(j), and clause (5) states that generic pharmaceuticals and their associated ingredients "shall not be subject to tariffs pursuant to section 232 at this time". (LGOV-S21)

**2026-04-14, UK publishes an evaluation with a leverage ratio.** More than £80M across three companies at regional sites, plus an Ipsos evaluation reporting £12 of private investment per £1 of grant for the 2021 to 2023 cohort. This is the only outcome evaluation located in this dossier that reports a quantitative ratio. (LGOV-S32)

**2026-05-12 to 2026-06-30, EU Critical Medicines Act reaches provisional agreement.** Resilience criteria become obligatory in critical-medicine procurement, an EU-preference reward scale is permitted, strategic projects gain access to funding and faster permitting, five member states can trigger Commission-led collaborative procurement, and orphan medicines are in scope. The Council release was updated on 30 June 2026 to include the text of the provisional agreement; no source located records a Coreper endorsement on that date. The regulation is not yet adopted. (LGOV-S30)

**2026-06-15, ASPR opens the DS-MRN prize challenge.** Up to $2.04M for a secure digital repository of manufacturing specifications connected to a vetted domestic manufacturer network. The challenge page gives a launch date of 2026-06-15 and a Phase 1 deadline of 2026-08-28; the ASPR release announcing it is dated 2026-06-16. (LGOV-S17, LGOV-S51)

## 5. Unoccupied or weakly occupied intervention points

Each claim below is stated with the evidence that supports it and the evidence that would overturn it.

**5.1 IP7, release assurance, is occupied, not open. [inference]** Telo's remaining defensible asset is a conformal release layer. The federal government named that capability, funded four performers to build it, and framed it in regulatory rather than scientific terms: EQUIP-A-Pharma "aims to develop informatics-based models to create a real-time digital regulatory approval framework for qualification of pharmaceuticals produced in agile pharmaceutical manufacturing platforms" (LGOV-S15). Three of the four performers are building it on sterile injectables (lidocaine, carboplatin, bupivacaine, diltiazem) with in-line metrology and real-time quality assessment (LGOV-S12). DEKA's Caspian ties automated quality controls to a cloud-based quality manager and a unit went to FDA for review (LGOV-S16). JPEO-CBRND's Innovation in Biologics Manufacturing RFI, posted 2025-10-28 with responses due 2025-12-22, makes "Technologies that Reduce Product Release Time" its third technical area and says the office "is evaluating mechanisms to enable real-time release testing or dramatically accelerate release timelines" (LGOV-S36, LGOV-S37). That RFI is the weakest-sourced item in this finding: no sam.gov posting was located, and the two sources are an opportunity aggregator and consultancy commentary on it. A methods paper on public NIR data does not clear this bar. What would overturn this: evidence that the four EQUIP-A-Pharma performers failed to file ANDAs, or that FDA rejected the digital-submission approach.

**5.2 Independent verification of AI-based release claims is genuinely unoccupied. [inference]** Every entity building release-assurance technology is also the entity asserting its validity. ASPR funds the performers. FDA reviews individual applications. No program in this dossier funds a party whose job is to test whether a vendor's release model holds under distribution shift, and the DME rule as reported in the repo contains no discussion of real-time release testing, PAT, continuous manufacturing or AI models at all (LGOV-R04). The gap is a coverage-audit and conformal-guarantee function sitting between the vendor and the regulator, not another vendor platform. This is weakly defended rather than empty: FDA's own review function partially covers it, and a paid third-party assurance role has no established buyer. What would overturn this: an FDA guidance or a BARDA solicitation creating an independent evaluator role.

**5.3 The largest 2025 to 2026 industrial-policy lever does not reach shortage-critical drugs. [inference]** The Section 232 proclamation applies to patented pharmaceuticals and their ingredients, and the Secretary's findings explicitly distinguish these from "generic pharmaceuticals or pharmaceuticals approved pursuant to section 505(j)" (LGOV-S21). Shortages concentrate in generic sterile injectables. So the instrument that has moved the most private capital into US pharmaceutical construction is aimed at the segment that is not short. Grant programs remain the only lever pointed at generics, and those are an order of magnitude smaller: roughly $200M for one BARDA solicitation, $22M cumulative for APIIC. What would overturn this: a later Federal Register notice extending Section 232 tariffs or preferences to generic injectables or their APIs.

**5.4 Every program funds capital, none funds operations. [inference]** DPA Title III creates, maintains, expands or restores productive capacity (LGOV-S06). LSIMF is a capital grants scheme (LGOV-S32). BioMaP awards are project agreements with 24-month periods of performance (LGOV-S23). GAO's finding on CIADM identified precisely the failure this produces: sites were built, then had no regular manufacturing work, then could not hold quality capability (LGOV-S11). Nothing in this dossier pays a manufacturer to run a loss-making shortage-drug line year after year. EU FAB is the nearest exception and it is vaccines only. The 2022 tender notice gives an estimated total value of EUR 160 million over a 48-month contract (LGOV-S44); the Commission's own announcement of the same call describes it as "a yearly budget of €160 million" (LGOV-S45) for the 325 million doses per year of reserved capacity that the framework contract secured (LGOV-S28). The two readings differ by about four times, and the per-year reading is used here because it is the less favourable one for this argument. Read per year, keeping 325 million doses of vaccine capacity warm costs the EU each year roughly what the ceiling of one BARDA large-volume-parenteral solicitation costs once. Standby is not cheap. It is the only line item in this dossier that recurs. This is the strongest structural gap identified, and it is a policy gap rather than a company-shaped one.

**5.5 The cheapest known shortage-insurance mechanism has never been extended to civilians.** DLA Warstopper buys guaranteed access to production capacity rather than inventory. DLA's own estimate was roughly $12.7 million to maintain a six-month supply of 30 shortage-risk medications over five years, and 28 of those 30 were already under Warstopper contract [LGOV-S08, indexed excerpts, not fetched]. Congress directed a feasibility study; DLA concluded expansion was feasible and needed no new statutory authority; GAO found the cost estimate incomplete and asked for a sensitivity analysis (LGOV-S07). Three years later the expansion has not happened. The blocker DLA itself identified was manufacturer reluctance to hold larger volumes they might not sell. That reluctance is a contracting problem, not a technology problem.

**5.6 Nobody measures whether these programs reduce shortages.** GAO criticises Warstopper for lacking outcome-oriented goals (LGOV-S07). GAO finds HHS has no mechanism to coordinate drug shortage activities and the recommendation remains open into 2026 (LGOV-S10). The ECA finds EMA lacks data on alternatives, stock, supply, demand and manufacturing capacity, and that reshoring by member states is uncoordinated (LGOV-S31). EO 14336 asserts that billions were spent without increasing domestic production (LGOV-S20). Four independent oversight bodies converge on the same measurement gap. This is weakly occupied and it is the one place where an analytical rather than industrial intervention has an argument. It is also close to what the DS-MRN challenge and the ECA both describe needing.

**5.7 IP8, digital coordination, is newly and thinly occupied.** ASPR launched the DS-MRN challenge on 2026-06-15 with up to $2.04M and a Phase 1 deadline of 2026-08-28 (LGOV-S17, LGOV-S51). Total prize money is roughly one percent of a single BioMaP solicitation. The concept is a specification repository plus a vetted manufacturer network. Nothing has been built. This is the least crowded US intervention point in the dossier, and it is also the least capitalised, which is why it is uncrowded.

**5.8 What is definitively not open.** Point-of-care and modular sterile manufacturing hardware (ODP, DEKA, and the BioMaP solicitation's own preferred architecture). Generic API and KSM onshoring (Phlow, APIIC, Manus, Bright Path, Rutgers, National Resilience, Batelle, Aprecia, CONTINUUS, all in the IBx portfolio). Sterile fill-finish capacity (Apiject, Par Sterile Products). Anyone entering these is a late entrant against incumbents with six-year contracts and federal sponsors.

## 6. Unconfirmed leads and gaps

**Phlow contract value has four public figures and the repo carries a fifth that matches none.** The federal award record gives $696,665,953.98 obligated and $969,754,681.90 as base and all options (LGOV-S18). HigherGov's mirror of the same record reports "up to $969,754,682." JPEO-CBRND describes it as $1.2 billion over ten years (LGOV-S19). The repo's `docs/startup-outline.md`, `outreach/lab-contacts.md` and `funding/emergent-ventures-proposal.md` state $812M (LGOV-R02). No source located supports $812M. Recommendation: strike $812M from all repo artifacts and use "approximately $697M obligated against a ceiling of approximately $970M" with the DoD $1.2B figure noted as a separate agency characterisation. The deck's $696.7M figure is the correct one.

**Whether RPP 26-2-AM-LVP produced awards is unresolved.** The solicitation closed 2026-03-16 with a period of performance expected to start in Q3 of government fiscal year 2026. No award announcement was located on aspr.gov, medicalcountermeasures.gov, biomap-consortium.org or in trade press. Tried: Exa searches for award announcements, the BioMaP awards page (which publishes only aggregates), and the ASPR newsroom. The BioMaP awards page moved from 16 projects and $181M to 17 projects and $202M between two reads on 2026-09-02, a delta of roughly $21M and one project, which is far below the $200M solicitation ceiling. Either awards have not been made, or they are not yet reflected. A verifier should query USAspending for ATI subawards.

**Total federal spend on shortage-relevant generic capacity is not obtainable from public sources.** ASPR claims "more than $1 billion" for APIs and finished drug products (LGOV-S14) and BARDA PCI claims "$2B+" (LGOV-S01), but the PCI figure covers vaccines and biologics and the two are not reconcilable. The IBx portfolio index publishes award amounts for a minority of entries. No public accounting separates sterile-injectable capacity from PPE, swabs, pipette tips and sterilization, which make up most of the portfolio by entry count.

**Whether the ASPR move under CDC actually happened is unconfirmed.** The March 2025 fact sheet announced it (LGOV-S22). ASPR press releases through June 2026 still carry ASPR branding and an ASPR Principal Deputy Assistant Secretary. Congressional Research Service raised a statutory obstacle. Not resolved: the current reporting line of the Assistant Secretary, and whether IBMSC survived the reorganisation intact.

**EO 14336 implementation status is unknown.** The order required ASPR to name roughly 26 critical drugs within 30 days and to ready the SAPIR repository within 120 days, both subject to available funds. No published list of 26 drugs was located, nor any statement that the repository is operational, nor any procurement notice for API purchases. Tried: aspr.gov newsroom, sam.gov searches were not run. This is the highest-value open question in the dossier, because a federal API purchase program would be the first sustained-demand instrument on the US side.

**HHS OIG has not audited industrial-base program outcomes.** The OIG series located covers physical security and inventory records at Strategic National Stockpile sites A through E, issued between Sept 2024 and July 2025, and found controls adequate with minor discrepancies. No OIG report on IBx, BioMaP, DPA Title III award performance, or Phlow was located. Oversight of whether these programs deliver rests entirely with GAO.

**EU FAB has no publicly located generic-injectable analogue.** EU FAB reserves vaccine capacity (LGOV-S28). Searches did not locate an equivalent reserved-capacity contract for antibiotics or sterile injectables. The Critical Medicines Act's strategic-projects mechanism may become one, but the regulation is not adopted and no strategic project has been designated.

**UK critical-medicines policy beyond LSIMF is under-researched.** LSIMF is a capital grants scheme for manufacturers, not a shortage-response instrument. DHSC's Serious Shortage Protocol regime, the Medicine Supply Notification system, and any UK reserved-capacity contracting were not investigated in this pass.

**Repo corrections beyond the Phlow figure.** `funding/applications.md` (LGOV-R01) correctly records that RPP 26-2-AM-LVP closed 2026-03-16 and that the MRL bar is 6 against Telo's 1 to 2. It omits three things that change the reading. First, the same solicitation's own example platform is "distributed manufacturing systems with a special focus on vertical integration of the systems with fill and finish lines," which means Telo's architecture is the government's stated preference and therefore crowded, not novel (LGOV-S23, LGOV-S24). Second, the doc's sweep of federal opportunities missed EQUIP-A-Pharma entirely, which is the program that most directly occupies Telo's remaining asset (LGOV-S12). Third, it missed the DS-MRN prize challenge, the only federal opportunity with no incorporation, MRL or cost-share bar, whose Phase 1 deadline passed on 2026-08-28, five days before this dossier (LGOV-S17). The repo's conclusion that "no incubator on this list is a cleanroom" remains correct and is reinforced here: none of the federal programs in this dossier supply subsidised aseptic pilot capacity to pre-revenue entrants either.

**Claims removed or restated in the 2026-09-03 citation audit.** Nine assertions in the first draft could not be traced to any source that can be fetched now, and were struck or replaced. Struck outright: that the metoprolol extension was the first use of DPA Title III authority for a finished dosage form; that the extension covered metoprolol tablets; that the April 2026 UK round went to four sites including Norgine; that Coreper endorsed the Critical Medicines Act compromise on 2026-06-30; that HERA Invest was forecast to reach EUR 200M by 2027; that the September 2025 tariff was paused during MFN pricing negotiations; that CIADM was reduced from three sites to one; that Apiject's ASPR money originated in a $138M DPA Title III award in May 2020; and that the Amneal collaboration was announced in May 2025 targeting 250 to 300 million units annually. Project Call 9.1 was also misdated: it was announced in 2025 and awarded in 2026.

Restated with a source that was fetched on 2026-09-03: the ODP contract value and date, its DARPA lineage and its ONR funding; the Just-Evotec award and the 100-calendar-day target; the JPEO-CBRND RFI posting and closing dates and its third technical area; the Apiject award amount; the Manus Bio award totals; the NIIMBL renewal and project-call figures; the EU FAB tendered value and its per-year reading; the Critical Medicines Alliance membership count and five-year term; the Council general approach and Parliament position; the ECA publication date; the DS-MRN launch date and Phase 1 deadline; Presidential Determination 2024-03; the $35M for sterile-injectable key starting materials; and FDA PreCheck.

**One verifier finding was rejected.** The verifier reported that no provision of the Section 232 proclamation grants a zero rate to companies holding MFN pricing agreements, and asked for that clause to be struck. Clause (3)(e) does grant exactly that. The full proclamation text was re-fetched on 2026-09-03 and reads: "For companies that are eligible for the tariff treatment outlined in clause (3)(b) of this proclamation, and that have entered into MFN pharmaceutical pricing agreements with the Secretary of Health and Human Services, the applicable ad valorem tariff rate for pharmaceuticals and associated pharmaceutical ingredients shall be zero until January 20, 2029." The dossier's claim was incomplete rather than wrong. It omitted the eligibility condition and the 2029 sunset, both of which have now been added. The claim was made precise, not deleted.
