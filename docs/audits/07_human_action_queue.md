# Human-action queue (prioritized)

Items a coding agent cannot legitimately do: obtain proprietary or gated data, hold interviews, get qualified
regulatory opinions, sign anything, or decide founder-level questions. Each item names what is needed, why the model
is blocked without it, and where the result goes. Status values: open, in progress, done (with date and evidence
pointer). **Nothing is marked done here.** Every item below is open.

**PRELIMINARY / ILLUSTRATIVE / NOT FOR EXTERNAL CLAIMS.** Every model result cited as a decision value is behavior
under tier-5 illustrative inputs and describes the model, not sterile-injectable manufacturing. All sixteen gates in
`config/regulatory_gates.yaml` are UNCERTAIN, so no strategy carries a favorable decision class. 503B is a
time-varying legal state and never a durable pathway. Nothing here records an interview, a partner, a customer, a
review or a regulatory opinion that has occurred.

## How this file is ordered

Section 1 is the ranked queue: every item in the file, ordered by decision value rather than by section. Decision
value means the package result the item could reverse, named with its artifact. Sections A to E below keep the
original grouping, the original wording, and the original ids, with two columns added to every row: the same decision
value, and the rank from section 1.

Three items are ranked above their decision value, and the reason is stated rather than hidden. HA-06, HA-07 and
HA-08 reverse no model result at all. They sit at ranks 1 to 3 because the exposure they close is live today, the
first of them costs one command, and no analysis in this package is worth more than that.

Ranks are a working order, not a schedule. Several items are cheap and can run in parallel with the expensive ones.

### G. HA-34: public-source screen run 2026-09-06, and what it did and did not close

`docs/route_screen/` ran the public half of HA-34 so the reviewer's time is spent only on what a person can answer.
The item **stays open at rank 13**. Three things changed and one disagreement is recorded rather than resolved.

**The premise is confirmed and measured.** "Sterilization route is not published per marketed presentation on any US
label" holds: 0 of 65 manufacturer labels across 50 applications behind the six longlist candidates state a
sterilization method. The wording should be sharpened, because "no US label states the route" is false as a general
claim: on 2026-09-06 the openFDA label corpus of 262,663 labels returned 17 `"terminally sterilized"` hits (12 of them
negations), 21 `"sterilized by autoclaving"`, 7 `"aseptically filled"` and 2 `"parametric release"`. The true claim is
narrower and is about label vintage and format: no *shortage-prone generic injectable* label states it.

**The blocker is redaction, not absence.** FDA's Product Quality Review template carries a field labelled "Method of
Sterilization:" and publishes it with the value withheld under `(b)(4)`; a second review states "The proposed drug
product is (b) (4) sterilized". Any plan that assumes more searching will surface the route from US public sources is
wrong. The remaining public lever is a different jurisdiction (UK and EU GMP certificate scope), and whether such a
certificate enumerates a third-country site's entire sterile capability or only the operations inspected for the
certifying market is **UNCERTAIN and is now an HA-31 question**.

**The questions are now specific enough to ask, and there are three.** For ANDA 202747 furosemide 10 mg/mL vial at
Baxter Pharmaceuticals India, Chacharwadi-Vasana: (a) does it receive a validated terminal moist-heat cycle on the
sealed vial; (b) **which of the two US-market filling lines named in the 2023 Form FDA 483 fills it, and is that line
terminally sterilized**; (c) does the site operate any aseptic fill line outside the scope of certificate UK GMP 44139.
For ANDA 217523 sodium bicarbonate 8.4% 50 mL vial at Amneal Pharmaceuticals Private Limited: does it receive one.

**Recorded disagreement on acceptable evidence.** This queue says "Label text is not acceptable: it does not carry the
route". `docs/route_screen/method.md` section 2 reads that as an absence rule (a silent label is evidence of nothing)
rather than a source ban, and holds that an affirmative label statement naming the method for that presentation reaches
`established`. On that reading the screen proposes HA-34 **partially closed for acetazolamide 500 mg vial only**, on
label text present verbatim in all six manufacturer labels, and for the freeze-drying half only; the aseptic-fill half
is not proposed closed. That closure is *proposed*, not taken: it requires the HA-34 reviewer to accept the
reinterpretation. Nothing else is closed, and no other presentation is affected.
