**PRELIMINARY / ILLUSTRATIVE / NOT FOR EXTERNAL CLAIMS. NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Nothing here has been reviewed by a qualified sterile-manufacturing, microbiology or generic-drug CMC professional. Method: `method.md`, whose categories, confidence ladder and evidence tiers govern. Prior art: `../design_space/prior_art/11_terminal_sterilization_release.md` (`F11-Sxx`). Human action: **HA-34**. External sources fetched 2026-09-06 unless the row says otherwise.

---

## 1. What this pass can and cannot decide

The method's evidence hierarchy puts everything in this file at **tier E6**: "reasoning from container type, excipient profile, pH, thermal-stability chemistry, F0 arithmetic, or the existence of an autoclavable competitor product", ceiling `weakly_inferred`. Chemistry answers a different question from route, and conflating them is the failure mode this file is built to avoid.

So each row carries **two determinations on two axes**, and they do not imply one another:

- **Moist-heat compatibility** (`compatible`, `compatible_with_conditions`, `incompatible`, `not_applicable`, `unknown`): can this formulation in this container survive a validated moist-heat cycle. A chemistry question, answerable from literature and from label text about withstanding heat.
- **Route** (the five `method.md` categories): how is a named marketed presentation actually sterilized today. A manufacturing question. **Compatibility never establishes route.** A product that survives a cycle is not thereby a product that receives one.

Both axes use the `method.md` confidence ladder. Evidence quality is flagged `fetched_quoted` (I fetched the source and the quote is character-accurate) or `snippet_only` (the wording reached me through a search summary and is not verbatim). `snippet_only` rows cannot exceed `weakly_inferred` on any axis.

**Population.** The six `config/longlist.yaml` presentations, plus the molecule families that dominate the FDA injectable shortage list. Family sizes below are counts of **current shortage presentations** (individual rows, not distinct products) from snapshot **S01** `2026-09-02/Drugshortages.csv`: 836 current injectable rows across 50 distinct generic names.

## 4. Documented failures, and the three distinct mechanisms behind them

Failures matter more than successes here, because a single documented failure closes a row and a success only opens one.

1. **Container and excipient attack, not molecule degradation.** Sodium bicarbonate at 121 C in glass produced sodium dawsonite crystals from aluminium leached out of the vial [F11-S31]. The molecule was fine; the package was not. The same mechanism is the live risk for furosemide at pH 8 to 9.3 and acyclovir at pH 10.85 to 11.50 in glass.
2. **Oxidation of a labile co-solute.** The bupivacaine autoclave permission is scoped away from epinephrine-containing solutions [E03-S02]. Norepinephrine's patented terminal route needs a sulfite antioxidant, an ion chelator, nitrogen flushing and an oxygen scavenger in the overwrap to survive the same cycle [F11-S32; E03-S06]. Catecholamines are not thermally fragile so much as oxygen-fragile under heat, which makes the fix an **oxygen-exclusion packaging system**, a container qualification programme, not a formulation tweak.
3. **Heat-driven pH shift degrading the active.** Reported for lacosamide, where terminal sterilization changes solution pH which then degrades the drug [`snippet_only`, off-list, included because it is a mechanism the other two do not cover]. Sodium bicarbonate is the same mechanism in reverse: CO2 evolution during the cycle converts bicarbonate to carbonate and raises pH, which is why the process patent ballasts the chamber with CO2 at 0.1 to 0.22 MPa [E03-S07].

**Consequence for the model.** Mechanism 3 implies a **gas-ballasted autoclave with overpressure control**, not a plain saturated-steam vessel; mechanism 2 implies **inert-gas fill and overwrap** on the line. If a Telo node's terminal branch is costed as "add an autoclave", it is costed wrong for at least two of the six longlist candidates.

## 5. Three structural findings that outrank any individual row

**A. The dataset HA-34 wants exists, in a named public document class, in a named field, redacted.** FDA's Product Quality Review template carries a field labelled "Method of Sterilization:" in its Product Background block. In the published review for NDA 207987 (Ablysinol, Dehydrated Alcohol Injection USP, Belcher Pharmaceuticals, manufacturing site Sintetica SA, Switzerland) the field is present and its **value is withheld behind a (b)(4) redaction**, as are many other quality fields throughout the document [E03-S20, `fetched_quoted`, PDF extracted locally]. This reframes HA-34. The route is not merely unpublished; it is a field FDA records and withholds as confidential commercial information. The action is a targeted FOIA against a known field in a known document class, or the holder, not a harder search. It also means the corpus is **NDA-only**: ANDAs get no comparable published review, and the shortage list is overwhelmingly ANDA product.

**B. EU and UK GMP certificates are a public, structured, per-site route register, and no family-11 source cites them as one.** Each MHRA (and EudraGMDP) certificate of GMP compliance enumerates the site's authorized operations under fixed codes: "1.1.1 Aseptically prepared", "1.1.2 Terminally Sterilised", and separately "1.4.2 Sterilisation of active substances/excipients/finished products" split into "1.4.2.1 Filtration", "1.4.2.2 Dry heat", "1.4.2.3 Moist heat" [E03-S21, `fetched_quoted`, Macarthys Laboratories t/a Martindale Pharma, certificate UK MIA 1883 Insp GMP 1883/2700-0061[H], inspection 09/04/2026]. A site with no 1.1.2 entry is not authorized to terminally sterilize **for the operations the certificate covers**, so absence of the code is a genuine negative *within that scope* rather than silence. **UNCERTAIN, and assigned to HA-31, marked 2026-09-06.** Whether a UK or EU certificate for a third-country site enumerates that site's *entire* sterile-product capability, or only the operations inspected for the certifying market, is a regulatory reading this file is not entitled to make. UK GMP 44139 states on its face that it was "issued following an inspection ... in connection with marketing authorisation(s) listing manufacturers located outside of the European Economic Area", which is language about scope of supply. The specific question for HA-31: *does a UK or EU GMP certificate for a third-country site enumerate all of that site's sterile-product operations, or only those in scope for the certifying market?* Until that is answered, a missing 1.1.1 is a negative about certified operations and not about the site. Joined to the manufacturing site named on an application, this generates leads at scale, for free, for every site that supplies the EU or UK. *Corrected 2026-09-06:* it does **not** on its own support `strongly_inferred`. A certificate is site-and-dosage-form granular, so reaching a presentation needs a bridging premise (that every sterile product at the site takes the certified route), and any second line that also runs through that premise is not independent of it under `method.md` rule 1. The one attempt made in this screen landed at `weakly_inferred` for that reason. Limits, stated plainly: it is site-level not presentation-level; a site holding both 1.1.1 and 1.1.2 tells you nothing about which product went which way; and US-only sites are not covered. This is the closest thing to the missing enabling dataset found in this pass, and it belongs in the next evidence pass rather than this one.

**C. US labels do carry autoclave statements, and they are not route statements.** Three of the highest-volume shortage products publish explicit cycle conditions on the approved label [E03-S02, E03-S03, E03-S04]. A fourth product publishes the opposite instruction: the Fresenius Kabi morphine sulfate label reads "Do not autoclave." [E03-S17, re-fetched 2026-09-06]. Every one of the four is a **user-directed instruction** about the supplied container, not a statement of how the manufacturer sterilizes. They settle the chemistry axis at `established` and leave the route axis exactly where it was. `method.md` section 2's reconciliation of HA-34 holds and is now tested against real text: an affirmative *route* statement would reach `established`, and none of these is one.

**The one published F0 on the whole shortage list, and why it is ambiguous.** Naropin's label states stability demonstrated "using a targeted F0 of 7 minutes at 121°C" [E03-S04]. The Ph. Eur. reference condition is 121 C for 15 minutes, and EMA defines a moist-heat process with **F0 below 8 minutes** as a *post-aseptic processing terminal heat treatment*, explicitly not a terminal sterilization: "Moist heat processes with an F0 < 8 min may be suitable as a post-aseptic processing terminal heat treatment for formulations that cannot withstand a complete terminal sterilisation cycle" [E03-S16, `fetched_quoted`]. Two readings fit that number and the label does not distinguish them. Read one: F0 7 is a conservative demonstration for a user convenience, an extra cycle the product tolerates *on top of* whatever it received at manufacture, in which case it says nothing about the manufacturing budget and may even show headroom. Read two: F0 7 is near the tested ceiling, in which case ropivacaine cannot reach a Ph. Eur. terminal cycle and lands in `aseptically_filled` with a post-fill heat treatment, a category `method.md` already provides for and one that reaches **no** parametric release under CPG 490.200 or Annex 17. The general point survives either reading, and is the trap this file exists to flag: chemistry compatibility at `established` and route ineligibility can coexist in the same product, because the threshold that matters is an F0 number and no label on this list publishes the manufacturer's.

## 7. What would overturn this file as a whole

- A fetched Indian Pharmacopoeia, older British Pharmacopoeia or other monograph that **directs** a sterilization method for any of these products. Whether any current monograph still does is **unverified**: I could not fetch a monograph body in this pass, and the claim that older pharmacopoeias carried sterilization directions while current Ph. Eur. and USP do not is an untested assumption, not a finding. A single such monograph is E1 and closes rows at `established`. This is the highest-yield next move after finding B.
- A EudraGMDP or MHRA certificate scrape joined to marketing-authorisation site data, per finding B, which would convert this file's E6 reasoning into E1-adjacent site evidence for the EU-supplied fraction of the shortage list.
- Any FOIA release of the "Method of Sterilization" field per finding A.
- A showing that the VIAFLEX "moisture absorption during the sterilization process" sentence describes pre-fill container sterilization, which removes the file's only route determination.
- A CDMO, consultancy or ANDA holder marketing route conversion as a service, which would falsify `prior_art_review.md` section 6.1's unoccupied claim independently of anything here.
