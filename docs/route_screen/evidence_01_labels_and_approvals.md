**Question this stage answers.** Not "what is the route". It is: *does the US public documentary record carry the route at all, and where.* The determinations below are a by-product. The map of document classes is the deliverable.

**Answer in one line.** For the six longlist presentations the US record carries the route in **one** of six cases, and that one case is the lyophilized comparator, whose route is disclosed only because freeze-drying is a formulation fact the label has to state anyway. For the five aqueous-solution candidates the record is empty, and the review document that would carry the answer exists, is written, and is redacted as confidential commercial information.

## 2. Does any section of a US package insert state the sterilization method?

**In the candidate corpus: no. Zero of 65 states a *sterilization method*.** Six of the 65, the acetazolamide labels, state the drying step from which the `lyophilized` category is taken, which is a dosage-form fact the label has to carry anyway and is not a statement about how sterility was achieved (*clarified 2026-09-06*, so this count and the `established` determination in section 6 row 6 stop contradicting each other). Each SPL was tag-stripped and searched for `steriliz|sterilis|autoclav|terminal|aseptic|moist heat|dry heat|irradiat|heat treat`. Every hit was one of three non-route things:

1. **Handling instructions to the user.** "When introducing additives, use aseptic technique, mix thoroughly and do not store" (12 of the 14 sodium bicarbonate labels, verbatim across Hospira, Exela, Civica, Amneal, Fresenius Kabi, Glenmark and Milla). "Use aseptic technique for single or multiple entry and withdrawal from all containers" (Sterile Water, Hospira / Hikma / Civica). This is the principal trap in the whole stage: a naive full-text search for "aseptic" returns bedside technique, not process route, and would produce a confidently wrong route table.
2. **Pharmacokinetics.** "The terminal half-life of furosemide is approximately 2 hours" (12 of 13 furosemide labels). A word-stem match on "terminal" is worthless.
3. **One container statement**, section 3.2 below.

**In the US label corpus as a whole: yes, but rarely, and not for these products.** Phrase counts from `E01-S01` on 2026-09-06: `"terminally sterilized"` 17, `"sterilized by autoclaving"` 21, `"aseptically filled"` 7, `"terminal sterilization"` 1, `"parametric release"` 2, `"sterilized by moist heat"` 0. Against a corpus of order 10^5 labels. Those counts are label-level hits on exact phrases, they overlap, and a large minority is negative. *Corrected 2026-09-06:* re-querying the same API on 2026-09-06 against an unchanged corpus of 262,663 labels reproduces every count in the sentence above exactly, but **not** the negative count previously printed here as eight. The exact phrase `"this kit has not been terminally sterilized"` returns **4**; the looser `"has not been terminally sterilized"` returns **12**. Eight is not reproducible from either phrase and is withdrawn. The qualitative point survives on the looser phrase, which is the one that matters: of 17 `"terminally sterilized"` label hits, 12 are negations. So tier E2 of the method's hierarchy is **not empty, it is negligible**, and the affirmative hits cluster in two legacy label families:

- Iodinated contrast: "OMNIPAQUE injection and OMNIPAQUE oral solution are sterilized by autoclaving and contain no preservatives"; "All solutions are sterilized by autoclaving and contain no preservatives" (Optiray, Liebel-Flarsheim); "ULTRAVIST is a sterile (sterilized by autoclaving), clear, colorless to slightly yellow, odorless, pyrogen-free aqueous solution" (Bayer). [`E01-S01`]
- Radiopharmaceuticals, in both directions: "STERILITY AND APYROGENICITY This product is terminally sterilized by autoclave. Apyrogenicity is confirmed before release by a Limulus test." (Indiclor Indium Chloride In-111, SPL `3d83fdf7-78f7-4ea0-97a9-e7434c82067d`); and "This kit has not been terminally sterilized", which matches 4 SPLs on the exact phrase and 12 on the looser `"has not been terminally sterilized"` (*corrected 2026-09-06*). [`E01-S01`]

Two further hits matter for what they show is possible. "The drug product is aseptically filled into 10 cc clear glass vials with gray rubber stoppers and flip-off seals" (Duocaine, Amphastar Pharmaceuticals Inc., NDA 021496, DailyMed set id `a60d1f55-2c9f-432d-b230-c0f71f171cbd`, version 2, effective 2009-12-15; set id written in full and the holder identified 2026-09-06) is a clean E2 `aseptically_filled` at `established` for a small-volume glass vial, which proves the format exists for exactly the presentation type Telo cares about and that no candidate holder uses it. And a B. Braun temporary-importation Dear Health Care Provider letter **dated 29 August 2014**, filed as a US SPL (`271e8b0f-d051-48a7-9093-8b53078a6a07`, SPL revision 2/2023, version 2; *the document date is 2014, corrected 2026-09-06, because `method.md` rule 7 requires the date of the document and not of the file revision*), carries a product comparison table whose "Sterilization Process" row reads, for the German Ecoflac product, "Terminal Steam Sterilization / Sterility Test (Ph. Eur.) / Sterility Assurance Level of 10-6", and for the **US EXCEL product**, "Terminal Steam Sterilization / **Parametric Release** / Sterility Assurance Level of 10-6". [`E01-S01`] That is a US-marketed injectable, disclosed in a US label file, that is terminally steam sterilized *and* on parametric release, with a sister presentation on the same route released on a sterility test. It is the "third state" of `method.md` section 4 shown as two adjacent columns of one table. Three qualifiers that the register row now carries and that belong here too: DailyMed flags the file "This drug has not been found by FDA to be safe and effective, and this labeling has not been approved by FDA"; the imported product's marketing category is "Unapproved drug for use in drug shortage" with marketing start 08/19/2014 and end 12/31/2020; and a Dear Health Care Provider letter is not approved labelling, so it sits at tier E3 rather than E2 and its ceiling is `strongly_inferred`, not `established`. **Reconciliation with HA-34, applied.** HA-34's "label text is not acceptable" holds as an absence rule and fails as a source ban, exactly as `method.md` section 2 anticipated. Silence in 65 of 65 candidate labels is evidence of nothing. An affirmative statement, where one exists, reaches `established`.

## 3. Are "sterile", terminal-sterilization and preservative-free statements route-diagnostic?

### 3.1 "Sterile", "nonpyrogenic", "contains no preservative": no, and the correlation runs backwards

Every candidate label carries a sterility assertion, and it is a statement of the required attribute under 21 CFR 211.167(a), not of how the attribute was achieved. "Furosemide Injection 10 mg/mL is a sterile, non-pyrogenic solution in vials" (Baxter, ANDA 202747). "Sodium Bicarbonate Injection, USP is a sterile, nonpyrogenic, hypertonic solution … The solution contains no bacteriostat, antimicrobial agent or added buffer" (Hospira, ANDA 202432). Preservative-free status is a container-and-dosing choice: USP requires an antimicrobial preservative in multi-dose containers whatever the route, and terminal sterilization neither requires nor forbids one. The historical correlation runs the other way: the multi-dose, preservative-containing local anesthetics are the products whose labels state an autoclave cycle. **Never infer route from preservative status. It carries no information in either direction.**

### 3.2 The one route-diagnostic sentence in the candidate corpus, and why it is not about the candidate

"Some opacity of the plastic due to moisture absorption during the sterilization process may be observed. This is normal and does not affect the solution quality or safety. The opacity will diminish gradually." Appears in Baxter Sterile Water for Injection VIAFLEX Pharmacy Bulk Package (NDA 018632) and ICU Medical Sterile Water for Injection 1000 mL flexible container (NDA 018233). This is an affirmative statement that the plastic of the container the user is opening took up water during a sterilization process, which is only coherent if the sealed, filled container went through a steam cycle. It is the standard large-volume-parenteral terminal-sterilization artifact. Two limits keep it off the top rung: it does not name the modality, and it does not say the process was applied post-fill rather than to the container. It reaches `weakly_inferred` alone. **It belongs to the 1000 mL and 2000 mL flexible containers, which are not the longlist presentation** (`sterile_water_for_injection_vial`, single-dose vial). No vial SWFI label in the corpus contains it.

### 3.3 The second trap: "may be sterilized by autoclaving" is an instruction to the user

"Single-dose vials and multiple-dose vials of POLOCAINE may be sterilized by autoclaving at 15 pound pressure, 121°C (250°F) for 15 minutes" (Fresenius Kabi). "Vials may be sterilized by autoclaving or by immersion in a sterilizing solution." [`E01-S01`] These are external-surface resterilization instructions for the hospital. They establish that the container and the formulation survive a 121°C cycle. They say nothing about the manufacturer's route. They are the strongest *convertibility* evidence in the whole US label corpus, and `method.md` section 4 forbids the screen from outputting convertibility, so they are recorded and not used.

## 4. Do the Orange Book, establishment registration, or listing data carry it?

**Orange Book: no.** The product file has fourteen fields and none is process-related: `Ingredient~DF;Route~Trade_Name~Applicant~Strength~Appl_Type~Appl_No~Product_No~TE_Code~Approval_Date~RLD~RS~Type~Applicant_Full_Name` [`E01-S04`, S17]. Therapeutic equivalence codes describe interchangeability, not manufacture. A route change by supplement leaves no Orange Book trace.

**Establishment registration and listing: partially, and this is the one new lever found.** The SPL establishment section carries FDA business-operation codes, published per NDC and rendered publicly in DailyMed's Establishment table. One of them is **STERILIZE, NCI code C84382** [`E01-S05`]. Across the 65 candidate SPLs: 46 carry an establishment section, and **6 carry a STERILIZE operation**:

| candidate | application | establishment registering STERILIZE | NDCs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **sodium bicarbonate 8.4% 50 mEq/50 mL single-dose vial** | ANDA 217523 (Amneal) | Amneal Pharmaceuticals Private Limited, DUNS 675474666 | 80830-2305 |
| **furosemide 10 mg/mL single-dose vials, 2 / 4 / 10 mL** | ANDA 202747 (Baxter) | Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Limited, DUNS 918603338 | 36000-063/-064/-065/-282/-283/-284 |
| **furosemide 10 mg/mL 4 mL vial, Civica label, same ANDA** | ANDA 202747 | same | 72572-190 |
| sodium bicarbonate 12.6 mg/mL 1000 mL Viaflo (not the candidate) | NDA 218524 (Baxter) | Baxter Healthcare Corporation, DUNS 005083209 | 0338-0822 |
| sodium bicarbonate LVP (not the candidate) | NDA 220790 (Amneal) | Amneal Pharmaceuticals Private Limited | 80830-2613 |
| Sterile Water VIAFLEX bulk (not the candidate) | NDA 018632 (Baxter) | Baxter Healthcare Company / Corporation | 0338-0013 |

What this does and does not license. It is registration data of record, filed by the holder, naming an establishment that performs a sterilization operation on **that finished NDC**. It does **not** name the modality, and it does **not** distinguish sterilization of the sealed final container from sterilization of components, containers or bulk. It therefore does not discriminate between any two of the method's five categories, and on its own it cannot reach even `weakly_inferred` *for a category*. **The absence rule applies with full force in the other direction**: 40 of 46 establishment sections show MANUFACTURE with no STERILIZE, and that is evidence of nothing about route, because a firm running its own autoclave in line has no obligation to register the step separately. The code is a **lead-generation instrument, not an evidence tier.** Its value is that it names, for exactly two of the six candidate presentations, a specific site and DUNS that a person can now ask a specific question about.

This evidence class was not anticipated in `method.md` section 3. It should be added there as a row below E6, call it E7, registration and listing metadata, ceiling **`unknown`, contributes leads only**, rather than being smuggled in at E5. That file is not edited here.

**One structured field is genuinely route-bearing, and only for one category.** The SPL dosage-form code distinguishes `INJECTION, POWDER, LYOPHILIZED, FOR SOLUTION` from `INJECTION, SOLUTION`. Five of six acetazolamide manufacturer SPLs carry the lyophilized code. It is not reliable on its own: Sagent's SPL for ANDA 219775 is coded `INJECTION, POWDER, FOR SOLUTION` while its own carton text reads "Lyophilized. Sterile, Nonpyrogenic, Preservative-free." **Where the code and the label text disagree, the label text is the evidence and the code is not.**

**Where a package does exist, the route word is redacted.** FDA's Cross-Discipline Team Leader Review for NDA 214313, Norepinephrine Bitartrate in 5% Dextrose Injection (Baxter), reads [`E01-S06`, verbatim, redaction marks as printed]:

> "2.2.1. Manufacturing: The manufacturing process involves (b) (4) . The validated process for (b) (4) sterilization, and container closure integrity testing are adequate."

> "2.2.2. Microbiological Aspects: The proposed drug product is (b) (4) sterilized. The product sterility is the key critical quality attribute of the proposed product."

> "Microbiology tests, i.e. sterility, and bacterial bioburden testing are performed as per USP <71> and USP <85>, respectively, as part of the release testing of the finished product."

This is the most important paragraph in the stage. FDA writes a sentence whose entire content is the sterilization route, and withholds the one word, twice, under FOIA exemption 4. The prior-art note carried the third sentence as `F11-S20`; the first two were not recorded there. The correct reading is not "labels and approvals are silent". It is: **the route is written down in a public-facing FDA document and deliberately withheld as confidential commercial information.** The document class is not missing. It is classified. The third sentence is independently useful and orthogonal to route: it establishes at `established` that this product is released on a completed USP `<71>` test, so whatever its route, it is not on parametric release.

The 214628 product quality review [`E01-S07`] was extracted and searched to the same end: its readable text contains a labeling-chapter table row reading "Statement of being sterile (if applicable) | Yes | Acceptable" and no process statement. Same pattern.

## 8. Which document class carries the answer, and who can obtain it

Ranked by strength, with the cost of getting it.

1. **The approved application, module 3.2.P.3.5 and 3.2.P.5 (sterility assurance).** Names the cycle, the F0, the load pattern and the release strategy. Definitive. Held by the application holder and by FDA. Not obtainable by Telo. A FOIA request returns the same `(b)(4)` redaction demonstrated in section 5, because the redaction is the holder's trade-secret claim, not an FDA filing lag. **Who can obtain it:** the holder; a CDMO under a confidentiality agreement with the holder; a consultant with prior privileged access, who then cannot cite it. This is the structural reason HA-34 exists.
2. **EU and UK GMP certificates, EudraGMDP and MHRA-GMDP. Public, free, primary, government, and not yet used by this study.** The certificate scope is a structured table that distinguishes `1.1.1 Aseptically prepared` from `1.1.2 Terminally Sterilised`, sub-coded by dosage form (`1.1.2.1 Large volume liquids`, `1.1.2.3 Small volume liquids`, `1.1.1.2 Lyophilisates`), and separately lists `1.4.2 Sterilisation of active substances/excipients/finished products: 1.4.2.1 Filtration / 1.4.2.2 Dry heat / 1.4.2.3 Moist heat` [`E01-S08`, verbatim scope headings]. It is **site-and-dosage-form** granular, not presentation granular, so it cannot close a row on its own. It is a strong **falsifier**: a site whose certificate scope carries no terminally-sterilised small-volume-liquids line and no moist-heat entry is not terminally moist-heat sterilizing a small-volume liquid there. Coverage reaches non-EU sites inspected by EU authorities, which includes most of the Indian sites behind these ANDAs. **Who can obtain it:** anyone, today, for free. This is the recommended next evidence stage and it should displace further US label work.
3. **National public assessment reports (MHRA PAR, Dutch MEB, German BfArM, Irish HPRA) for the same molecule and presentation.** E1 by the method's hierarchy where the quality section speaks about the drug product.
   *Corrected 2026-09-06.* The quoted sentence was attributed to "[`E01-S08` sibling document]", and `E01-S08` is a bare domain (`cms.mhra.gov.uk/mhra/gmp/`) for GMP certificate scope tables, which contain no narrative quality assessment. The sentence is genuine but comes from a different document class and, in the instances located, is about a **solvent** rather than a drug product. Its own source id: **`E01-S09`**, MHRA UK Public Assessment Report, *Menopur 600 IU & 1200 IU Powder and solvent for solution for injection (menotrophin), PL 03194/0106-0107*, `https://mhraproducts4853.blob.core.windows.net/docs/dcdb1f0e855415b2a52c8d5ee6705557a4a2c5a5`, fetched 2026-09-06. Verbatim at section 5.3.3, which sits under the report's section 5 headed "SOLVENT": "The manufacturing process is a standard procedure for terminally sterilised parenteral solutions." The same sentence appears in a sibling PAR at `.../ed38f028f9dfce7157010c7cb6f9609c6e2bb379`.
   `method.md` section 2 states that "a statement about the drug substance, a component or a diluent is not a statement about the drug product", so **as located, this sentence would not be admissible for a drug-product determination.** The claim that PARs "routinely state the route in plain words" for drug products is therefore **downgraded to unevidenced in this pass**: the document class plainly can carry a route sentence in the required form, and no instance about a drug product was located. Limitation that stands either way: a different holder and a different site, so a PAR establishes the route for that authorisation, never for the US one. Free, public, per-product searching.
4. **FDA establishment inspection records: EIR under FOIA, and Form 483 observations.** An EIR for a sterile drug process inspection under CPGM 7356.002A [F11-S15] names the autoclave and the cycle where one exists. Redacted, slow, per-site, and often years old. **Who can obtain it:** anyone, by FOIA, at months of latency. Now targetable, because section 4 named two specific sites and DUNS numbers.
5. **SPL establishment registration STERILIZE flag.** Free, instant, per-NDC. Generates leads, settles nothing. Section 4.
6. **The holder, asked directly.** The fastest path and the one this study cannot fabricate. `~/telo/feasibility/CLAUDE.md` forbids inventing the answer; HA-34 already carries the question. The two questions are now specific enough to ask: *does ANDA 202747 furosemide 10 mg/mL vial use a validated terminal moist-heat cycle on the sealed vial at the Ahmedabad site, and does ANDA 217523 sodium bicarbonate 8.4% 50 mL vial use one at the Amneal Private Limited site.*

## 9. What this stage changes

- **HA-34's premise is confirmed and its wording should be sharpened.** "Sterilization route is not published per marketed presentation on any US label" is right and is now measured: 0 of 65 manufacturer labels, 50 applications, 6 presentations. But "no US label states the route" as a general claim is **false**: a few dozen do, across roughly 10^5 labels, and one of them names parametric release on a marketed US product. The correct claim is that no *shortage-prone generic injectable* label states it, which is a statement about label vintage and format, not about what a label may contain.
- **The blocker is not absence, it is redaction.** Section 5 shows FDA writing the route and withholding the word. Any plan that assumes more searching will eventually surface the route from US public sources is wrong. The next move is either a different jurisdiction (item 2 above, free and unstarted) or a person (item 6).
- **Two named leads now exist where there were none**, both on longlist presentations, both with a site and a DUNS: Baxter Pharmaceuticals India for furosemide ANDA 202747, and Amneal Pharmaceuticals Private Limited for sodium bicarbonate ANDA 217523. Neither is a determination. Both are askable.
- **Nothing here moves a model parameter.** No row reached terminal at any confidence, so `sterilization_route` in `product_architecture.py` stays unset for all six, `sterility_incubation_days` stays at 14/14/18, and the parametric-release `ReleaseScenario` remains unevaluable. The only model-relevant fact recovered is negative and firm: the one candidate-adjacent product with a published FDA review is released on a completed USP `<71>` test [`E01-S06`], not parametrically.

## 10. Limitations of this stage

1. Manufacturer labels only. Repackager and distributor SPLs were excluded by name filter, which may have dropped a manufacturer whose corporate name matched the filter.
2. The acyclovir and sodium bicarbonate populations are complete only to the extent that live DailyMed pages 2 and 3 were fetched; a holder marketing under a name not matching `drug_name` in the DailyMed query would be invisible.
3. Phrase counts in section 2 are exact-phrase and case-insensitive. A label stating the route in wording not matching those five phrases would not be counted. The counts are lower bounds.
4. The `(b)(4)` redactions were read from `pdftotext -layout` output of the FDA PDFs. The redaction marks are rendered as printed text in that extraction; they were not visually confirmed against the page images.
5. *Restated 2026-09-06 after re-parsing the XML.* The STERILIZE establishment attribution in section 4 is **structural, not document-order**, and the earlier text understated it. In the Baxter furosemide SPL (`c71371a7-75c5-45b8-b762-8d782d4c71bc`, version 23, effective 2025-06-30) the shape is `<assignedEntity><assignedOrganization><id extension="918603338"/><name>BAXTER PHARMACEUTICALS INDIA PRIVATE LIMITED</name></assignedOrganization><performance>…C84382 STERILIZE…</performance>`: the `performance` blocks are siblings of `assignedOrganization` inside the same innermost `assignedEntity`, which is the HL7 SPL binding of an establishment to its business operations. That block contains exactly one organisation name, and ANALYSIS, STERILIZE, LABEL, MANUFACTURE and PACK are each bound to all six NDCs (36000-063/-064/-065/-282/-283/-284). The "disagreeing" result reproduces only if a parser walks `.iter()` from an outer `representedOrganization`, which pulls nested entities' performances up to the registrant, and that is a parser artefact rather than a genuine ambiguity. It is corroborated by the label's own "Manufactured by: Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd Ahmedabad 382213, India" and by the matching postcode on the MHRA certificate ("AHMEDABAD, IN 382213"). Both the site attribution and the operation-to-NDC binding are `established`. **The real residual risk is stage and scope, not site**: the code does not say whether the operation is sterilization of the sealed final container or of components, containers or bulk, and it does not say which of the site's filling lines the NDCs run on.
6. No non-US source was worked. That is deliberate: item 2 of section 8 is the next stage, not this one.
7. Presentation assignment within each ingredient was made from each label's own Dosage Forms and Strengths or How Supplied text. Where one application covers several fill volumes of the same strength, all are treated as one presentation.
   *Reconciled 2026-09-06.* That practice conflicted with `method.md` section 5 item 2, which forbids transferring a route between presentations of one manufacturer and names fill volume as one of the things that changes the thermal argument. `longlist.yaml` leaving a fill unfixed is a reason the study has no single fill to model; it is not evidence that the fills share a cycle. `method.md` section 5 item 2 now carries an explicit exception clause: several fills may share a row **only** as a declared load family, naming the NDCs, stating that it is an assumption, and carrying its own falsifier. The furosemide ANDA 202747 row in `route_determinations.csv` is written that way. This limitation is governed by that clause.
