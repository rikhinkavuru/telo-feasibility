## 1. What the screen found

**The two numbers that decide whether family 11 is live for this study, stated first.** **Zero `established` route determinations exist for any in-archetype longlist candidate.** **For the two product configs the model actually runs on, sodium bicarbonate 8.4% vial and norepinephrine 1 mg/mL vial, the screen determined nothing at any confidence: 0 of 24 manufacturer labels and 0 of 18 applications.** One in-archetype determination exists anywhere on the longlist, furosemide under ANDA 202747, and after audit it stands at `weakly_inferred` with a recorded contradiction, on a protocol 2.5 reserve candidate that carries no product config. The one `established` row in scope is the acetazolamide comparator, and it is established for freeze-drying only, not for a sterilization route. Everything below refines those numbers; none of it moves them.

**The screen was applied to 7 in-scope presentation rows covering the six `config/longlist.yaml` candidates, and to 6 adjacent rows recorded because they were screened and produced determinations. ## 2. The six longlist candidates

### 2.1 Furosemide 10 mg/mL vial, ANDA 202747 (Baxter Healthcare Corporation, and Civica under the same ANDA): `terminally_sterilized_moist_heat`, `weakly_inferred`, `contradicted`

**Downgraded 2026-09-06 from `strongly_inferred`, for two independent reasons.** This is still the only longlist row on which family 11 might exist as an intervention. It is no longer a row anyone should act on.

*Reason 1: the independence claim does not survive the screen's own rules.* One lead-only registration line plus two site-scope lines that share a single bridging premise is not "three independent lines". Independence of **filer** is not independence of **inference chain**.

1. **Holder-filed US registration. This is a lead, not a line.** The SPL establishment section binds ANALYSIS, STERILIZE (NCI code C84382), LABEL, MANUFACTURE and PACK to Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Limited, DUNS 918603338, against all six Baxter furosemide vial NDCs and Civica's 72572-190 [`E01-S05`]. `evidence_01` section 4 rules that this class "does not discriminate between any two of the method's five categories, and on its own it cannot reach even `weakly_inferred` *for a category*" and is "a lead-generation instrument, not an evidence tier". That recommendation has now been actioned: `method.md` section 3 carries tier **E7**, registration and listing metadata, ceiling `unknown`, leads only. A row may cite E7 to say where to look and may never count it among the converging lines `strongly_inferred` requires. Counting it was the first error.
2. **UK inspectorate certificate from an on-site inspection.** [`RS4-S01`], verbatim scope: `[ 1.1.2 ] Terminally Sterilised (processing operations for the following dosage forms) / [ 1.1.2.1 ] Large volume liquids / [ 1.1.2.3 ] Small volume liquids / [ 1.1.2.5 ] Other terminally sterilised prepared products Emulsions`, and `[ 1.4.2 ] Sterilisation of active substances/excipients/finished products: / [ 1.4.2.1 ] Filtration / [ 1.4.2.3 ] Moist heat`. **The certificate carries no `[ 1.1.1 ] Aseptically prepared` entry and no Restrictions or Clarifying remarks.** A 2 to 10 mL vial is a small volume liquid.
3. **FDA document of record.** [`RS4-S02`], on the January 2023 inspection of that site: "Equipment used in the manufacture of terminally sterilized drugs at your facility is not designed or maintained appropriately." The word *aseptic* appears nowhere in that letter, re-checked 2026-09-06.

Lines 2 and 3 are both site-scope, neither names furosemide, and both reach this presentation only through one unstated and unverified premise: that every sterile product made at Chacharwadi-Vasana takes the certified route. `method.md` rule 1 says lines tracing to one underlying argument are not independent of one another, and `method.md` section 2 defines `weakly_inferred` as "one line of indirect evidence, or converging lines that are not independent of each other". That is this row.

*Reason 2: a primary counter-signal from the same inspection, in a document this screen had not read.* The first pass cited the warning letter and never opened the **Form FDA 483** behind it [`RS5-S01`]. The warning letter sentence is the agency's rendering of Observation 4; Observation 4 itself reads, verbatim:

> item 1, page 10 of 19: "The filling rooms and capping rooms for both the [(b)(4)] and [(b)(4)] filling lines used for US market product are not designed to permit operators to move freely to the required areas."

> item 3, page 11 of 19: "The [(b)(4)] filling line is not designed to permit viewing from windows or cameras from outside of the aseptic gowning areas. Entry records show QA is not providing routine oversight during filling operations."

So the site runs **at least two filling lines for US market product**, FDA describes one of them in aseptic-processing terms, and nothing in this screen ties ANDA 202747 furosemide to either line. The row's own falsifier already said that a showing the site runs a US-only aseptic line outside the UK certificate's scope drops it to `weakly_inferred`. This is that showing, short of naming the line. The pair of documents is also internally tense in a way the screen should not smooth over: in the warning letter FDA characterises the drugs made on that equipment as "terminally sterilized", and in the 483 covering the same observation FDA refers to aseptic gowning areas at one of the two lines.

*A counter-signal inside line 2 that the first pass quoted and never addressed.* The MHRA certificate's finished-product sterilisation scope carries `[ 1.4.2.1 ] Filtration` alongside `[ 1.4.2.3 ] Moist heat`, and filtration is the sterilising step of an aseptic process. Recorded as an unresolved counter-signal rather than explained away.

**How `method.md` rule 6 is applied here, recorded and not resolved by preference.** Rule 6 sends a determination with contradicting primary evidence to `unknown` pending resolution. The 483 contradicts the bridging premise rather than the determination, because it does not name furosemide. The row is therefore held at `weakly_inferred` with the `contradicted` flag set, and a reviewer who reads the 483 as contradicting the determination should move it to `unknown`. Either way it is not a fact and it sets no model parameter. What would close it is one sentence from Baxter or one FDA review.

**On the scorecard framing, corrected.** Furosemide is the repository scorecard's 2026-07-16 pick for the **flagship paper** rather than for the commercial molecule, at a margin the scorecard itself calls non-decisive ("Runner-up: Dopamine HCl Injection… The margin is 0.4 weighted points. State it that way; don't pretend it's decisive"), and it carries no product config in this package. The earlier closing line of this section borrowed authority the scorecard disclaims.

### 2.2 Acetazolamide sodium 500 mg vial: `lyophilized`, `established` for freeze-drying only

## 3. What this means for the study

**The release decomposition, computed from `config/global.yaml`.** Not measured. *Heading corrected 2026-09-06.* `release_assurance.conventional_release_days` is `max(parallel) + serial QA`. From `config/global.yaml`: sterility 14/14/18, environmental monitoring 5/7/7, assay 1/5/7, endotoxin 0/2/3, serial QA 0/2/4.

| corner | aseptic, today | terminal with approved parametric release | days removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| low | `max(14, 5, 1, 0) + 0` = **14** | `max(5, B, 1, 0) + 0` = **5** if B <= 5 | 9 days, conditional on B <= 5 |
| base | `max(14, 7, 5, 2) + 2` = **16** | `max(7, B, 5, 2) + 2` = **9** if B <= 7 | 7 days, conditional on B <= 7 |
| high | `max(18, 7, 7, 3) + 4` = **22** | `max(7, B, 7, 3) + 4` = **11** if B <= 7 | 11 days, conditional on B <= 7 |

*Caption, and it governs every cell.* The 16-day base denominator includes `qa_review_days`, which `config/global.yaml` carries at `evidence_tier: 5`, `validation_status: illustrative`. `feasibility/CLAUDE.md` forbids presenting tier-5-driven outputs as findings, so the percentage that previously appeared here in bold, 7 days as 43.8 percent of 16, is withdrawn: it is arithmetic on an illustrative input, not a finding. The same applies to the "2 days, 12.5%" and "zero days" figures in the paragraph below, whose denominator is the same 16. `B` has no value in the model and must be elicited, so every cell of the terminal column is conditional on an unmeasured parameter and none of them is a result.

`B` is the per-batch pre-sterilization bioburden component that parametric release requires [F11-S04 10.3, 10.4]. It has no value in the model and must be elicited. If B exceeds 7 days the saving shrinks by the excess.

Two comparisons bound the prize from the other side. Zeroing the entire chemical queue removes **2 days, 12.5%**, and gate `G15` in full removes **zero**, because `REDUCIBLE_COMPONENTS = {"assay"}` and assay sits under EM which sits under sterility. And **terminal route without parametric release removes zero days**: it keeps the 14-day component, adds an autoclave as a capacity-constrained unit operation, and adds Annex 1's per-sub-batch sterility test at the note to 10.6. `RS2-S08` indicates a period of commercial release against completed sterility tests is a precondition of the parametric filing, so the zero-saving state is probably a mandatory first phase rather than a modelling option.

- **Furosemide ANDA 202747.** Determined at **`weakly_inferred` with the `contradicted` flag set** (*revised 2026-09-06*), and it moves no number today, for three reasons rather than two: furosemide is a protocol 2.5 reserve candidate with no product config; the parametric supplement does not exist; and at `weakly_inferred` the row does **not** set `ProductFeatures.sterilization_route`. The 13-of-16 figure is the pre-R004 run `abl_20260902_phaseA`, which `bottleneck_decomposition.md` line 473 flags as "pre-R004 and must be regenerated before it is used again" and which MD-3 in that file corrects downward on 2026-09-06, the same day this screen was written. **Every ablation figure in this paragraph is superseded for quantitative use pending re-run after R004, R005 and R008** (`bottleneck_decomposition.md` lines 6 and 894). On that ranking a confirmed terminal route with parametric release is worth about 7 days at base and a small number of cell flips. It is not what stands between the study's designs and their target.

**The dating lever is separate and may be larger, with two qualifiers that were missing.** 503B Appendix B Table D gives 6 days at controlled room temperature for aseptic product released without a completed sterility test, 14 days for terminally sterilized product on a validated indicator-monitored cycle, and 28 days for either route with a completed passing test [F11-S45]. Those numbers were re-verified against the fetched source on 2026-09-06 and are exact.

*Qualifier 1, added 2026-09-06.* The instrument is **revised draft guidance**. Every page of it is stamped "Contains Nonbinding Recommendations" and "Draft - Not for Implementation" (49 pages carry each stamp in the fetched copy). It is not in-force FDA policy, and the parent prior-art note already records the falsifier: a final version of the guidance that changes Table D removes the lever.

A 503B outsourcing facility also cannot hold the parametric-release approval the rest of this section turns on, because that is a prior approval supplement filed by an application holder.

With both qualifiers stated: `bud_503b_days` should be a lookup on route by test-completion by storage condition, not a scalar. A model holding beyond-use date constant while varying release time is answering the wrong question for the 503B archetype.

## 4. The dataset gap, stated honestly

**How far a diligent public search gets.** Zero of 65 manufacturer labels states a sterilization *method*; six state the lyophilization step, which is a dosage-form fact.

*Corrected 2026-09-06, on the review channel.* Drugs@FDA holds **no published chemistry or product-quality review** for any application behind the candidate presentations, because they are pre-1997 NDAs or ANDAs and FDA publishes no chemistry review for an ANDA. The earlier and stronger claim, that Drugs@FDA holds zero documents of type `Review` for every candidate application, is false on the frozen snapshot that was cited for it. `S16` carries `Review`-type rows for three candidate applications: NDA 007513 (the longlist norepinephrine vial, 2024-11-05), ANDA 074930 (a longlist acyclovir application, 1998-05-13) and ANDA 200880 (acetazolamide, 2012-10-09). All three were fetched and read on 2026-09-06. `007513Orig1s038` is an approval package whose contents page marks only the approval letter and labelling, `74930ap` is a scanned `(b)(4)`-stamped ANDA package, `200880Orig1s000Approv` is an approval letter. None carries a route statement, so the conclusion stands and its basis is now the documents rather than their absence. `evidence_01` section 5 carries the table.

The Orange Book has no process field. Establishment registration names a STERILIZE operation on 6 of 46 establishment sections and never names the modality or the stage; it is now tier E7 in `method.md`, ceiling `unknown`, leads only. Across roughly 10^5 US labels (262,663 on 2026-09-06), exact-phrase counts give 17 `"terminally sterilized"`, of which **12 match the negation `"has not been terminally sterilized"` and 4 match the exact phrase `"this kit has not been terminally sterilized"** (*corrected 2026-09-06; the figure eight, previously printed here and in `evidence_01`, is not reproducible from either phrase*), 21 `"sterilized by autoclaving"`, 7 `"aseptically filled"` and 2 `"parametric release"`. The affirmative hits cluster in iodinated contrast and radiopharmaceutical label families of a much older vintage. There is no public register of parametric-release-approved products in either jurisdiction; the best public figure is twelve US firms as of 2015, from a trade article by an FDA scientist [RS2-S08].

**The blocker is redaction, not absence.** FDA writes the route down and withholds the word. In the NDA 214313 review: "The proposed drug product is (b) (4) sterilized." In FDA's Product Quality Review template there is a field labelled "Method of Sterilization:", and in the published NDA 207987 review its value is withheld behind `(b)(4)` [E03-S20]. Any plan that assumes more searching will eventually surface the route from US public sources is wrong.

**Which document class holds the rest, and who can get it.**

1. **Module 3.2.P.3.5 and 3.2.P.5 of the approved application, and the "Method of Sterilization" field of the Product Quality Review.** Definitive. Held by the holder and by FDA. A FOIA request returns the same `(b)(4)`, because the redaction is the holder's trade-secret claim rather than an agency lag. Obtainable by: the holder; a CDMO under confidentiality; a consultant with prior privileged access, who then cannot cite it.
2. **UK and EU GMP certificates (MHRA-GMDP, EudraGMDP).** Public, free, primary, government.

   *Corrected 2026-09-06, twice, because both the strength claim and the coverage claim were overstated.* First, this pass does **not** prove the lever works. It produced one determination on one site out of two attempted; the success is the furosemide row, which after audit sits at `weakly_inferred` with a contradiction recorded, and the failure is uninformative in either direction because the register's site-name URL parameter did not filter (limitation 3 below). Observed: one of two attempts, resolved once, at the bottom rung. The falsification row for this claim in section 6 says "untested", which is the honest word.

   Second, the scope table does distinguish `1.1.1 Aseptically prepared` from `1.1.2 Terminally Sterilised` by dosage form and separately lists `1.4.2.3 Moist heat`, so **absence of a code is a genuine negative for the operations the certificate covers**. Whether those are *all* of a third-country site's sterile-product operations, or only the operations inspected for supply to the certifying market, is **UNCERTAIN and belongs to HA-31**. UK GMP 44139 states on its face that it was "issued following an inspection ... in connection with marketing authorisation(s) listing manufacturers located outside of the European Economic Area". The question for HA-31, stated so it can be asked: *does a UK or EU GMP certificate for a third-country site enumerate all of that site's sterile-product operations, or only those in scope for the certifying market?* The Form FDA 483 [`RS5-S01`] showing two US-market filling lines at the certified Baxter site is exactly the case that makes the question live, and this screen's whole "what it would take to complete" pipeline in section 5 rests on the answer.

   Limits that stand either way: site-and-dosage-form granular rather than presentation granular, so a site holding both 1.1.1 and 1.1.2 settles nothing; coverage reaches only sites that supply the UK or EU, and the Amneal India site behind a longlist lead was not surfaced at all [`RS4-S05`]; and the certificate reflects the site at its inspection date. Obtainable by: anyone, today, for free.
3. **FDA enforcement documents.** A warning letter or Form 483 can name the process in passing, as `RS4-S02` did. Public, free, unindexed for this purpose, and available only where an inspection went badly. **Read the pair, not one of them.** This pass cited the warning letter and did not open the Form 483 behind it, and the 483 carried the counter-signal that changed the determination (section 2.1). A warning letter is the agency's summary; the 483 is the observation. Any future use of this class must fetch both.
4. **National public assessment reports** (MHRA PAR, Dutch MEB, BfArM, HPRA). Tier E1 where the quality section speaks about the drug product, and always for a different holder and a different site. *Corrected 2026-09-06:* the claim that they "routinely state the route in plain words" was carried by one quoted sentence attributed to an unresolvable source. The sentence is real ("The manufacturing process is a standard procedure for terminally sterilised parenteral solutions"), it is now given its own id `E01-S09` with a URL and an access date, and in both instances located it sits under a section headed **SOLVENT** and describes the solvent for Menopur rather than a drug product. `method.md` section 2 holds that a statement about a component or a diluent is not a statement about the drug product, so as located it is inadmissible. The claim is downgraded to unevidenced until a PAR is cited where the sentence is about the drug product.
5. **FDA establishment inspection reports under FOIA.** Redacted, slow, per-site, now targetable because two specific sites and DUNS numbers are named.
6. **The holder, asked directly.** The fastest path, and the one this study cannot fabricate. HA-34 already carries it, and the questions are now specific enough to ask.

## 5. What Telo could build here, assessed

**Is a route dataset an asset?** Conditionally, and the condition is not met yet.

*The case for, restated 2026-09-06 because the first version overstated three of its four points.* It is the enabling input for the only intervention **among the fourteen dossiers the sweep collected** that acts on the binding release constraint rather than compressing something that is not binding (`prior_art_review.md` section 6.1). That "only" is a statement about what the sweep looked at, not about the world: the study's own release memo (`~/telo/regulatory/release-constraints.md`) carries a second one, and it is item 6 of the case against below. The GMP-certificate join is free, public and government-sourced. It has been **attempted twice and resolved once, at `weakly_inferred`**, not "demonstrated": the earlier claim that a longlist row moved from `unknown` to `strongly_inferred` no longer holds after the audit. It does detect negatives as well as positives, which most evidence in this space does not, subject to the scope question now at HA-31. Product selection is repeatedly the cheapest lever in the study's own ablation, and route is one of the three product-architecture attributes the study named as decision-relevant. One thing that reads as an advantage and is also a liability: "free, public, government-sourced" is the reason the dataset is cheap to build and the reason it cannot be owned. See item 8.

*What it would take to complete.* A repeatable pipeline, not more reading. For each presentation-holder pair: take the manufacturing site from the label's "Manufactured by" text, join it to MHRA-GMDP and EudraGMDP certificate scope, and classify. Sites whose sterile scope resolves to one category yield a **lead**; sites holding both categories are escalated, not guessed. *Corrected 2026-09-06:* the pipeline as first written promised `strongly_inferred` "when a second line exists", and the one attempt shows why that is too generous. A certificate reaches a presentation only through the premise that every sterile product at the site takes the certified route, and any second line that also runs through that premise is not independent of it under `method.md` rule 1. To reach `strongly_inferred` the pilot needs a genuinely presentation-specific second line, and it should report how often it finds one rather than assuming it will. Applied to `evidence_04`'s scoped population, that is an **upper bound of 184 entry-holder pairs**, not 6, and the bound is soft in three ways `evidence_04` records and this file must carry: it is over-counted by at least 48 rows across six entries visible by inspection alone (section 6.3, where 48 is a floor on the over-count and not an estimate of it), four of the ten archetype flags cannot be evaluated from the snapshot at all and the blind spots are suspensions and emulsions (section 6.1), and the shortage listing carries no dosage form, so each pair needs a label-level reconstruction before the route question can even be posed (section 9 item 2). The unknown is coverage: US-only sites are outside the register entirely, and one of the two longlist leads did not appear in it. Nobody should commit to a completion figure before a coverage pilot on 20 to 30 pairs reports what fraction resolves.

*Who else would want it.* FDA and ASPR shortage analysts, who currently have no route field either; 503B operators, for whom the route changes beyond-use dating by a factor of two or more before it changes release time; CDMOs pricing a conversion; purchasers assessing which shortage-prone presentations have a structurally shorter path to supply. **None of these has been asked. No purchaser has been found for anything in this study**, and open questions 17 and 18 of the prior-art review record that no US hospital system, GPO or state has ever been found paying for resilience. Treat the demand side as unevidenced.

**And the party who can act on it is not on that list, which is item 7 below.** Route conversion is a prior approval supplement under 21 CFR 314.70(b)(2)(iii), with 21 CFR 314.97(a) applying the same regime to ANDAs, and parametric release is a second prior approval supplement (`evidence_02` section 7). Both are filed by the application holder. Telo holds no application. Every buyer named above is a party who cannot file the supplement.

*The case against, which is strong.* **Eight points, three of them added 2026-09-06**, and any one of them could be decisive.

1. **The thing it enables is third order.** The study's own ablation flips 4 of 16 cells on sterility, and sterility-related delay never ranks above 3rd of 17 in any cell. Inventory alone (`bounds:ss365+base_stock`) reaches 11 of 16 feasible with the hold intact on run `abl_post_R008`; the 13-of-16 figure previously quoted here is the pre-R004 run and is superseded (*corrected 2026-09-06*). All of these figures are superseded for quantitative use pending re-run after R004, R005 and R008, and the argument rests on the **ranking**, which the supersession notes do not disturb. A perfect route dataset does not change that ranking.
2. **The dataset is 88 percent unknown after a diligent search, and the residual is classified, not merely unfound.** A route table that is mostly blank is not a product, and the blanks are exactly the presentations a US-focused user would care about most.
3. **No platform effect.** Route is per presentation and per site. Parametric release appears to be per-site on two independent instruments, the EU granting authorisation "for specified sites" [RS2-S01] and the US abbreviated filing route requiring "the same manufacturing site" [F11-S03], so the asset does not aggregate the way a platform does.
4. **It decays and the half-life is unknown.** A route can change by supplement with no public trace, and a GMP certificate reflects a site at one inspection date. Maintenance is a recurring cost against an unpriced benefit.
5. **The one instance found is in the wrong archetype, and it is weaker than it was written.** The single product in this whole screen with a public affirmative statement of terminal moist heat plus parametric release is a large-volume parenteral in a flexible container [`RS4-S03`], and after audit it stands at `strongly_inferred` rather than `established`: a Dear Health Care Provider letter for a temporary importation is not approved labelling, so it sits at tier E3 with ceiling `strongly_inferred`. The statement is also dated **2014**, not 2023, and DailyMed flags the file as labelling FDA has not approved for a product whose US marketing ended 2020-12-31. `evidence_04` section 9 already said that if family 11 exists on the shortage list, the likeliest place is the plastic-container population the study's vial archetype does not represent. That is now evidenced rather than suspected, and it is an argument for changing the archetype before building the dataset.

6. **Route conversion is not the only intervention that reaches the binding constraint, and the other one is cheaper.** `bottleneck_decomposition.md` section 6.7 states that "any rapid method at or below 7 days delivers exactly the `loo:sterility_delay` column and no more, because EM waits underneath", and `method.md` section 4 concedes the same arithmetic through the Novartis result, 14 days to a 5-day method giving a 7-day release [F11-S28]. Both land on the same 9-day base pole this file computes for terminal plus parametric release. `~/telo/regulatory/release-constraints.md` records rapid sterility testing as commercially marketed and FDA-accepted with a time-to-result of 1 to 3 days, and calls the rapid-micro half a **buy-and-validate rather than a build**. A validated rapid microbiological method needs no prior approval supplement, no autoclave, no container requalification, no reformulation and no ANDA ownership. On the study's own model the two interventions are indistinguishable in release days, so a route dataset buys a harder path to a result that is already reachable by a purchasable one. The honest caveat on the other side is that the same memo records there is still no finalised FDA guidance on RMM for sterility testing.
7. **Telo cannot act on the dataset, and everyone who can already has the answer.** Route conversion and parametric release are both prior approval supplements filed by the application holder (`evidence_02` section 7). Telo holds no application; `docs/regulatory/approved_generic_cmo_map.md` is a map of other people's approvals. Every ANDA holder already knows its own route, because it wrote module 3.2.P.3.5, and FDA holds all of them, which is exactly what section 4 proves by quoting the redacted "Method of Sterilization:" field. A confirmed terminal route on a competitor's presentation gives Telo an observation, not an option. This bears on whether the coverage pilot is worth running at all, so it is stated before the recommendation rather than after it.
8. **No barrier to replication.** Every input to the proposed pipeline is a free public government register and the join is a two-source lookup. Nothing in it is proprietary data, a proprietary method, or a regulatory permission Telo holds. Any analyst, consultancy or the agencies themselves can reproduce it in a session, as this pass did. The dataset can be built; it cannot be owned. Its value is whatever a first mover captures before someone repeats the join.

*The recommendation, stated as a recommendation and not as a result.* Do not build the dataset yet. Run the coverage pilot in item 2 of section 4 on 20 to 30 entry-holder pairs from the `evidence_04` scoped population, report the resolved fraction, and ask HA-34's two now-specific questions of the two named holders. *Threshold, corrected 2026-09-06.* The earlier text set a go/no-go at "about a third" and nothing in this pass derives that number: not from the value of a resolved row, not from the cost of the join, not from what fraction of the 184 pairs' sites are even in the UK or EU registers. It is replaced by: **report the resolved fraction and decide then; no threshold is derivable from what this pass measured.** The two data points that exist are one resolved of two attempted, and the resolution is at `weakly_inferred`.

*Owner and size, which the earlier text omitted and without which this is not a runnable decision.* The pilot is search work with no confidential input, so it needs no named professional and does not belong in the human-action queue as an elicitation. Estimate: 20 to 30 pairs at roughly two fetches and one label read each, on the order of 6 to 10 hours of one analyst's time, no cost beyond it. It is tracked as falsification row `F11-ROUTE-03` in `../design_space/falsification_register.csv` with `human_action_id` `none (searchable)`. The two elicitation questions that do need a person stay under HA-34.

If the pilot resolves at a low fraction, the dataset is not completable from public sources and the honest deliverable is this map of what is knowable rather than the table itself.

## 7. Limitations of this pass

1. *Restated 2026-09-06; the earlier version overstated the risk in the direction of weakness.* The site attribution behind section 2.1 is **structural in the SPL XML, not document order**: the `performance` blocks carrying STERILIZE are siblings of `assignedOrganization` inside the same innermost `assignedEntity`, which is the HL7 SPL binding of an establishment to its business operations, and that block contains exactly one organisation name. It is corroborated by the label's own "Manufactured by: Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd Ahmedabad 382213, India" and by the matching postcode on the MHRA certificate. The parse that "disagreed" reproduces only if a parser walks `.iter()` from an outer `representedOrganization`, which pulls nested entities' performances up to the registrant, and that is a parser artefact. The real residual risks on that row are different and larger: **stage and scope** (the code does not distinguish final-container sterilization from component or bulk sterilization) and **which line** (the Form FDA 483 shows two US-market filling lines at the site and nothing ties the presentation to either).
2. The MHRA certificate was read through two independent fetches of the same page and the scope table agreed character for character between them. It was not verified against a PDF rendering of the certificate.
3. The Amneal negative [`RS4-S05`] is weak. The MHRA-GMDP site-name URL parameter did not filter the result set, so absence here means "not surfaced by search", not "not in the register".
4. Only two of the six candidates had a named manufacturing site to join against, because only two carry a registered STERILIZE operation. For the other four the site would have to come from label "Manufactured by" text, which this pass did not do. That is the cheapest remaining work on the longlist and it is unstarted.
5. No determination in this screen is `aseptically_filled` for a longlist presentation. That is not a finding that none is aseptically filled. It is the absence rule working correctly: silence in a label is never evidence of aseptic processing, and `method.md` rule 3 forbids recording it as such.
6. Every model number quoted in section 3 is arithmetic on `config/global.yaml`, whose serial QA term is tier 5 and illustrative and whose 14-day sterility figure is carried by a repository memo rather than a fetched copy of USP `<71>`, which is paywalled (HA-17). The section 3 table now carries that dependence in its own caption rather than only here.
7. **Scope-of-inspection confound, added 2026-09-06.** The GMP-certificate lever assumes a certificate enumerates a site's sterile-product operations. It certifies the operations the issuing inspectorate inspected, in connection with marketing authorisations for its own market. Whether that is the same set is UNCERTAIN and is now an HA-31 question (section 4 item 2). Every determination and every negative this lever produces inherits that uncertainty.
8. **One document class was cited from its summary rather than its source, added 2026-09-06.** The first pass cited an FDA warning letter and did not read the Form FDA 483 behind it. The 483 carried a counter-signal decisive enough to downgrade the screen's only terminal determination. Treat this as a general defect in the method rather than one missed fetch: a warning letter is a summary, and the observation is the evidence.
9. **The register's required fields were missing until after the fact, added 2026-09-06.** `route_determinations.csv` shipped without `application_holder`, `application_number`, `document_date` and `contradicted`, all four of which `method.md` section 2 requires. Two of the method's non-negotiable rules, currency (rule 7) and contradiction (rule 6), operate on the last two, so neither rule could be applied to any row until the fields were added. The date error on the B. Braun row is what that omission cost.
